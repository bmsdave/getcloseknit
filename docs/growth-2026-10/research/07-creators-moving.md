## How this list was built, and its gaps

- The shared WebSearch cap (200 per turn, used by all parallel agents) ran out part-way through. After that, discovery used only public platform pages (YouTube channel/video pages, TikTok profile/embed/oEmbed, Instagram share metadata, Substack archive API, gov.uk and legislation.gov.uk).
- Result: YouTube is well covered, TikTok partly, Substack one entry. NOT covered: podcasts, Threads, and Instagram-first creators. A follow-up search pass is needed for those (suggested queries at the bottom).
- The 5k-300k "London housing" niche on YouTube is thin. Several of the best-fitting channels are under 5k subscribers; they are flagged "under range". One entry is over 300k and flagged as a benchmark.

## UK rules for creator ads (sources)

What counts as an ad
- Anything with payment or an incentive. The gov.uk CMA creator guidance (updated 3 Sep 2025) says it applies "no matter how many followers", to products received free or discounted, direct payment or commission, and to posting about your own brand. Rewards include money, commission, discounts, free loans, and gifts of any product. Informal and verbal arrangements count, and "freebies" sent with no strings still count. https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/social-media-endorsements-guidance-for-content-creators and its full text https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/social-media-endorsements-guidance-for-content-creators/social-media-endorsements-being-transparent-with-your-followers
- It must be clearly labelled as an advert or ad and reflect the creator's genuine experience. This applies to videos, reels, stories, podcasts and other posts. Everyone involved must take responsibility for labelling.
- ASA/CAP + CMA "Influencers' guide to making clear that ads are ads", 3rd edition, 23 Mar 2023. The page says the advice "remains broadly correct" with updates planned for the DMCC Act. https://www.asa.org.uk/resource/influencers-guide.html (I read the landing page only, not the PDF.)
- ASA research page: "ad" or "#ad" is the stated minimum label. https://www.asa.org.uk/resource/labelling-of-influencer-advertising.html
- Secondary law-firm summaries of the guide (not the primary text, so verify in the PDF before relying on them): the label should be visible without tapping "see more" or being buried in a hashtag pile; plain "ad" / "advert" is preferred; "gifted", "affiliate", "sponsored" and "thanks to" alone are risky; where a brand has editorial control the CAP Code applies as well as consumer law; brands and creators are both responsible, including records and contract terms. https://www.mishcon.com/news/is-your-content-an-ad-updated-guidance-for-influencers-on-ad-labels, https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/advertising-and-marketing/what-is-the-right-hashtag-to-use-when-labelling-an-ad/
- Brand-side CMA guidance (28 Aug 2025): https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/reviews-and-social-media-endorsements-guidance-for-businesses-and-brands. It notes the old consumer-protection regulations were replaced by the DMCC Act unfair-practices rules from 6 Apr 2025.
- Not verified here: whether platform tools (TikTok branded content toggle, Instagram "Paid partnership") are enough on their own. Treat them as additional to an "Ad" label until the ASA guide PDF is checked.

DMCC Act 2024 and reviews
- Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024, Schedule 20, paragraph 13 (in force 6 Apr 2025). It bans a trader from submitting, or paying/commissioning someone to submit, a fake review or a review that conceals it was incentivised. "Commissioning" includes "incentivising by any means". Publishers must take reasonable steps to prevent and remove such reviews. https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2024/13/schedule/20
- Correction to the working assumption: the Act does not ban every incentivised review. Per a CMS law-firm summary, incentivised reviews are allowed if prominently labelled and genuine; rewarding five-star reviews, or hiding the incentive, is banned. The CMA can fine up to 10% of global turnover without going to court. https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/no-more-faux-five-stars-the-dmcc-act-bans-fake-reviews
- CMA fake-reviews guidance CMA208, published 4 Apr 2025, plus a short guide: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/fake-reviews (I did not read the 29-page PDF itself; the "conditional on positive review" detail is from the CMS summary only.)
- Enforcement is live: the CMA opened five fake-review investigations on 27 Mar 2026 (RPC summary, not read at source). https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/consumer/summer-2026/cma-launches-5-x-investigations-into-fake-and-misleading-review-practices/

What this means for Close Knit
- Any fee, commission, gift, or paid promotion = label "Ad" at the start, visible without expanding. Free access to a free tool is probably not an incentive on its own, but any payment is. Keep a written brief and a record, since both parties are responsible.
- Never offer anything for a rating or review of the web app, Chrome extension or Claude plugin, and never ask for positive-only reviews. Any review given for a reward must say so.
- The creator must say what they genuinely found. Do not script claims such as "best area" or anything about who lives where. This also matches the product rules: no per-home valuation, census not used to rank or filter people.
- Counting and tracking: the site collects no emails today; utm_source per creator works for the retention analytics.
