# Portal browser extensions: what competitors show, what they charge, what they earn

Research date: 2026-10-18. Written for Close Knit's browser extension (CUJ-17). Visual companion: the design canvas, journeys page, boards "CUJ-17 research: 5 ways + everything we show" and "CUJ-17 now (0.2.5) vs competitors".

**Confidence tags**
- [V] Verified on the page named, or seen live in Chrome on Rightmove on 2026-10-18.
- [S] From a search snippet or a third-party mirror. Treat it as approximate.
- [D] Derived by me: an estimate. The assumptions are stated.

**Limits**
- None of the companies publishes turnover; all file small or micro accounts.
- Profit is proxied by the year-on-year change in reserves. That is after dividends, so it understates profit.
- PropertyData, PaTMa and Property Tracker were not installed. They are described from their promo sites and store pages.

## 1. The field

16 extensions add data to UK portals. These are the ones that matter.

| Extension | Users / rating | Portals | Where the UI sits | Core data |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Property Log | 100k / 2.5 [S] | Rightmove | Listing page | Asking-price history |
| Property Tracker | 30k / 3.9 [S] | Rightmove | Arrow by each result price; hover shows the history | Up, down or same since first listed (crowd data) |
| Propbar | 10k / 3.8 [S] | RM, Zoopla, OTM | Banner on the listing, a panel once paid | Comps since 1995, EPC, flood, planning, AI summary, "unlock full address" |
| PaTMa | 10k / 4.7 [S] | RM, Zoopla, OTM, PropertyNews, PropertyPal | Panel and popup | Price history (with off-market gaps), yield, ROI, rent, comps |
| PropertyData | 10k / 3.8 [S] | RM, Zoopla, OTM | Green sidebar | £/sqft, average asking price and rent for the bedroom count, yield, 3-year growth, SDLT, report |
| Area360 | 9k / 4.5 [S] | RM, Zoopla | Grid under the price, on results and on listings [V] | See below |
| Locrating | 4k [S] | RM, Zoopla, OTM | Full-width map above the listing [V] | Schools, catchments, noise, flood layers |

**Area360's grid** [V] has:
- **About the home:** postcode, size and £/ft², EPC, last sale, location.
- **Area ratings (/10):** crime, deprivation, income.
- **Shares and counts:** social-housing %, schools with Ofsted dots.
- **Listing and nearby:** days on market and total reduction, nearest park with its size, nearest station with its lines.
- **Under "See more":** flood, noise, connectivity.

**Nobody offers:**
- commute minutes to your own places (the three commute extensions are gone from the store);
- council tax or the full monthly cost;
- broadband, conservation, listed buildings, Article 4;
- a personal match;
- SpareRoom and OpenRent coverage.

**What the accounts show**
- **PropertyData**, FY Dec 2025: cash £90k, 2 staff, equity −£566k after a £1.91M impairment of investments. Reserves grew £169k (2022), £230k (2023), £413k (2024) and about £414k in 2025 before the impairment.
- **Propbar** (Vepler Ltd), micro accounts, FY Feb 2025:
  - net liabilities −£126k, against −£77k a year earlier, so a loss of about £49k;
  - 2 staff;
  - new shares issued in December 2025.
- **Locrating**, micro accounts, FY Apr 2025:
  - net assets £33k;
  - current assets £161k;
  - accruals and deferred income £125k;
  - 4 staff, up from 2.
- **PaTMa** (Pither Technologies), micro accounts, FY Aug 2025: net liabilities −£255k, falling by about £50–65k a year. 1 employee.

**How the estimates are built.** Installs × paid conversion × price.
- Paid conversion: 1–5% for pro tools, 0.1–2% for consumer tools.
- Store user counts are a ceiling. Active users are fewer.

## 4. Takeaways for Close Knit

1. **Only PropertyData clearly makes money.** It earns about £1–2M a year from a professional web SaaS. Its extension is a shop window and a funnel, not the product.
2. **Charging home buyers for the extension itself is weak.**
   - Propbar and PaTMa lose about £50k a year each and are kept going by owner or investor money.
   - Property Log's paywall brings a 2.5 rating.
   - Property Tracker, with 30k users, earns hobby money.
3. **Installs don't predict revenue.** Pro pricing (£15–60) earns money; consumer price-history tools (£1–5) don't.
4. **The model that works**, and fits ours: a free, useful layer on the portal, with payment for depth elsewhere. Depth here means a home report, agent and B2B access, or an API.
5. **Openings nobody uses:**
   - partner links at "Before you offer": mortgage broker, conveyancer, surveyor, insurance;
   - an agent widget built on our card.
6. **Where we stand:** Close Knit already covers every row of the competitor matrix for free. The gaps are in presentation:
   - on the listing our card floats at the side, while Area360 sits under the price;
   - on results we show one small line, while Area360 shows a full grid.
7. **Same listing, two answers.** On Rightmove 89289420 (NW6 1DQ):
   - Area360 shows a last sale of 16 Aug 2013, £1.0M;
   - our flagged address match found sales in 2007 and 2012.

   Resolve this before `CLOSE_KNIT_ADDRESS_MATCH` is ever switched on.

## 5. Area360: how a free, anonymous extension plans to earn (checked 2026-10-18)

There are no ads, partner links or prices on area360.uk; Google Analytics is the only third-party
script. They build an audience instead:
- **SEO pages:** every postcode in England, area ratings, "Poorest / Wealthiest areas", "Property
  Log alternative".
- **Extensions and apps:** Chrome, Safari and an iPhone app.
- **Accounts:** an email base.
- **Tools that will carry money later:** Mortgage Rates Tracker, a stamp duty calculator, an
  investor calculator, and the new "Property Profile" home report.

**Likely ways to earn:** partner links (brokers, conveyancers), a paid home report, leads for
agents, or selling the user base. Staying anonymous works while they take no money. Their privacy
policy names only "Area360" and privacy@, which is weak under UK GDPR. Their terms carry an
"Independence / not affiliated" section and a trademark notice. More on the 22 extensions and the
portals' terms is in `10-extension-legal-risk.md`.
