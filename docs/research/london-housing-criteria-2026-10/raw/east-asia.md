# East Asia stream: Hong Kong, Singapore, Tokyo, Seoul, Beijing/Shanghai

Research date: October 2026. Stream of the "London housing criteria" study. Focus: how buyers, renters and agents search and price homes in five East Asian hubs, which criteria are primary vs secondary, and what is transferable to London (many London buyers and renters come from these markets: Hong Kong BN(O) movers, mainland Chinese students and investors, Singaporean, Japanese and Korean professionals).

Method note: WebSearch budget for the shared workflow ran out part-way, and Reddit, SCMP, PropertyGuru, 99.co and EdgeProp returned blocks (403/redirect). Forum evidence therefore leans on HardwareZone (Singapore), Batgung (Hong Kong, older), e-mansion (Japan, older) and the TLUG mailing list (Japan, older). Items marked "(snippet)" were seen only in search results and are not counted as read.

---

## 1. Cross-market headline

The five markets share one structural feature that London lacks: **pricing is anchored on standardised, unit-level, highly comparable data** (estate + block + floor + flat), and lenders publish or apply a formal valuation that caps borrowing. That makes the *valuation figure itself* a search and negotiation criterion:

- Hong Kong: bank online valuations by estate/block/floor/flat (HSBC tool sourced from Cushman & Wakefield) and the Centa-City Index, which is a hedonic index adjusting for floor area, age, location, direction, view and floor level.
- Singapore: HDB valuation fixes the maximum loan and CPF use; anything above it is "cash over valuation" (COV), paid in cash.
- Seoul: the KB (Kookmin Bank) market price ("KB sise") and appraisal values feed loan limits; banks now take the lowest of sale price, KB price and appraisal.
- Beijing/Shanghai: Beike/Lianjia's "Housing Dictionary" records every unit, and its Agent Cooperation Network (ACN) and VR tours turn listings into a near-MLS.
- Tokyo: the listings are standardised more than the valuations. Walking minutes to a station follow a regulated formula (80 m = 1 minute), and the build year matters because of the 1981 earthquake code.

**Transferable to London**: buyers from these markets expect (a) unit-level comparables, (b) price per *net* usable area, (c) floor/view/orientation premia made explicit, (d) a lender or index valuation visible before offer, and (e) a precise "minutes to station" figure. These are mostly absent or implicit in UK portals (Rightmove/Zoopla list bedrooms, not sq ft; EPC floor area is the nearest proxy).

## 2. Hong Kong

### Primary criteria
1. **Price per saleable square foot (not gross).** For many years HK agents quoted gross floor area (GFA), which includes a share of lobbies, clubhouses, corridors, bay windows and utility platforms. JLL (Denis Ma, 2012) explains the Estate Agents Authority guideline requiring agents to quote saleable area from end-2012. JLL expected buyers to compete for flats with high efficiency ratios (saleable/gross), so a good ratio would carry a premium, not a price fall. Older guidance (Batgung 2006) says an 80%+ ratio is good, and two flats with the same quoted area can differ a lot in usable space. In search snippets (not read), SCMP says new flats offered about 75% efficiency against 80%+ in the 1980s-90s and up to 90% in the 1970s, and that saleable-based psf is about 30% higher than gross-based psf. Knight Frank's August 2026 monthly quotes all deals in saleable sq ft and HK$ per saleable sq ft (e.g. Deep Water South 1,696 sq ft at about HK$44,530 psf). Saleable-area pricing is now the professional norm.
   - Sources: https://research.jllapsites.com/the-impact-of-saleable-floor-areas-on-residential-prices/ ; https://batgung.com/how-to-buy-a-flat-apartment-condominium-in-hong-kong.html ; https://content.knightfrank.com/research/676/documents/en/hong-kong-monthly-august-2026-13014.pdf ; (snippet) https://www.scmp.com/article/634165/small-area-concern
2. **Estate, block, floor, flat: unit-level comparables and bank valuation.** HSBC's online valuation tool walks the user through zone, district, estate, block, floor and flat. It returns a valuation with gross area, saleable area and building age. The figures come from Cushman & Wakefield and are "for reference only", not binding on the bank. Buyers check recent transactions in the same development and bank valuations before offering (Batgung guide).
   - Sources: https://www.hsbc.com.hk/mortgages/tools/property-valuation/ ; https://batgung.com/how-to-buy-a-flat-apartment-condominium-in-hong-kong.html
3. **Building age, through mortgage tenor.** Forum and guide evidence says buyers get "twitchy" above about 20 years. Banks cap the tenor by building age: the Batgung forum (2004) reports a cap of 30 years minus building age, and the 2006 guide gives a cap of 50 minus building age, together with a borrower-age cap of 70 minus your age. Large "blue-chip" estates (Taikoo Shing, Mei Foo, Telford Gardens, Heng Fa Chuen) were exempt in practice because banks see them as safe. A forum respondent advised buying flats about 5 years old so they resell easily after 10 years. The specific tenor rules differ between these sources and are from the 2000s, so verify current HKMA/bank practice before relying on them.
   - Sources: https://www.batgung.com/node/71.html ; https://batgung.com/how-to-buy-a-flat-apartment-condominium-in-hong-kong.html
4. **MTR proximity and "railway property".** MTR proximity is highly valued in the guide and forum evidence. In a search snippet (not read), Knight Frank's Thomas Lam says rail-linked projects are especially sought after.

### Secondary / culturally specific criteria
- **Direction (orientation), view, floor level** are explicit hedonic adjusters in the Centa-City Index (CityU/Centaline), so the HK market's own benchmark treats them as priced attributes. The index has been based at July 1997 = 100 and published monthly since then. A weekly Centa-City Leading Index (CCL) uses Centaline's current contract prices because Land Registry data lag by 1-2 months. https://www.cb.cityu.edu.hk/ms/research/centa-city-index
- **Sea/harbour view.** Knight Frank (Aug 2026) notes sea-view premises drawing strong enquiry and firmer rents (office context). Residential demand spans premium sea-view projects and older urban stock.
- **Unlucky/lucky numbers.** Fortin, Hill & Huang (2010) review Chau, Ma & Ho (2001), who found price effects for lucky floor numbers 8, 18 and 28 in 1,019 HK apartment sales. They caution that the sample came from one agency. Their own Vancouver data, nearly 117,000 sales, show that in tracts at least 18% ethnic Chinese, addresses ending in 4 sold about CAD 8,000 lower and those ending in 8 about CAD 10,000 higher (mean price about CAD 400,000). Many HK and Vancouver towers skip floors containing 4. In search snippets (not read), HKU abstracts say lucky-floor premiums are larger in booms.
   - https://aeaweb.org/conference/2011/retrieve.php?pdfid=215
- **"Hung jaak" (haunted / death-incident flats).** In search snippets only (SCMP, Bloomberg via EdgeProp), Centaline's Perry Fong puts the usual discount at 10-20%, with 20-40% cited for notorious cases. A Squarefoot survey reportedly found 54% would consider such a flat at the right price. Not read in full.
- **School nets.** The 2006 guide notes school catchments (Central/Western) carry premiums.
- **Feng shui** was not directly evidenced by a read source. Orientation and view adjustments in the CCL are the closest measurable proxy.

### Process / agents
Agent commission was officially 1% each side, and buyers negotiated it to 0.8-0.9% (2006). The 2006 guide's rule of thumb was that a flat must appreciate about 5% to cover transaction costs, and it advised trusting agents who admit "I don't know".

## 3. Singapore

### Primary criteria
1. **Valuation vs price: COV (cash over valuation).** HDB issues the valuation only after the Option to Purchase is granted. Loans and CPF are capped at the lower of price and valuation, so COV must be paid in cash.
   - Stacked Homes (Ryan Ong, 2021): about 1 in 3 resale flats carried COV. Typical COV was S$15-20k for 3-4 room flats, S$40-50k for jumbo/maisonette/DBSS and over S$100k for Pinnacle@Duxton. HDB stopped publishing COV data in 2014. Drivers: newly MOP'd flats (little lease decay), upgrader pressure and BTO delays.
   - HardwareZone "COV Discussion" (Aug 2022, about 10-12 participants): reported COVs ranged from S$0 (Kallang/Whampoa) through S$5k, S$15k and S$18k, to S$65k (rejected) for a high-floor Ang Mo Kio 4-room near the hub with a nice view, and S$90k "close to central". Forum members also pointed to crowdsourced COV databases.
   - HardwareZone "HDB Resale Valuation": posters said HDB valuers weigh recent transactions, floor, MOP status and nearby amenities. Some said renovation is ignored, but others said condition still shows; corner units value higher because they are larger. Members advised against offering on the hope of a high valuation.
   - Sources: https://stackedhomes.com/editorial/cash-over-valuation-to-keep-climbing-for-hdb-flats ; https://forums.hardwarezone.com.sg/threads/cov-discussion.6794281/ ; https://forums.hardwarezone.com.sg/threads/hdb-resale-valuation.6569608/
2. **Remaining lease (lease decay) and the age-95 rule.** From 10 May 2019, CPF use is pro-rated if the remaining lease cannot cover the youngest buyer to age 95. Full CPF (up to the valuation limit) and a full HDB loan need the lease to reach 95. Flats with under 20 years left get neither CPF nor HDB loans (CPF Board). PropertyNet (July 2026) restates SLA's Bala's curve: about 96% of freehold value at 99 years remaining, about 80% at 60 and about 75% at 50, steepening below 60. It notes that about a third of HDB stock is over 35 years old, and that VERS (voluntary redevelopment) is deliberately less generous than SERS. Stacked's Ryan Ong, answering on Seedly, warned that old resale flats appreciate least and advised choosing high-demand MRT-adjacent locations if buying old.
   - Sources: https://www.cpf.gov.sg/member/infohub/news/cpf-related-announcements/more-flexibility-to-buy-a-home-for-life-while-safeguarding-retir ; https://propertynet.sg/hdb-lease-decay-balas-curve-flat-values-60-year-mark-2026/ ; https://seedly.sg/posts/what-specifics-to-look-out-for-when-buying-a-resales-hdb-i-am-looking-for-a-place-in-mature-area-to-stay-and-the-flats-are-in-general-old-and-costly-i-do-want-to-sell-it-for-a-profit/
3. **Town / maturity, flat type and MOP freshness.** Real Estate Asia (PropNex data, Q2 2026): the HDB RPI fell 0.3% QoQ, the second quarterly fall. 491 million-dollar resales in Q2 was a record. Flats with 94+ years of lease left made up 5.9% of deals, about 58% of them in Tampines, Bukit Batok and Punggol. Million-dollar flats cluster in mature central towns (Toa Payoh, Queenstown, Bukit Merah, Ang Mo Kio), which search snippets put at about 90% of such deals.
   - https://realestateasia.com/residential/news/singapore-hdb-resale-prices-dip-03-in-q2
4. **MRT distance.** In a search snippet (PropertyGuru, Jan-Aug 2021 data, not read), flats within 500 m of an MRT station sell at about an 8% premium, and 4-room flats at about 10%. The premium is about 40% in Bukit Panjang and 30% in Geylang, but *negative* in the central area, where flats without a nearby MRT cost 42% more. Forum and Q&A evidence treats MRT access as the main liquidity protector for old flats.
5. **1 km primary-school rule.** MOE gives priority in this order: Singapore citizens within 1 km, then 1-2 km, then beyond 2 km, and only then PRs by the same bands. Families must live at the registered address for at least 30 months, or MOE may move the child. The phases using distance run from 2A to 2C Supplementary. The rule turns "within 1 km of a popular primary school" into a hard search filter.
   - https://moe.gov.sg/primary/p1-registration/distance

### Secondary / superstition
- Stacked Homes (Ian Tan, 2022) looked at 160,408 private buy-sell pairs from 1995 to 2021. Units with "inauspicious" numbers (4, 14, 44) earned *higher* capital gains than "auspicious" ones (8, 88), because they were bought cheaper. The exception is the Core Central Region, where auspicious units did better. New-launch auspicious units lost most, because developers price the premium in. https://stackedhomes.com/editorial/do-property-superstitions-really-affect-profits-we-look-at-26-years-of-data
- URA caveat data (private resale transparency) could not be read (the page did not render). This is a known gap.

## 4. Tokyo

### Primary criteria
1. **Walking minutes to the nearest station.** The display rules (Fair Competition Code) fix 80 m per minute along roads, with fractions rounded up. Slopes, crossings and signal waits are ignored. On a 2004 e-mansion forum thread (about 10 participants), users doubted advertised times, and ex-agents said they measure routes with a wheel and divide by 80 m. Diewert & Shimizu (UBC DP 13-07, 2014; Tokyo sales 2000-2010) estimate how land value falls with each extra minute's walk to the nearest subway station:

   | Walk to station | Land value per extra minute |
   |---|---|
   | 2-8 minutes | -0.35% |
   | 8-13 minutes | -2.01% |
   | Over 13 minutes | -1.71% |

   The effect is strongly **non-linear**, with a cliff around 8 minutes. Travel time from that station to Tokyo Station matters little up to about 28 minutes and then costs 1.3-1.9% per minute. The average walk in their sample was 9.9 minutes. In a Village House survey (June 2024), 25% call 5 minutes "near the station" and 31% call 10 minutes "close". Respondents' average walk was 13.6 minutes, and a 5- vs 10-minute walk changes rent by ¥10-20k. A vendor guide (realestateguide.jp, low reliability, internal index) gives resale price ratios of 100% within 5 minutes, 92% at 6-10 minutes and 85% at 11-15 minutes. In a search snippet (Japan Property Central citing Tokyo Kantei, not read), building age moves rent more than station distance: a 30-40% gap between buildings under 3 years and 30-year-old ones, against about 20% between a walk under 3 minutes and one of 11-15 minutes.
   - Sources: https://www.e-mansion.co.jp/bbs/thread/17837/ ; https://cigs.canon/event/report/uploads/pdf/151016_diewert_paper.pdf ; https://www.villagehouse.jp/blog/en/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/30420 ; https://realestateguide.jp/?p=1416
2. **Building age (chiku-nen) and the June 1981 seismic code.** Listings show the build year as a standard field. Buildings permitted after 1 June 1981 are "shin-taishin", and older ones are "kyu-taishin". The 1995 Hanshin quake showed far better survival for post-1981 stock, so buyers and renters are routinely told to filter for it. Diewert-Shimizu estimate net structure depreciation (after renovations) at about 2.5% a year for 0-10 years, 1.6% for 10-20 years and 0.3% after 20 years. In other words, Japanese structures lose value fast early on and later value is mostly land. In a search snippet (Japan Property Central, not read), units under 5 years old sold for 49% more per m² than 30+ year-old units in the 23 wards, and up to 3.7x more in Saitama.
   - Sources: https://resources.realestate.co.jp/living/chiku-nen-%E7%AF%89%E5%B9%B4-real-estate-japans-word-of-the-day/ ; https://cigs.canon/event/report/uploads/pdf/151016_diewert_paper.pdf
3. **Layout code (1K/1DK/1LDK/2LDK) and m²** are the standard search fields. No source dedicated to them was read in this stream, so this rests on general portal convention. Real Estate Japan lists building age, price, room count and station distance as its filters. Suumo did not load (503).

### Renters: costs and screening (primary for migrants)
- Upfront costs usually total 4-6 months' rent. That includes a deposit (shikikin) of 1-2 months, key money (reikin) of 1-2 months, the agent fee, the guarantor-company fee, insurance and a lock change. Example: a ¥80k flat needs about ¥390k upfront. Listings marked "gaikokujin fuka" (no foreigners) exist. https://realestateguide.jp/?p=844
- Income screens: annual income of at least 36x the monthly rent (Village House 2019), or rent no more than about 30% of take-home pay (e-housing 2025). In the 2016 MoJ survey, about 39-40% of foreign residents reported being refused because they were not Japanese, and about 27% gave up on "no foreigners" listings. Guarantor companies, now standard, charge about 0.5-1 month's rent plus annual renewals, and they run their own screening.
   - https://www.villagehouse.jp/blog/en/japanese-apartment-life/why-your-rental-application-is-getting-rejected/ ; https://e-housing.jp/post/why-your-apartment-rental-application-is-getting-rejected-as-a-foreigner
- A forum example (TLUG list, 2007): a foreigner needing a 2DK was asked for a personal guarantor by almost every agency. Respondents suggested Leopalace or the employer acting as guarantor. https://lists.tlug.jp/ML/0712/msg00096.html
- Market context: Savills (via Real Estate Asia) reports Tokyo 23-ward mid-market rents up 7.1% YoY in Q4 2025 to ¥4,639/m², and up 9.0% in the central five wards. Drivers are wage growth, return to office and demographic inflows. https://realestateasia.com/residential/news/tokyo-mid-market-residential-rents-71-in-2025

## 5. Seoul

### Primary criteria
1. **Tenure mode: jeonse vs wolse.** Under jeonse the tenant pays a lump-sum deposit (often quoted at 50-80% of value) and no monthly rent for 2 years, with the deposit refunded at the end. Even small Seoul studios can need deposits of KRW 100m or more. Wolse (deposit plus monthly rent) and ban-jeonse are the usual foreigner routes. Jeonse fraud and "gap jeonse" (landlords buying with tenants' deposits) make **checking the registry and getting deposit insurance** (HUG/SGI, per search snippets) a primary diligence step. https://stay.enko.kr/blog/korean-housing-guide-for-foreigners
2. **School and hagwon district.** Asia Business Daily (Mar 2020, KB data): Gangnam-gu jeonse rose 9.32% from June 2019 to February 2020, against 4.26% for Seoul overall. Examples: Daechi I-Park 59.9 m² rose from KRW 800m to 1.05bn, and Mokdong New Town 7 from 680m to 850m. Drivers were the abolition of autonomous and foreign-language high schools, admissions changes, and tight supply in prestige school districts. A January 2020 piece names Daechi-dong and Jamwon-dong as education hubs where school placement and hagwon clusters support prices even for old, unrebuilt complexes.
   - https://www.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2020031108232854433 ; https://view.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2020012814380143099
3. **Apartment brand (Raemian, Xi, Hillstate, Prugio, etc.).** Hyun (2021, Real Estate Studies 27(1)) ran difference-in-differences on more than 40,000 Seoul transactions. Only brand-related renamings raised prices, the effect faded within about a year, and it did not spill over to neighbours (abstract only). A 2010 hedonic study treats brand as an "intangible" price component alongside physical and neighbourhood factors (abstract only). Brand is a real but secondary, partly transient premium.
   - https://kiss.kstudy.com/Detail/Ar?key=3874838 ; https://scholar.kyobobook.co.kr/article/detail/4010023217576
4. **KB price, appraisal and loan caps.** Money Today (Aug 2026): for DH Bangbae (Seocho), KB Kookmin lends the lower of 60% of the sale price or 50% of the appraised value. An 84 m² unit sold for KRW 2.2bn, but its occupancy rights traded at 2.7-3.9bn, so lending is anchored to the sale price. Post-2025 regulated zones cap mortgages at KRW 600m. Zigbang (via JoongAng, H1 2026): Seoul's top-1% threshold was KRW 4.65bn, against 1.85bn in Gyeonggi, concentrated on the Han River, Gangnam redevelopments and new luxury complexes. It eased from 5bn because of loan curbs and multi-home taxes.
   - https://www.mt.co.kr/en/finance/2026/08/25/2026082516222624952 ; https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/business/starting-price-for-seouls-top-1-of-apartments-reaches-47-billion-won/12781777

## 6. Beijing / Shanghai

1. **Xuequfang (school-district housing).** Huang, He, Xu & Zhu (Journal of Housing Economics, forthcoming version read) build a 4-wave panel of Beijing residential complexes, 2013-2016, from Lianjia and Fang.com transactions and use matching DiD. Eligibility for a **municipal-level key primary school adds about 4-8%** to prices, and a district-level key school about 2-3%. The effects are larger for newer, denser and more central complexes. Policies meant to equalise access ("multi-school dicing" lotteries, school federations) actually pushed prices up. An agent cited by Xinhua put elite-district prices about 30% higher in 2013. That is a raw gap, and the causal premium is much smaller. This disagreement matters for London analogies: raw "catchment premia" overstate the causal effect.
   - https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/36221/1/Elite%20School%20Designation%20and%20Housing%20Prices%20manu%20Resub2%20v2c.pdf
2. **Platform data and VR viewing (Beike/Lianjia).** OnlineMarketplaces (Mar 2023): Beike's ACN links about 406,000 active agents, of whom only about 25% are from its own Lianjia brokerage. Agents split commission by role in the deal (listing, showing, closing), and the "Housing Dictionary" plus true-listing de-duplication act as the market's source of truth. Realsee VR tours covered about 70% of listings (85% in major cities). GTV was about US$378bn in 2022. For a London tool, this is the closest real-world model of a unit-level, de-duplicated, VR-enabled listing database.
   - https://www.onlinemarketplaces.com/articles/ke-holdings-the-most-interesting-real-estate-company-in-the-world/
3. **South orientation and floor.** No source read in this stream quantifies these for mainland China. That is a gap. The HK Centa-City Index's explicit "direction" and "floor level" adjustments are the nearest read evidence that orientation is priced in Chinese-speaking markets.

|---|---|---|---|
| Primary | Net usable area and price per net sq ft/m² | HK (saleable), Tokyo (m²), Seoul (m², "pyeong") | Strong: JLL, Knight Frank, guides |
| Primary | Unit-level comps plus lender/index valuation (bank tool, HDB valuation, KB price) | HK, SG, KR | Strong: HSBC tool, CPF/HDB rules, HWZ threads, MT 2026 |
| Primary | Transit walk time (station minutes, MRT/MTR) | Tokyo (regulated metric), SG, HK | Strong: Diewert-Shimizu non-linear; SG premium snippet |
| Primary | Building age / lease remaining (seismic code; lease decay; mortgage tenor) | Tokyo, SG, HK | Strong: CPF rules, Bala's curve, depreciation estimates |
| Primary | School access (1 km rule, xuequfang, hagwon) | SG, Beijing, Seoul, HK | Strong: MOE rule; causal 4-8% Beijing |
| Primary (renters) | Upfront cost and screening (reikin/guarantor; jeonse deposit safety) | Tokyo, Seoul | Strong: surveys 39-40% rejection |

## 8. London relevance
- Hong Kong and mainland buyers in London will expect **psf on net internal area**. UK listings often omit it, so EPC floor area is a useful proxy filter.
- Singapore and Hong Kong buyers are used to **lender valuation gaps** (COV, bank tool). The UK down-valuation risk is a familiar concept to them, but less transparent here.
- Japanese movers think in **minutes on foot to a station** with a fixed formula. A London tool should compute walking minutes on the street network, not straight-line distance, and present them in bands (5/10/15).
- Families from all five markets treat **school admission geography** as primary. London's distance-based oversubscription criteria for state schools are a close analogue to Singapore's 1 km rule.
- **Superstitions** (4 and 8; death-incident flats) are niche in London but matter for some diaspora buyers and new-build unit numbering.
- **Renter screening shock** goes both ways. East Asian renters in London meet referencing, a 5-week deposit cap and guarantor services, which are comparable to Japan's guarantor companies but with no key money.

## 9. Gaps and caveats
- Forum evidence for HK and Japan is old (2004-2007). Reddit was unreachable. Recent HWZ threads (2022) are the strongest forum data.
- URA caveat data, Suumo/HOMES filters, LDK conventions, Shanghai/Beijing south-facing premia and HK school-net premia were not confirmed by a read source.
- realestateguide.jp resale ratios come from a vendor's internal index, so treat them as indicative only.
