# Verification: Academic housing search behaviour and portals

- **1. Rightmove drawn searches are hyper-local (about 50% cover 5 km² or less; 51.5% leave bedrooms unset)**: supported. Rae & Sener (Cities 2016) used about 100,000 drawn searches from March 2013. "Just over 50% are 5 km2 or less" and "just over half the time (51.5%)" no initial bedroom count is set. The paper notes that Camden is just under 20 km².
- **2. Trulia alerts: every alert sets a geography, about 2/3 set price (median band ±27%), 1/3 set bathrooms; restaurants and bars explain 25.3% of zip-level popularity, schools 2.6%**: supported. Piazzesi, Schneider & Stroebel (NBER w20823) report these figures. The 25.3% and 2.6% are univariate R² values in Table 5, for Bay Area zip codes.
- **3. School-quality info moved families to schools rated 0.10 SD higher; families trade 54 commute minutes per 10 school percentiles, about double the naive estimate**: supported. Bergman, Chan & Kapor (NBER w27209). The naive model gives 24 minutes, so 54 is about 2.25 times that (the paper says the naive model understates it by more than 50%). The subjects were housing-voucher families on GoSection8.
- **4. Removing SpareRoom's 'No DSS' tool raised listings open to benefit tenants from 9% to 48%; 96% of UK renters use Rightmove, SpareRoom, Zoopla or Gumtree**: supported. Meers (2024) compares a 2016 sample (155 listings, 9%) with a 2021 sample (802 listings, 48%). These are samples taken before and after the change, not a controlled test. The 96% figure is cited from Burgess (2019).
- **5. Funda clicks predict sale speed and then prices; a 1% higher Zestimate raises list prices by 0.733% and sold prices by 0.081%**: partially supported. Fu, Jin & Liu (NBER w29880) confirm 0.733% (pre-listing Zestimate on list price) and 0.081% (post-listing Zestimate on sold price, conditional on list price). The DNB WP 481 URL returned "Access Denied", so the Funda clicks claim could not be checked.
