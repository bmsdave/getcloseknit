# Forums: how sellers and estate agents set a London/UK asking price

Stream: forum discussions (Mumsnet Property, MoneySavingExpert House Buying/Selling boards) plus a few trade-press pieces (Property Industry Eye, The Negotiator, Which?/Times, Mortgage Introducer via MPA) that put numbers on the forum anecdotes. Research date: October 2026.

Access notes: Reddit (www, old, .json, pullpush mirror) returned 403/429 for every request, so r/HousingUK could not be read. PropertyTribes thread bodies load through JavaScript and came back empty, so they are not counted. ukhillwalking and Estate Agent Today returned 403. Coverage therefore leans on Mumsnet and MSE, which are the two largest UK consumer property forums anyway.

Read in full: 26 forum threads and 4 trade-press articles. Forum threads span 2010-2026, most from 2018-2025. London threads are flagged as such; non-London ones show general UK practice that London sellers follow too.

## 1. How the number is actually produced: the seller's view of the process

### 1.1 The "three agent valuations" ritual and the range it produces
- The default UK seller workflow on both forums: invite three (often four or five) local agents to give a "market appraisal", then pick an asking price and an agent. Posters often point out that an agent's "valuation" is not a valuation in the RICS sense. It is a recommended marketing price, and the agent is pitching for the instruction while giving it. (Mumsnet "How are houses really valued", c.2024; MSE "Estate agent valuation or surveyor", 2022.)
  - https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/property/5226962-how-are-houses-really-valued
  - https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/6381261/estate-agent-valuation-or-surveyor
- The spreads are large. Reported ranges:
  - SE London house, four agents: £525k-£600k, a 14% spread. The highest was from KFH, which locals said habitually "overvalues" and then reduces. https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/property/3218246-Range-of-Valuations-SE-London-Confused
  - London 1-bed flat (2019), six agents: £310k-£335k. The seller had already failed to sell once at a level agents set about 25% too high, with three offers falling through. https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/6035344/selling-my-london-flat-which-agent
  - N14 studio (2024-25): agents said £200k-£265k, a 32% spread. It was listed at £265k, cut to £260k, and the best offer was £250k. https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/6587309/should-i-accept-lower-offer-for-my-london-flat
  - Unusual 2-bed house with no comparable sale for 8 years (2023): four agents said about £225k and one said £180k. https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/6424141/estate-agent-valuations
  - Blackpool (2010): £105k-£170k. Other posters reported £40k-£150k spreads. https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/property/960981-Had-house-valued-today-by-3-Estate-Agents-50K-difference
- Rule of thumb the forums repeat: ignore the highest figure, anchor on the middle or the lowest credible one, and test it against sold prices. One poster summed up a common story: they went with the highest of three valuations and ended up reducing to the lowest. (Mumsnet 960981; MSE 6424141, where the consensus was to list at about the midpoint, roughly £210k.)

### 1.2 Overvaluing to win the instruction ("buying the listing")
- This is the single most discussed theme. Forum posters, and agents in comment sections, treat it as structural. Sellers pick the agent with the highest number, the agent gets a tie-in contract (8-20 weeks in the 2019 London example), and price reductions follow.
- Trade numbers backing the anecdotes:
  - GetAgent survey, reported by Property Industry Eye (Sept 2026 article): 95% of agents have seen rivals give unrealistic valuations and 89% have lost an instruction to a higher valuer. 67% think the practice is getting more common, and 95% say an initial overvaluation hurts the seller's outcome. Agents blame sellers favouring the highest figure (33%), competition (28%), market-share pressure (22%) and internal listing targets (11%). Reader comments from agents describe corporates as the main offenders and talk of a "50% failure rate". https://propertyindustryeye.com/estate-agents-at-odds-over-overvaluing-to-win-instructions/
  - The Times/Zoopla analysis, reported by Which? (2019): the largest agencies were the most likely to overvalue. Sellers with the 10 worst overvaluers paid about twice the commission of those with the 10 most accurate. A third of listings were cut, by an average £23,400 (6.9%). An earlier Which? study (2017) put the cost of overpricing at £4.3bn, with 1 in 5 England and Wales listings cut by at least 5% in 2015-16. https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/selling-a-property-the-priciest-estate-agents-are-most-likely-to-overvalue-aYDod7M6gswp
  - Agent Software "Spectre" data, reported by The Negotiator (2018): in a competitive market some agents overvalue to win instructions. Overvalued homes stay on the market longer and leave the vendor weaker in negotiation. https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/property-valuations-over-agent-software/
  - Property Solvers analysis of 89,582 Land Registry sales, reported by The Negotiator (2019): the gap between asking and achieved price was widest in London. It was £82,100 in SW London, £80,299 in NW London and £59,192 in W London, against £35,220 in N London, £32,901 in Kingston and £31,166 in Harrow. https://thenegotiator.co.uk/asking-prices-sale-prices-gap/
- Brand reputation is a criterion in its own right on London threads. Specific chains are named as habitual overpricers: KFH in SE London, and Foxtons in a WC2B thread where posters said the agent "notoriously" overprices. Dexters and KFH are said to "test the market" off-portal at inflated prices before listing on Rightmove. (Mumsnet 3218246; 5572707; 5028690.)
- Some disagreement: a minority, mostly agents, say low valuers are underpricing for a quick sale or so that a connected investor can buy. One Blackpool poster suspected the lowest valuer wanted to buy the house himself. Agent commenters on PIE argue that the seller is complicit because they reward the highest number.

### 1.3 What sellers are told to do instead: criteria for choosing the agent
- The forum and agent consensus (MSE 6424141; MSE 6035344; PIE GetAgent survey): pick the agent on
  1. a track record of selling your type of property in your postcode,
  2. average time to sell and the share of listings that actually complete,
  3. marketing quality (photos, floorplan, copy),
  4. fee and contract terms. Posters favour 4-8 week tie-ins and roughly 1-1.5% + VAT fees in London (2019 range 1.25-1.75% + VAT).
- In the GetAgent survey, the agents' own ranking of what sellers should weigh was previous sales performance (33%), similar local sales (28%) and average time to sell (22%).

## 2. Evidence used to set or check the price

### 2.1 PRIMARY: recent sold prices of comparables
- Every thread converges here. Posters stress that what counts is the price comparable homes sold for, not their asking prices. That is also what the lender's surveyor will use. MSE posters give a rough surveyor rule: completed sales of similar property within about 0.5 miles in the last ~120 days. (MSE 5923285 East London; MSE 6597469 / 6436658.)
  - https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/5923285/disaster-hsbc-have-valued-the-flat-at-20k-under-what-i-offered
- How comparables are adjusted, per agents posting on Mumsnet (5226962):
  - Location dominates: the street, schools, transport and green space.
  - Configuration (bedroom and bathroom count, layout, an extra box room or en-suite) moves price more than raw floor area. In towns and cities, £/sq ft is "often the starting point".
  - A new kitchen or bathroom "seems to have little impact on value".
- The same point shows up in "Is my house overpriced?" (Mumsnet 5169231, c.2024). A seller who had spent about £30k on radiators, loft boarding and floors asked £435k against an almost identical £400k sale on the same estate. That comparable had a downstairs shower and an open-plan kitchen. Posters told them the spend adds little and advised cutting to £400-415k.
- Unique homes break the comparable method. With no similar sale for 8 years, valuations diverge and posters fall back on agent track record and gut feel (MSE 6424141).

### 2.2 Sold-price data sources and their flaws (Rightmove / Land Registry / Zoopla AVMs)
- Rightmove sold prices are Land Registry data with a lag of several months. Some entries mislead: shared-ownership sales record only the share price, and family transfers, buy-outs and occasional outright errors also appear. One poster's record showed the wrong price and the wrong property type. (MSE 6094425, 2020.) https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/6094425/are-sold-prices-always-correct-on-rightmove
- In London the lag matters more because flats are re-priced quickly. A Zone 2 SE London seller complained that Land Registry data was outdated and missed sales that had fallen through (Mumsnet 4573075, 2022).
- Automated valuations are seen as unreliable. In MSE 6597469 (2025):
  - for one property, Zoopla said 106% of the 2010 price while Rightmove said 58.6%;
  - another poster saw Rightmove £78k against Zoopla £120k;
  - a London-type example had Zoopla at £1.093m against a local agent at £1.5m.
  - Errors in bedroom and bathroom counts feed through to the estimates.
  - Posters explain that Zoopla leans on past sales plus area trend, while Rightmove factors in live listings.
  - https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/6597469/rightmove-vs-zoopla-valuation
- SECONDARY: Mouseprice and Zoopla ranges were used as a sanity check as early as 2010 (Mumsnet 960981).

### 2.3 The mortgage surveyor as the real price-setter (down-valuations)
- Forum regulars repeat that the lender's valuer, not the estate agent, sets the effective ceiling for a mortgaged buyer. Examples:
  - East London flat (2018): asking £275k, accepted offer £280k, HSBC valued it at £260k. Advice was to renegotiate to £260k, make up the gap in cash, try another lender, or walk away. (MSE 5923285.)
  - South London 2-bed flat (2021): asking £580k, offer £550k, lender valuation £520k. The seller would not move, the buyer applied to a second lender, and posters said the seller might be "trying it on". https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/6263927/advice-on-down-valuation
  - In the 2023 cooling market, posters said sellers give more weight to a lender's valuation than to a buyer-instructed survey. A typical outcome is splitting the gap; one 2018 £10k down-valuation was settled at £5k. https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/6436658/seller-reject-survey-valuation
- London-specific trend (Mortgage Introducer, via MPA, Nov 2025): brokers report rising surveyor down-valuations concentrated on London flats in larger blocks. The causes given are thin comparable evidence, agent pricing set earlier in the year that is out of line with demand, and landlords selling up. One central London example lost 5% of value in 6 months. Family houses near strong schools are holding up better. https://www.mpamag.com/uk/news/general/london-buyers-hit-by-sharp-rise-in-surveyor-markdowns/557684
- Bidding wars, which mostly happen in London and the South East, run into the same ceiling. In best-and-final rounds, winning bids of 5-14% over asking are reported. Posters warn that these can down-value, so agents favour "proceedable" buyers (cash, no chain, proof of deposit) over the very highest bid. https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/property/4213242-Best-and-final-how-high-do-people-actually-go

## 3. Asking-price formats and strategy

| Format | What forum posters say | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed asking price (most common in England) | Buyers expect to negotiate below it. Even "fixed" prices get cut if the home sits. | MSE 5792329 (2018) |
| "Offers over" / OIEO / "guide price" | Read as a sign the agent is not confident of the value. Widely disliked by English buyers, some of whom won't view. Used to fall into a lower Rightmove price band and generate viewings. Often sells below the "offers over" figure (one buyer paid £15k under). | MSE 5792329; MSE 6106057 (2020); Mumsnet 2607870 (2016, Zone 2 London) |
| Price low to create competition, then sealed bids / best and final | Works when demand is strong (10+ viewings booked). The usual London result in hot markets is 5-10% over asking. Seen as an agent device to put pressure on bidders. | Mumsnet 4213242 (2021) |
| Scotland "offers over" | Normal there, and bids are expected above it. English buyers carry this over by mistake. | MSE 5792329 |

- The Zone 2 London OIEO example (Mumsnet 2607870): the seller needed £465k and the agent insisted on "OIEO £450k". The flat had 15 viewings and one offer, which was withdrawn at £460k. Posters said the market was telling the seller the price was too high and that buyers knock off the cost of updating work.
- The 2020 MSE thread (6106057) concluded that a conventional asking price is better than "offers over". One counter-example sold at £134k against "offers over £130k" within weeks, but posters said that worked only because the house was unusually strong for its area.

### 3.1 Price reductions and "the market is telling you"
- Forum heuristics for when to cut:
  - few viewings in the first 2-4 weeks means the price is wrong (4 viewings in 3 weeks was judged weak in Mumsnet 5169231);
  - many viewings with no offers means price or presentation is wrong (15 viewings and 1 offer in Mumsnet 2607870);
  - a seller-guide site claims about one offer per seven viewings; this was seen only in a search snippet, not read, and is unverified (https://www.homesellingexpert.co.uk/guides/selling-a-flat-with-viewings-but-no-offers).
- Cuts of about 5-7% are typical. The Which?/Times average was 6.9%. Posters warn that a listing reduced several times "doesn't look good" to buyers (Mumsnet 3218246).
- Large cuts happen when there is a leasehold problem. A Sydenham conversion flat went from £465k in 2018 to an asking price of £350k and sold in 2020 for £325k, because service charges had gone from £1.5k to £6k a year with £10k proposed (Mumsnet 3396285). A WC2B flat listed at about £800k was told by posters it was nearer £650k because of layout, dated decor and a lease under 100 years (Mumsnet 5572707).
  - https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/property/3396285-Impossible-to-sell-flat-with-extortionate-service-charge
  - https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/property/5572707-feedback-for-london-flat

## 4. What moves a London price up or down (seller-side criteria)

### Primary (cited across many threads)
1. **Recent sold comparables, adjusted for location** at street, school and station level (all threads).
2. **Floor area (£/sq ft) and configuration**: bed and bath count, layout. Bathroom-through-kitchen and single "second bedrooms" draw repeated negative feedback (Mumsnet 5572707; 2849349).
3. **Tenure and lease terms for flats**. This is London-specific in scale because most London stock sold is leasehold flats:
   - lease length (under 100 years "rings alarm bells", under 80 years cuts value sharply);
   - service charge level and trend;
   - ground rent;
   - share of freehold versus leasehold. Share-of-freehold Victorian conversions are said to outperform modern leasehold blocks (Mumsnet 4573075).
4. **Building-safety and cladding status** (EWS1) for flats since 2020-21 (MSE 6251832; Mumsnet 4573075).
5. **Outside space**: post-COVID, flats without a garden or balcony struggle. Zone 2-3 one-beds without outside space were withdrawn (Mumsnet 4573075; MSE 6251832).
6. **The mortgage valuation ceiling**, set by what a lender will lend against (section 2.3).

### Secondary / contextual
- Condition and decor: dated decor is a viewing-stage objection, but upgrades return little (Mumsnet 5169231; 5226962; 5572707).
- Noise and adjacency, such as a busy road or a railway behind, slow a sale (MSE 6035344; Mumsnet 5028690).
- Parking and driveway length in outer London and the suburbs (Mumsnet 5028690).
- Season, policy shocks and stamp-duty changes: Brexit and SDLT in 2017, the April 2025 first-time-buyer SDLT threshold (Mumsnet 2849349; MSE 6587309).
- Local new-build oversupply competing with resales (Mumsnet 2849349, West Drayton; Mumsnet 5407449).
- Buyer "proceedability", which affects price achieved rather than value (Mumsnet 4213242).

### Leasehold flat value erosion: a cross-forum consensus
- Several threads report leasehold flats flat or falling in nominal terms since about 2016-2021 while houses nearby rose:
  - Surrey flats bought at £300k now sell at £280k;
  - a NE flat went from £167k (2006) to £95k;
  - SE London Zone 2 sellers reported losses on 2016 purchases.
- Causes cited: service charges, cladding, short leases, uncertainty over leasehold reform, investor exit and new-build supply. https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/property/5407449-drop-in-value-of-leasehold-flats-all-types-in-last-5-7-years-trend-to-continue ; https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/property/4573075-london-flat-market
- The Mortgage Introducer (via MPA) piece confirms that lenders' valuers now treat London flats in large blocks as the riskiest segment.

## 5. Online versus high-street agents

- The majority forum view is that high-street agents have buyer databases and negotiating skill that can more than cover their fee. One poster said their agent "paid for their fees" through negotiation. Online-agent sellers report weaker viewer commitment, cancelled viewings and some buyers avoiding the listings altogether. (MSE 6289166, 2021; MSE 6580748, 2025; Mumsnet 2849349, 2017, where viewings started within hours of switching from an online to a high-street agent.)
  - https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/6289166/high-street-estate-agent-or-online
  - https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/6580748/purplebricks-vs-local-estate-agent
- The minority view, with evidence: Yopa produced two offers within two weeks after 16 weeks without one on the high street, and a Purplebricks flat sold in a week at asking. Online agents are said to work in active markets for standard stock.
- On valuation: online agents also overvalue. Purplebricks valued the 2019 London flat at £325k against a realistic £310k, and their fee is paid up front so there is no incentive to sell. Fees in that London example: Purplebricks £1,400 flat against 1.25-1.75% + VAT on the high street, and Nested at 1.75% including VAT.

## 6. London versus elsewhere (from this stream)

- **London**:
  - Leasehold flat issues dominate (lease, service charge, cladding, share of freehold).
  - The asking-to-sold gaps in Property Solvers' 2019 data were the largest in the country.
  - Flats in large blocks are more prone to down-valuation.
  - Chain brands (Foxtons, KFH, Dexters) are named for overpricing and off-market "testing".
  - In hot pockets (SE London family houses, March 2024, sold within a month and within £25k of asking) sealed bids and best-and-final are common.
  - Buyers increasingly "pick on unchangeable features" and arrive with architects' plans and low offers.
- **Rest of England**: the same three-agent and comparables process applies. Unusual homes and rural stock lack comparables, and plot or land value matters more (Mumsnet 5226962).
- **Scotland**: "offers over" is the norm and bids are expected above it. The Home Report includes a surveyor valuation, unlike England (MSE 5792329).

## 7. Disagreements and caveats
- Sellers blame agents for overvaluing; agents blame sellers for choosing the highest number. Both are documented in the PIE comment section and in forum threads.
- "Offers over" is said to raise viewings but to annoy English buyers. There is no quantified evidence either way in these threads.
- Some posters say renovation adds value; agents and most posters say it adds little beyond what comparables show.
- A down-valuation is described both as "just the surveyor's opinion" and as hard evidence of the market price. In practice, how much it matters depends on whether the buyer needs the mortgage and how desperate each side is.
- Forum samples skew toward problem sales: people post when a sale is not going well.

## 8. Implications for an area/price tool (stream synthesis)
- Show recent completed sales (Land Registry) next to live asking prices. Show the asking-to-sold gap and the share of listings reduced, by area and property type.
- Show £/sq ft by area (EPC floor area joined to sales), split houses from flats and leasehold from freehold.
- Flag, for flats: lease length, service charge, building-safety status and outside space.
- Warn that automated valuations and agent "market appraisals" are not lender valuations, and that sparse comparables raise down-valuation risk.
