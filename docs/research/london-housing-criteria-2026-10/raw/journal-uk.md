# UK professional journals: buyer/renter criteria and valuation practice (stream: journal-uk)

Research date: 5 October 2026. Sources: Property Industry Eye (PIE), The Negotiator, Knight Frank research articles. Every source listed in `journal-uk.sources.json` was fetched and read unless marked otherwise.

**Method note / gaps.** The session's WebSearch budget was used up before this stream started, so I found articles through the publications' own site search (PIE `?s=`, The Negotiator `?s=`) and the Knight Frank research index. Savills' research pages returned HTTP 429 on four tries. Estate Agent Today returned HTTP 403. RICS Modus' homepage had no residential-valuation articles. So Savills, EAT/LAT, RICS Modus, Property Week, Estates Gazette and FT/Times are **not represented** in what I read; another stream should cover them. Most trade-press articles report surveys by agents, portals or lenders, and those surveys often have commercial motives (GetAgent, LRG, MFS, London Square). Treat their percentages as indicative only.

## 1. How buyers search: primary filters versus secondary "nice-to-haves"

### 1.1 Primary filters (hard constraints)
- **Price, location and bedroom count are still the core filters.** GetAgent's 2026 agent survey found that most buyers still search with the traditional portal filters (location, price, bedrooms) and browse listings. Only 22% of agents had heard buyers mention AI as a discovery route, and 93% saw no AI visibility effect yet. Even so, 84% expect AI search to become the main route eventually. [PIE – GetAgent AI](https://propertyindustryeye.com/estate-agents-expect-ai-to-transform-how-buyers-find-homes/)
- Rightmove's conversational search, launched in 2026, keeps location, price and bedrooms as the basic parameters. It then adds layered free-text constraints: solar panels, near transport links, green space, and "cottage with sea view within a 30-minute commute". [PIE – Rightmove AI search](https://propertyindustryeye.com/rightmove-upgrades-ai-tools-to-improve-property-search/)
- **Tenure is a top keyword.** In Zoopla's annual search data, "freehold" was the No. 1 search keyword for the second year running. "Cottage" and "double garage" entered the top five, "in need of modernisation" rose 134 places and "study" rose 22 places. 80% of buyers look at the floorplan before the photos. The three-bed semi is the most-viewed property type. [PIE – Zoopla 2025 trends](https://propertyindustryeye.com/whats-next-for-the-housing-market-in-2025-home-buyer-behaviour-and-user-trends-unveiled/)
  - *Implication for London:* tenure (freehold vs leasehold) acts almost as a filter, because flats are leasehold. Zoopla reports UK flat prices falling for 15 straight months (−1.3% y/y) while houses rose +1.3%. In London, a third of flats coming to market have been listed before, and only about 3 in 10 London flats find a buyer within three months, against about half in the north. [PIE – Zoopla flats](https://propertyindustryeye.com/house-price-growth-stalls-as-flat-prices-fall-for-15th-month-in-a-row/)

### 1.2 Ranked "what matters" surveys
- **Market Financial Solutions (2024 repeat of a 2021 survey):** broadband/mobile connectivity 82%, garden/outdoor space 76%, then a three-way tie at 74% for nearby parks/public space, build quality/finish and **transport links**. Transport links jumped from 14th to joint 3rd, which the firm attributes to the return to office working. Extension/conversion potential fell from 8th to 14th. [Negotiator – MFS](https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/uk-housing-market-news/quality-transport-links-rise-up-list-of-homebuyers-priorities/)
- **What triggers an offer (LRG, 3,700 buyers in England and Wales, Oct 2025):** updated kitchens and bathrooms 76%, attractive garden 55%, spotless cleanliness 49%, low-maintenance outdoor space 30%, neutral décor 26%, kerb appeal 23%. LRG concludes that buyers want move-in-ready homes because cost-of-living pressure leaves little budget for refurbishment, and that cleanliness now counts for more than kerb appeal. [Negotiator – LRG offers](https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/uk-housing-market-news/report-reveals-major-shift-in-what-triggers-homebuyers-offers-says-big-estate-agency/)
  - **Disagreement:** Zoopla reports *rising* searches for "in need of modernisation", while LRG reports that turnkey condition triggers offers. The two can both be true: value-seekers filter for fixer-uppers, but most offers still go to ready homes.
- **What deters buyers (LRG, Sept 2026):** the cost of moving (75% want it lower), certainty that the sale completes (34%), a simpler process (30%), and only then lower mortgage rates (28%). Under-45s care most about rates (70%). Over-55s care most about certainty (39% vs 23%). [Negotiator – LRG deterrents](https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/products-services-news/mortgage-rates-arent-only-factor-deterring-demand-research-reveals/)

### 1.3 Secondary and emerging criteria
- **Schools (families):** In a Santander survey of 2,000 parents (2025), 73% would pay extra to be in a top-school catchment, up from 63%. The average premium they would pay is 15% (about £43.5k on a £290k home); one in ten would pay more than 25%. Parents would move 31 miles on average, up 9 miles. Homes near the top 50 schools average £538k, 42% above the UK average. [PIE – catchment](https://propertyindustryeye.com/most-parents-willing-to-pay-premium-to-live-in-top-catchment-areas/)
- **Overheating / comfort in hot weather (new in 2026):** after near-38°C heat, buyers are asking about insulation, ventilation, shading, south-facing rooms and hot home offices. Air conditioning helps a home stand out at the top end but doesn't yet add much to value on its own. [PIE – overheating](https://propertyindustryeye.com/the-new-viewing-question-how-hot-does-this-house-get/)
- **Aesthetic and lifestyle keywords:** Rightmove's "AI Keywords" feature names "exposed brick", "river view" and "underfloor heating" as terms that drive engagement. [PIE – Rightmove AI](https://propertyindustryeye.com/rightmove-upgrades-ai-tools-to-improve-property-search/)
- **Views:** Rightmove puts sea-view asking prices up 27% since 2019 against 22% nationally. The biggest premium is in West Mersea, at about +51% (£222k). Experts caution that flood resilience, insurance costs and transport also matter. [Negotiator – sea view](https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/uk-housing-market-news/rightmove-asking-prices-of-a-sea-view-homes-up-27-since-2019/)
- **Unmeasured "soft" attributes in valuation:** ValPal's 2026 seller tool lets owners flag things AVMs miss: views (e.g. a river view), station proximity, landscaped gardens, kitchen extensions. [PIE – ValPal](https://propertyindustryeye.com/new-tool-gives-agents-early-insight-into-sellers-price-expectations/)

## 2. What is London-specific

1. **Station proximity carries a larger price premium in London than in other UK cities.** Nationwide's hedonic analysis (July 2024 to June 2025), controlling for type, bedrooms and neighbourhood, compared homes 500 m from a station with homes 1,500 m away. The premium was **£42,700 (8.0%) in London**, against £10,900 (4.9%) in Greater Manchester and £8,800 (4.6%) in Glasgow. All three premiums were smaller than in 2021. **Over 80% of Londoners** say station proximity matters when they buy or rent, against about 60% in Manchester and Glasgow. [PIE – Nationwide transport](https://propertyindustryeye.com/good-transport-links-still-add-most-value-to-house-prices-in-key-uk-cities/)
2. **Flat-heavy, leasehold-heavy stock is underperforming.** London flats relist more often and sell more slowly (see 1.1). This makes tenure, service charges and building issues effectively primary criteria in London in a way they are not in house-dominated regions. [PIE – Zoopla flats](https://propertyindustryeye.com/house-price-growth-stalls-as-flat-prices-fall-for-15th-month-in-a-row/)
3. **Space versus commute, with an outward pull.** New-build sell-through in the year to March 2026 was Essex 27.6%, Herts 23.7%, Surrey 21.2% and Kent 18.3%, against **London 8.8%**. London new-build prices fell 1.5% while Herts rose 3.5%. Buyers want "value for money and more living space", green space and amenities, while keeping a commute into London. [PIE – new-build commuter](https://propertyindustryeye.com/new-build-buyers-favour-commuter-counties-over-london/) Internal migration in the year to mid-2025: 420,500 left London for elsewhere in the UK and 288,500 arrived; 235,000 came from abroad and 183,000 left for abroad. Destinations include Braintree, Maidstone, Milton Keynes and Hemel Hempstead. [PIE – Cleverly](https://propertyindustryeye.com/london-house-prices-could-fall-by-100000-under-cleverlys-mayoral-plans/)
4. **First-time-buyer stock is thin.** Yopa (Q3 2026) finds only 1.8% of listed homes in London suit FTBs, against 2.1% in Plymouth. Demand is strongest in Liverpool (51% of suitable stock sold STC) and much weaker in the South. [PIE – Yopa FTB](https://propertyindustryeye.com/first-time-buyer-demand-slips-as-just-1-8-of-homes-deemed-suitable/)
5. **Selling and completion take longer.** OneDome (2024 data) puts the UK average at 108 days from offer to completion. Islington takes 150 days, Bexley, Greenwich and Redbridge over 125, and Tower Hamlets 62. [Negotiator – OneDome](https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/uk-housing-market-news/london-buyers-wait-the-longest-to-complete-property-sales/) Zoopla (April 2026) says London homes took about 6 days longer to sell than a year earlier: Harrow up 65% to 54 days, South East London up 34% to 43 days. [PIE – Zoopla enquiries](https://propertyindustryeye.com/buyer-enquiries-rebound-as-well-priced-homes-continue-to-sell/)
6. **Renter competition is extreme, and space is not the binding criterion.** Foxtons (Aug 2026) reports **24 renters per new London listing**, the 2026 high, against a year-to-date average of 17.9. The average rent is £571/week, and £632/week in Central London. Registrations rose 5.2% in West London but fell 17.3% in North London. [PIE – Foxtons](https://propertyindustryeye.com/renters-face-fiercest-competition-of-2026-as-demand-jumps/) Lomond (Aug 2026) puts London's average rent at £2,418/month, 76% above the UK's £1,369. Renters nationally spend almost a third of income on rent and increasingly choose on "affordability, connectivity and lifestyle". [PIE – Lomond](https://propertyindustryeye.com/renters-now-spending-almost-a-third-of-income-on-rent-as-demand-shifts/)
7. **The prime segment runs on its own logic.**
   - Knight Frank (Sept 2026): prime central London prices rose +0.3% q/q, the first quarterly rise in four years, and are −2% y/y and 22% below the August 2015 peak. Prime outer London is −0.6% y/y. There were 121 £10m+ deals, with spend up 14% to £2.4bn. Knight Frank says the drivers are macro rather than property attributes: mortgage rates heading toward 3.5%, the absence of bad headlines, and buyers "sensing value". [KF – no news](https://www.knightfrank.co.uk/research/article/2026/10/no-news-has-been-good-news-for-the-prime-london-market); [PIE – KF prime](https://propertyindustryeye.com/prime-london-house-prices-rise-for-first-time-in-four-years-as-rents-accelerate/)
   - LonRes (Aug 2026) gives a contrasting picture for prime central London: transactions −19% y/y (the weakest August since 2008), achieved prices −7% y/y, and **half of stock reduced in price**. [PIE – LonRes](https://propertyindustryeye.com/prime-london-property-market-suffers-worst-august-in-18-years/) **Disagreement:** Knight Frank's index shows stabilisation while LonRes' achieved-price data shows continued falls. The likely reason is different samples and methods (a valuation index versus transactions).
   - Prime rents: prime outer London +3% y/y with **8.9 prospective tenants per new listing** and new listings −6.4%; prime central London has 5.6 tenants per listing, the highest in four years. Landlords are pricing in Renters' Rights Act risk and buy-to-let rates of 4.7%. [KF – rents](https://www.knightfrank.co.uk/research/article/2026/10/prime-london-rents-rise-as-tenants-feel-the-pre-budget-squeeze)
8. **International buyers (relevant to migrant source countries).**
   - London Square surveyed 1,000 people in New York, Singapore, Dubai, Hong Kong and Paris. 64% would relocate to London; they associate it with stability (70%), culture (83%) and education (79%). A London two-bed is about 23% cheaper than New York's and about 60% cheaper than Singapore's, and foreign buyers' transaction costs are lower than in Hong Kong or Singapore. A weak pound adds to the appeal. [Negotiator – London Square](https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/uk-housing-market-news/london-remains-attractive-for-international-buyers/)
   - Rightmove (May 2025): US enquiries were up about 20% y/y, an eight-year high. 47% sought 1-2 beds (investment or pied-à-terre) and 32% sought 3-4 beds (family relocation). Top targets were Edinburgh, Westminster, Camden and Glasgow. Some short-stay Americans rent rather than buy because of stamp duty. [Negotiator – US buyers](https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/uk-housing-market-news/property-buyers-from-us-defy-trump-tariff-chaos-rightmove/)
   - *Takeaway:* international buyers compare cities first (price per m² against their home market, currency, stability, schools, tax and stamp duty) and pick a property second.

## 3. How sellers and agents decide what a home can sell for

### 3.1 Primary valuation inputs used in practice
- **Comparable evidence for the same type and attributes, not area averages.** The Negotiator (2019) describes data tools that match on location *plus* property type and attributes, and a "Sold Tracker" that compares initial asking prices with achieved prices. Agents publish postcode-level market velocity, the buyer mix, and achieved-versus-asking ratios to win instructions. Richard Donnell (Hometrack/Zoopla) notes that consumers "think locally"; 31% already know their target area. [Negotiator – local knowledge](https://thenegotiator.co.uk/features/local-knowledge/)
- **AVM and data products.** Hometrack's agent product (2024) draws on comparables with photos and floorplans, Zoopla listing data, about 200m data points (pricing structure, supply and demand, affordability) and lender survey data. It competes with PriceHubble/Dataloft, REalyse and When Fresh. No accuracy figures are published. [Negotiator – Hometrack](https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/products-services-news/hometrack-launches-arm-offering-valution-data-for-estate-agencies/)
- **AVM start point, then human adjustment.** ValPal's flow runs AVM estimate → seller's own price expectation (slider plus reasons) → agent market appraisal, with the agent deciding whether views, station proximity, landscaping or an extension justify an uplift. [PIE – ValPal](https://propertyindustryeye.com/new-tool-gives-agents-early-insight-into-sellers-price-expectations/)
- **Market-condition overlays:** sales-to-stock conversion, relisting rates, share of stock reduced, and time on market. Zoopla's advice to price for "local market conditions" applies especially to flats and larger southern homes. [PIE – Zoopla flats](https://propertyindustryeye.com/house-price-growth-stalls-as-flat-prices-fall-for-15th-month-in-a-row/) The RICS survey (Sept 2026) adds buyer enquiries at −19 net balance, agreed sales at −17 and new instructions at −17. [KF – signs of a turn](https://www.knightfrank.co.uk/research/article/2026/9/signs-of-a-turn-in-the-uk-housing-market)

### 3.2 The overvaluation problem (the central practitioner debate)
- GetAgent's 2026 agent survey: 95% have seen rivals give unrealistic valuations, 89% have lost instructions to higher valuations, and 67% say the practice is increasing. The causes they cite are sellers choosing the highest figure (33%), competition (28%), market-share pressure (22%) and internal targets (11%). 83% say sellers should judge agents on other metrics: % of asking price achieved (33%), track record with similar local homes (28%) and average time to sell (22%). [PIE – overvaluing](https://propertyindustryeye.com/estate-agents-at-odds-over-overvaluing-to-win-instructions/)
- **Cost of overpricing (Rightmove, Aug 2025):** a third of homes needed a price cut. Homes that never needed one sold in about **32 days**; those that did took about **99 days**. [PIE – Rightmove realistic pricing](https://propertyindustryeye.com/realistic-pricing-sees-most-active-july-since-2020-says-rightmove/) Zoopla (Apr 2026) agrees: well-priced homes sell as quickly as a year ago, while overpriced ones sit unsold. [PIE – Zoopla enquiries](https://propertyindustryeye.com/buyer-enquiries-rebound-as-well-priced-homes-continue-to-sell/)
- Simon Bradbury (PIE opinion, Oct 2026) cites Zoopla's claim that nearly half of UK listings don't sell within three years, against 85%+ in the US, and blames overvaluation. He offers two explanations. One is honest error, especially for unusual homes with few comparables. The other is deliberate overvaluation to secure a long sole-agency contract and then cut the price later. Rightmove's "3 P's" puts Price first, then Presentation and Promotion. [PIE – cock-up or conspiracy](https://propertyindustryeye.com/cock-up-or-conspiracy/)
- Trust context: estate agents score 32% on the Ipsos Veracity Index (2025), and only 51% of movers trust their agent. [PIE – candour premium](https://propertyindustryeye.com/the-candour-premium-britains-rudest-estate-agent-proves-honesty-pays/)

### 3.3 Presentation as a price lever
- LRG's offer triggers (updated kitchen and bathroom, cleanliness, garden) are, in practice, the "Presentation" P. Valuers rarely add value for presentation, but in agents' experience it decides whether a home achieves the asking price. [Negotiator – LRG offers](https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/uk-housing-market-news/report-reveals-major-shift-in-what-triggers-homebuyers-offers-says-big-estate-agency/)

## 4. Other UK cities and regions compared with London
- **Station premium:** London 8.0% against about 4.6-4.9% in Manchester and Glasgow (see 2.1).
- **Speed:** Scotland is fastest at about 15 days to sell (Zoopla). Glasgow tops the UK lists. Liverpool is the fastest in England and Wales at 19 days. [PIE – Zoopla 2025](https://propertyindustryeye.com/whats-next-for-the-housing-market-in-2025-home-buyer-behaviour-and-user-trends-unveiled/)
- **Regional renters** look for 2-3 bed family homes in Kent, rural areas between Liverpool and Manchester, and Yorkshire terraces and semis. Kent new tenancies rose 121% y/y. [PIE – Lomond](https://propertyindustryeye.com/renters-now-spending-almost-a-third-of-income-on-rent-as-demand-shifts/)
- **Flatsharers:** in SpareRoom's data, 37.8% spend over half their income on rent and only 15.3% stay under 30%. Room rents have exceeded budgets in about half of 35 cities. Edinburgh's gap is the largest at £86/month. [PIE – SpareRoom](https://propertyindustryeye.com/renters-squeezed-as-asking-rents-outstrip-budgets/)
- **Tenancy horizon:** TDS (2,000+ tenants) finds an average tenancy of 4.7 years, about a year longer than a decade ago. 44% expect to rent permanently, 75% would rather buy, and nearly half struggled to find a suitable property. [Negotiator – TDS](https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/rental-market/tenants-expect-to-stay-renting-despite-desire-to-buy-property/)

## 5. Synthesis: primary versus secondary (UK trade-press view)

| Tier | Buyer criterion | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | Budget / affordability (price, mortgage, moving costs) | GetAgent filters; LRG deterrents 75% costs |
| Primary | Location (area, commute, **station distance in London**) | Nationwide 8% London premium; MFS transport 74% |
| Primary | Bedrooms / size / type | Portal filters; US buyers 1-2 vs 3-4 bed split |
| Primary in London | Tenure (freehold vs leasehold), flat vs house | Zoopla "freehold" No. 1 keyword; London flats relist a third |
| Primary (families) | School catchment | Santander 73%, 15% premium |
| Secondary | Outdoor space / garden, parks | MFS 76%/74%; LRG 55% |
| Secondary | Condition (updated kitchen/bath, finish) | LRG 76%; MFS 74% |
| Secondary | Broadband / connectivity | MFS 82% |
| Niche / emerging | Overheating, AC, study/home office, views, solar | PIE heat; Zoopla "study"; Rightmove AI queries |

| Tier | Valuation input | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | Recent comparable sold prices (same type and attributes, hyper-local) | Negotiator local knowledge; Hometrack comparables |
| Primary | Market conditions: stock, sales-to-stock, time on market, share reduced | Zoopla, Rightmove, LonRes, RICS |
| Secondary | AVM estimate as a starting point | ValPal, Hometrack |
| Secondary | Unmeasured attributes: views, station, garden, extensions | ValPal |
| Distorting | Competitive overvaluation to win instructions; sellers' anchoring | GetAgent 95%/89%; Bradbury |
