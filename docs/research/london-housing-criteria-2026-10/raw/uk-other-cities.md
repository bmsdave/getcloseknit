# Other UK cities, Scotland and Northern Ireland: how search criteria and valuation differ from London

Stream: Other UK cities and Scotland. Research date: October 2026.

Method note: the session's web-search budget was used up before this stream started, so sources were found through site search and listings: the MoneySavingExpert (MSE) forum board, The Negotiator site search, Hamptons research listings, Zoopla and Rightmove news pages, OpenAlex for papers, ONS and mygov.scot. Reddit, Property Industry Eye, Estate Agent Today, Savills and Registers of Scotland could not be read (blocked or HTTP 429). Fetched pages were turned into notes by a summariser. The key numbers in the two academic PDFs (Thanos & White; Gibbons et al.) were checked against the extracted full text, and one wrong figure from the summariser was corrected (see section 3). Treat thread-level details from forum summaries as approximate.

---

## 1. The big structural difference: affordability sets which criteria matter at all

Outside London, the share of income a home costs is far lower. Because of that, buyers can trade up on size, garden, parking and house type. In London the budget is often the only binding constraint.

- **Price-to-earnings ratios, ONS 2025 data (released 26 March 2026):**
  - England: the median home (£300k) costs 7.6 times median earnings (£39.3k).
  - Wales: 6.0.
  - London: 10.6, the highest region.
  - North East: 5.0, the lowest region.
  - Most affordable local authorities: Hyndburn and Kingston upon Hull, both 4.1.
  - Least affordable: Kensington & Chelsea at 25.2.
  - Affordability has improved since its 2021 peak: earnings are up 25% while prices are up only 5%.
  - Source: https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/housing/bulletins/housingaffordabilityinenglandandwales/2025
- **The 2024 edition** (released March 2025) gave England 7.7, Wales 5.9 and London 11.1. Only 9% of local authorities (27) were under 5x. The most affordable were Blaenau Gwent (3.8), Burnley (3.9) and Blackpool (3.9), and the least affordable was K&C (27.1). The North East had a tight cluster of authorities under 5x. Source: https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/housing/bulletins/housingaffordabilityinenglandandwales/2024
- **Rent as a share of income (ONS, financial year ending 2024, released 18 August 2025):**
  - England: 36.3% (£1,232 rent against £3,396 monthly income).
  - London: 41.6%, the least affordable region.
  - North East: 19.8%, the most affordable region.
  - Wales: 25.9%. Northern Ireland: 25.3%.
  - Lowest local authority: Hartlepool at 15.9%. Highest: K&C at 74.3%. All ten least affordable areas are in London.
  - Outside London, the least affordable areas named are Bristol, Bath & NE Somerset, Brighton, Trafford, Sevenoaks and Watford.
  - So Bristol and the commuter belt behave like "mini-Londons" for renters.
  - Source: https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/housing/bulletins/privaterentalaffordabilityengland/2024
- **North–south price momentum.**
  - Rightmove, reported by The Negotiator in June 2026: prices rose 63% in Manchester over ten years against 7% in London. All four of the fastest-growing suburbs are in Greater Manchester, and no southern city is in the top 10.
  - Rightmove's explanation is that markets with lower starting prices had more room to grow. Local agents point to jobs, inward investment, transport links and lifestyle.
  - Source: https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/uk-housing-market-news/manchester-fastest-london-slowest-north-south-divide-in-house-prices/
- **Zoopla House Price Index, September 2026.**
  - Annual price change: North West +3.1%, Scotland +2.6%, North East +2.3%, London −1.0%, South East −0.7%. Liverpool +4.3%, Newcastle +2.7%, Glasgow +2.6%.
  - Share of homes selling within three months: about 75% in Scotland against about 30% in London.
  - Semi-detached houses were the most resilient type (+1.7%), while flats fell 1.3%.
  - Source: https://www.zoopla.co.uk/discover/property-news/house-price-index/
- **Disagreement between sources.**
  - Kate Faulkner's tracker (The Negotiator, October 2025) shows Manchester down 4.2% on Land Registry figures and Belfast up 7.6% on Hometrack.
  - Only 12 of 30 cities beat inflation since 2005, and 17 were still below 2022 levels.
  - So whether a city is "hot" depends on the index used: Land Registry completions lag, while Zoopla and Hometrack are faster.
  - Source: https://thenegotiator.co.uk/columns/town-and-city-tracker-manchester-lows-and-belfast-highs/
- **Monthly cost pressure** (Zoopla via The Negotiator, June 2026):
  - About 5% mortgage rates added roughly £125 a month to a typical payment.
  - The increase was £232 a month for a London first-time buyer and £66 in the North East.
  - Three in five homes listed since January had not sold, and sales agreed were down 7%.
  - Source: https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/uk-housing-market-news/three-in-five-homes-not-selling-reports-zoopla/

**Implication for criteria:** In London, the top filters are price, transport and commute (tube or rail zone), and floor area. Outside London, price is less binding, so property-type criteria come first in the search: detached or semi, number of bedrooms, garden, driveway or garage, and a home office.

## 2. Primary vs secondary criteria outside London: evidence

### Primary
1. **Property type and size: house rather than flat, semi or detached, extra bedroom or home office.**
   - Zoopla's 2026 figures (semis rising, flats falling) match what agents say.
   - A Manchester agent (The Negotiator regional report, 2022) described intense demand for "big houses with bigger gardens" and homes that support remote working. A three-bed semi was about £300k and selling 4–5% over asking.
   - On MSE, a family of five in an 863 sq ft three-bed terrace wants a dedicated home office, a bigger garden, extension or loft potential, and to be near family. Respondents add secondary-school catchments as a deciding factor (2026).
   - Sources: https://thenegotiator.co.uk/features/regional-property-reports/regional-report-70/ ; https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/6685015/move-now-or-wait-until-2030-family-of-5-needing-more-space
2. **Off-street parking, driveway or garage.**
   - A West Yorkshire agent (Ripponden, The Negotiator 2023) lists gardens, garages and parking as the top priorities, together with M62 access to Leeds and Manchester. Average sale price was about £350k, with roughly three weeks to sell.
   - In an MSE thread about a stalled four-bed townhouse in Bicester (cut from £425k to £400k), ten respondents named unclear or shared parking, which looked like a shared court rather than a driveway, as a main reason for few viewings. They also cited competition from semis and detached houses at the same price.
   - In a Glasgow-area thread, respondents warned that turning a parking area into garden reduces appeal to many buyers.
   - Sources: https://thenegotiator.co.uk/features/regional-property-reports/regional-report-81/ ; https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/6684254/why-aren-t-we-getting-more-viewings-honest-feedback-welcome ; https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/6682564/home-improvements-translating-into-value
   - *London contrast:* parking is a secondary or niche filter in inner London. Outside London it is a deal-breaker for most family buyers, because car dependency is higher.
3. **Garden and green space.**
   - Hedonic study of 1 million transactions in England, 1996–2008 (Gibbons, Mourato & Resende, SERC DP74 and *Environmental & Resource Economics*):
     - A 1 percentage point increase in the ward's share of domestic gardens, green space or water raises prices by about 1%, roughly £2,000 capitalised.
     - National Park designation adds about 5%, around £9,400 at the 2008 mean of £194k, though imprecisely estimated.
     - Greater distance to rivers, National Parks and National Trust sites lowers prices.
     - The model uses Travel-to-Work-Area fixed effects, so the estimates are within local labour markets.
   - Source: https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/33594/1/sercdp0074.pdf
4. **Schools.**
   - Gibbons, Machin & Silva (SERC DP18, 2009; *Journal of Urban Economics* 2013) compare otherwise similar homes on either side of admissions-authority boundaries across England. A one standard deviation rise in school value-added, or in intake prior attainment, raises prices by about 3%.
   - That premium applies nationally, not only in London.
   - Hamptons (June 2026) reports falling rolls easing the pressure:
     - 71% of primaries offered places to every first-choice applicant in 2025/26.
     - Only 62 schools offered places to fewer than half of first-choice applicants, down from 134 in 2015/16.
     - London has seen 15 primary closures since 2024, out of 38 in England.
     - Reception numbers are forecast to fall 2.5% over four years.
     - Fertility fell to 1.4 in 2024.
   - The school premium may therefore soften, first in London and then in the regions.
   - Northern Ireland: agents cite the "high quality of education" (including selective schools) as a main draw for returnees (CPS Property, 2024).
   - Sources: https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/30814/8/sercdp0018.pdf ; https://www.hamptons.co.uk/articles/getting-into-an-oversubscribed-school-is-becoming-easier ; https://thenegotiator.co.uk/features/regional-property-reports/northern-ireland/
5. **Commute and road or rail access.**
   - NerdWallet survey (n=1,000, September 2021, via The Negotiator):
     - 92% of hybrid workers would accept a longer commute for the right home.
     - 43% would add 15–29 minutes and 32% would add 30–44 minutes.
     - 69% commute three days a week or fewer.
     - 58% would go further to afford a first home or move up the ladder.
   - Outside London, commute is usually measured by motorway access (M62, M6), not by tube zone.
   - Sources: https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/the-commuter-belt-is-widening-says-new-survey/ ; https://thenegotiator.co.uk/features/regional-property-reports/regional-report-81/

### Secondary
- **Running costs, EPC and building age.**
  - Surrey thread (MSE, 11 participants): a 240 sqm converted stable with solid 9-inch brick walls and EPC band D (65). Opinion split on heating: some expect bills to "at least double", while one experienced owner said solid brick performs better than assumed.
  - Respondents mention external woodwork repainting costing £10k+ and cast-iron gutters.
  - Larger regional and commuter-belt homes raise the importance of EPC, heating and maintenance.
  - Source: https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/6680888/maintenance-and-running-costs-for-a-larger-older-house
- **Building era as a style signal.**
  - Hamptons (May 2026): Georgian homes £531/sq ft, 2020s new builds £511, 1920s–30s £507, a spread of about 17%.
  - Georgian average about £687k against a 2000s average of about £328k.
  - London holds the largest share of Victorian stock (28.5%), so a "period" preference is more London-heavy. In the regions, new-build and interwar semis carry more weight.
  - Source: https://www.hamptons.co.uk/articles/period-drama-what-age-of-property-commands-the-highest-prices
- **Accessibility and step-free living for downsizers.** A Belfast thread (9 participants) about a two-bed bungalow: 5–6 steps at the doors and more to the lower garden undermine what bungalow buyers want. Advice was to cut the price rather than install ramps, or to market the views to first-time buyers. Source: https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/6680543/bungalow-proving-to-be-a-niche-property
- **Views, countryside and lifestyle** for South-East leavers (Dartmoor, Dorset, Somerset agents): people moving to be near family, remote workers, and downsizers wanting space for live-in carers. Sources: https://thenegotiator.co.uk/features/regional-property-reports/regional-report-81/ ; https://thenegotiator.co.uk/features/regional-property-reports/regional-report-70/

## 3. Scotland: a different selling system changes how buyers and sellers think about price

**Process (mygov.scot):**
- The seller must provide a **Home Report** within nine days of a request. It contains:
  - a surveyor's single survey with a **valuation** (an opinion of what the home is worth),
  - a property questionnaire covering 16 areas, including council tax band, past damage and alterations,
  - an EPC.
- Exemptions include off-plan new builds and seasonal holiday homes.
- Buyers' solicitors **note interest**. If several parties do, the seller sets a **closing date** and sealed offers are submitted by letter from the solicitor.
- The contract becomes binding once the exchanged **missives** are concluded.
- Most homes are marketed at "offers over" or "offers around" a minimum price.
- Sources: https://www.mygov.scot/buying-a-home/home-report ; https://www.mygov.scot/buying-a-home/making-an-offer

**What this means for valuation:**
- **The surveyor's valuation, not the agent's, anchors the price.**
  - Scottish agents interviewed in 2014 (The Negotiator) said Home Reports made "market value transparent". Buyers make value judgements from them, and speculative overpricing is reduced.
  - Problems raised: lenders sometimes rejected Home Report valuations; the report goes stale after about three months, so it has to be refreshed for unsold homes; it costs sellers money up front. The Law Society of Scotland's representative argued it was not fit for purpose after the 2008 crash.
  - Source: https://thenegotiator.co.uk/features/home-reports-model-follow/
- **Asking prices are set from comparable sales.** Lo et al. (2021, *IJHMA*) used about 79,000 Aberdeen transactions from 1998–2013. After the Home Report, spatial autocorrelation of prices rose, meaning asking prices tracked nearby comparables more closely. The practice of setting artificially low asking prices to draw bidders "dwindled". This source was read at abstract level only. Source: https://pure.ulster.ac.uk/en/publications/f3cbd6be-6056-4beb-a95e-d9d147657fe8
- **Bid premium over asking is the market's pressure gauge.** Thanos & White (2013, *Housing Studies*) studied about 19,300 Aberdeen sales from 2004–07, mostly first-price sealed-bid sales; only 6.8% were sold by private treaty or fixed price.
  - The premium of sale price over asking was under 5% in 1995–2001, when 20–25% of homes sold at a fixed price.
  - It rose above 10% from 2003 and reached about 35% of asking at the Q1 2007 peak. The mean was 42.7% in Q1 2007 against 18.6% in 2004.
  - At the first sign of the credit crunch the premium dropped about 32% within two quarters, while asking prices lagged.
  - The authors conclude that sellers' asking prices are conservative and sticky. Buyers' bid premiums react quickly, almost like a financial market, and sealed bids let sellers capture the buyer's surplus.
  - Correction: the fetch summariser wrongly gave "about 5%" and "2–3%" premiums for Glasgow. The full text shows the study covers Aberdeen with the figures above.
  - Source: http://irep.ntu.ac.uk/id/eprint/7701/1/217709_PubSub962_White%2520.pdf
- **Forum reality in 2026:**
  - A first-time buyer on MSE saw the closing date for a four-in-a-block flat brought forward to a few hours after their viewing. Respondents said this is normal Scottish practice.
  - Consensus advice: appoint a solicitor *before* viewing, because offers without one carry no weight. Have the mortgage in principle ready. The Home Report replaces most of the buyer's own survey.
  - Scotland was described as fast-moving while England's flat market is weak.
  - Source: https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/6684715/ftb-question-accelerated-closing-date
  - Near Glasgow (good schools, short commute), a buyer paid **10% over Home Report value**. Respondents said a loft conversion with a building warrant adds value; kitchens date quickly; and every street has a price ceiling, citing a £500k bungalow with expensive upgrades that did not sell. Source: https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/6682564/home-improvements-translating-into-value
- **Scottish market levels:**
  - Registers of Scotland, via The Negotiator (June 2026): average price £198k (+4%), 104,408 sales (+5%). Over ten years Glasgow is up 57%, Aberdeen down 29% (oil), and semis up 50%.
  - DJ Alexander (July 2025): +5.9% to £191.9k against +3.1% in England and Wales. Range from Inverclyde (£113.7k) to East Lothian (£297k, the Edinburgh commuter belt).
  - Sources: https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/uk-housing-market-news/highest-ever-values-recorded-in-scottish-property-market/ ; https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/uk-housing-market-news/scottish-housing-market-much-stronger-than-rest-of-uk/
- **Scottish renting differs:**
  - Hamptons summarises the 2022–25 rent freeze and caps: 0%, then 3%, then up to 12%.
  - Only 28% of Scottish tenants had an in-tenancy rise, against 53% in England. Increases on new lets were 8.2% a year against 6.7% across Great Britain.
  - Rental listings were 39% lower by March 2023, while England's rose 16%.
  - New lets are 27% of tenancies against 35% nationally, so tenants move less.
  - For renters this means fewer listings and bigger jumps between sitting and market rents.
  - Source: https://www.hamptons.co.uk/articles/what-weve-learnt-from-scotlands-rental-system

**Scotland in one line:** the key price signals are the Home Report valuation, the "offers over" figure and the expected premium (closing date or not). In England the equivalents are the agent's asking price and the agreed discount to it.

## 4. England outside London: how agents value, and where it goes wrong

- **Comparable sales plus days on market.**
  - On MSE, a first-time buyer looked at a share-of-freehold flat cut from £300k to £285k after nearly a year on the market. An identical flat in the same block sold for £280k the previous year.
  - Seven respondents suggested opening at £260–270k. Their reasons: the market has cooled, the seller has moved away, and the buyer is chain-free.
  - Valuation here relies on the latest same-block sale, time on market and price cuts, not on the asking price.
  - Source: https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/6682321/ftb-what-would-you-offer-for-this-flat
- **Over-valuing to win instructions.**
  - In a boom year (2022), a Salisbury agent openly said agents over-value to win instructions, and in that market the homes still sold.
  - Nine in ten homes went to best and final offers with 3–8 bidders, typically about 10% over the guide price.
  - Non-local, mostly London, buyers were less price-sensitive to local values. This pushes commuter-belt prices above what local comparables support.
  - Source: https://thenegotiator.co.uk/features/regional-property-reports/regional-report-70/
  - By 2026 the message has reversed. Zoopla and the agents quoted say correctly priced homes sell while overpriced homes sit, so realistic pricing from day one matters most, especially in the South.
  - Source: https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/uk-housing-market-news/three-in-five-homes-not-selling-reports-zoopla/
- **House type within a price band.** In the Bicester case, buyers compared a three-storey townhouse with semis and detached homes at the same £400k. Layout, parking and garden presentation decided interest, more than the number of bedrooms. Outside London, buyers compare across house types at a given budget. London buyers tend to compare across locations within one type.
- **Negative equity has gone in Northern Ireland.** CPS Property (2024) says only in the past year have they stopped seeing negative-equity appraisals.
  - NI growth is steady at about 5–6% a year, and the average sale price is about £165k.
  - About 14% of their buyers are returning from England, Australia or the US, citing schools, working from home and family support.
  - Garrington, via The Negotiator (March 2026): NI prices +7.5% in 2025 against 2.4% for the UK, average £195.9k in Q4 2025, and 12 years of growth.
  - Sources: https://thenegotiator.co.uk/features/regional-property-reports/northern-ireland/ ; https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/agencies-people-news/northern-ireland-is-uks-hottest-market-claims-leading-property-group/

## 5. Commuter belt specifics

- **Non-local buyers set the price.** Salisbury, Bicester and Surrey threads show London leavers and hybrid workers competing for larger older houses. They put lifestyle (garden, rooms) ahead of running-cost scrutiny. Forum respondents push back with EPC and maintenance warnings.
- **Rent pressure spills over.** ONS lists Sevenoaks, Watford, Trafford, Bath and Brighton among the least affordable areas for rent outside London, so commuter-belt renters face London-like rent-to-income ratios.
- **Commute tolerance is elastic once hybrid work allows two or three office days**, with 15–45 extra minutes accepted (NerdWallet 2021). This widens the search radius and pushes up the weight of home-office space.

## 7. Disagreements and open questions

- **Which index:** Land Registry shows Manchester falling (−4.2%, 2025), while Rightmove and Zoopla show the North West leading growth. Timing and mix effects explain much of the gap.
- **Home Report value:** some agents see it as a transparent anchor. The Law Society representative (2014) and lenders treated it as unreliable after the crash. Buyers routinely pay over it in strong markets (10% in the Glasgow thread; Aberdeen's historic premiums of 20–40%) and under it in weak ones.
- **Solid-wall older houses:** forum users disagree on running costs ("double" vs "less than half" of peers). EPC may not capture real performance.
- **Schools:** academic evidence finds a robust premium of about 3% per SD. Hamptons argues falling rolls are reducing oversubscription, so the premium may weaken, starting in London.
