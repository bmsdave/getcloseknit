# Migrants: South Asian and African-origin households in the London housing market

Stream: how Indian, Pakistani, Bangladeshi, Sri Lankan, Nigerian, Ghanaian and other African-origin households search for homes in London, and which home-country habits they bring. Research date: October 2026.

## Method and coverage

- 22 sources read in full: 9 forum threads (Mumsnet x4, Nairaland x3, MoneySavingExpert, LandlordZONE), 4 academic items (3 peer-reviewed papers and 1 university research report), 2 trade/consultancy pieces, 2 government statistics pages, and 5 web/community-press articles.
- Limitations:
  - Reddit (including r/indians_in_uk, r/IndiansInUK and r/LegalAdviceUK) was not reachable from this environment. No Reddit thread is cited.
  - The web-search quota was used up, so discovery went through a browser. Some threads were read on one page only; those pages are named below.
  - No source read covered Sri Lankan or Ghanaian households specifically. Statements about them are not made.
  - OpenAlex was used to find papers until its rate limit ran out.

## 1. Primary criteria (what decides the search)

### 1.1 Co-ethnic community and family proximity (primary, especially South Asian; strong for Bangladeshi elders)

- **Peer-reviewed evidence.** A mixed-logit residential location model for London commuter households (London Household Survey) finds a positive and statistically significant preference for a higher share of same-ethnic households in the zone, for both owners and renters. Asian and Black households are modelled separately, using "share of Asian households x Asian dummy" and "share of Black households x Black dummy". Willingness to pay for a higher co-ethnic share sits alongside school quality, job access and public transport as positive drivers; crime and commercial land use are negatives (Haque, Choudhury & Hess 2020, Journal of Transport Geography). https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/165851/1/JTRG_Haque.pdf
- **Long-run census panel.** The ONS Longitudinal Study 1971-2011 shows that first-generation migrants have the lowest residential mobility. The authors read this as having "completed" their moves, and as prioritising living with their own community over moving up socio-economically. The 1.5 and second generations move more (Pandya, Kulu & Liu 2026, Demographic Research). https://www.demographic-research.org/volumes/vol54/36/54-36.pdf
- **Bangladeshi elders in East London** (Tower Hamlets, Newham, Hackney, Redbridge; n=76, Open University / Bangla Housing Association, 2026). For older Bangladeshis "neighbourhood equals home": family proximity, mosques, halal shops and community networks matter more than anti-social behaviour. Most want to live with or near their children and established community. https://oro.open.ac.uk/109445/
- **Forum behaviour, North-West London.** A Harrow-based Indian/Pakistani family planning a move to Chesham for grammar schools asked first whether Chesham has an Indian/Pakistani community. Replies pointed to a mosque and a sizeable, well-integrated Asian community. Later posters, also from Harrow, asked the same question in 2022, 2023 and 2024, giving "Amersham too expensive" as their reason (Mumsnet, 2020-2024). This shows an outward chain from Harrow and Edgware into the Chilterns, where people search for the community first and the school second. https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/education/4091018-Are-there-any-Indian-Pakistani-community-in-Chesham
- **Fear of racism as a location filter.** A London couple of Indian parentage, priced out of a 2-bed flat, weighed moving to a cheaper northern rural area. Their pros were family links and a big house; their cons were "potential racism", weak schools and loss of London diversity. The OP said minority buyers must add "will we be targeted?" to the usual checklist of space, schools and transport. Replies split: some reported a warm welcome in rural areas, others reported micro-aggressions or having to anglicise names. Several advised keeping the London flat as a foothold, or choosing suburban London (Bromley, Chislehurst) (Mumsnet 2018, page 1 of 18 read). https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/am_i_being_unreasonable/3425947-can-we-as-an-asian-family-move-to-a-rural-area
- **Affordability against family closeness (community press).** A British-Pakistani writer describes how London prices (about 9.7x earnings) are pulling second-generation South Asians away from family clusters, for example Barnet, Hackney and East London. Families weigh a bigger house in Basingstoke or Leicester against weekly drives back for weddings, Eid and birthdays. Racism and xenophobia are cited as reasons people won't move "too far from London" (MyLondon 2022). https://www.mylondon.news/news/uk-world-news/for-south-asian-community-housing-24412829
- **Nigerian/African pattern.** On Nairaland, a new arrival dreaming of Greenwich was told he "wants to be among his own people". Peckham is the historic Nigerian hub, but after council-estate regeneration "old Peckham residents" moved to Woolwich, Plumstead, Abbey Wood, Thamesmead and parts of Essex (Nairaland "Relocating to UK", April 2021, page 3 read). The search map for African-origin households therefore runs through the South-East London / Thames Gateway band rather than Peckham itself. https://www.nairaland.com/6487635/relocating-uk/2

### 1.2 Schools, and grammar / 11+ in particular (primary for Indian, and also for Pakistani, Sri Lankan Tamil and Chinese-origin, families with children)

- **Grammar schools drive moves out of London boroughs.** Harrow parents say "all good schools are private" in Harrow and move to Buckinghamshire (Chesham) for grammar schools. Locals reassure them that, in Chesham, passing the 11+ almost guarantees a place at Chesham Grammar, and that the secondary-modern fallback (Chiltern Hills Academy) is acceptable. https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/education/4091018-Are-there-any-Indian-Pakistani-community-in-Chesham
- **Ethnic composition of selective schools.** A Redbridge parent weighed Henrietta Barnett (Barnet, 15 miles away, 1-hour coach), Woodford County High and Chelmsford County High. A former Woodford parent described the intake as roughly 90% Asian, mostly commuting from Redbridge. Another poster said some Asian friends avoid the school because they want more of a mix (Mumsnet 2023). This means the "grammar factor" drives demand in Redbridge/Ilford and out into Essex, not just in the borough where the school sits. https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/secondary/4904191-hbs-woodford-or-chelmsford-grammar-school-dilemma
- **2026 debate.** A thread on a Telegraph piece about why few white British children get grammar places relays these claims:
  - one north-London grammar with about 3,000 applicants for 104 places admitted one white British child in 2024-25;
  - a 2016 Sutton Trust report found disadvantaged Indian pupils four times as likely as disadvantaged white British pupils to attend grammar school;
  - average Beaconsfield prices (Bucks grammar catchment) exceed GBP 1.1m.

  Posters, including a self-described South Asian "tiger mum", attribute the pattern to tutoring and parental investment. Others note that grammars cluster in diverse areas such as London, Slough and Manchester (Mumsnet, Feb 2026, page 1 of 13 read; the Telegraph article itself was not read directly). https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/secondary/5490180-why-white-children-are-not-getting-into-grammar-schools
- **Renters do this too.** An Indian renter on about GBP 1,700/month was advised to try Gants Hill, Barkingside, Ilford, West Drayton, Uxbridge, Feltham and Bexley, and to check Ofsted. Advisers stressed transport links and "things Indians prefer", meaning groceries and restaurants. This came from a Reddit thread seen through the browser; its URL could not be captured, so it is not cited as a source. The same pattern appears in the Mumsnet sources above.

### 1.3 Tenure, house type and size (primary for buyers; strongly shaped by household size)

- **Home ownership by ethnicity, England, 2021-23.** The overall rate is 65%. By group: White British 70%, Indian 68%, Pakistani 55%, Black Caribbean 31%, Bangladeshi 37%, Black African 22%, Mixed White/Black African 14% (English Housing Survey). Indian households own almost as often as White British households; Black African households rent far more. https://www.ethnicity-facts-figures.service.gov.uk/housing/owning-and-renting/home-ownership/latest/
- **Generational trend.** Descendants of Indian and Pakistani migrants are even more likely than the native population to move into home ownership. Caribbean and African groups have higher mobility and more renting (Pandya et al. 2026). https://www.demographic-research.org/volumes/vol54/36/54-36.pdf
- **Overcrowding by ethnicity, England, 2020-23.** Bangladeshi 18%, Black African 16%, Pakistani 14%, Indian 6%, White British 2%. In London, 7% of all households are overcrowded: 3% of White British households and 11% of all other groups combined. https://www.ethnicity-facts-figures.service.gov.uk/housing/housing-conditions/overcrowded-households/latest/
- **Multigenerational living.** Among Bangladeshi elders in East London, 83% live with family, commonly in inter- or multigenerational arrangements. The report introduces "functional overcrowding": homes built for nuclear families lack privacy, space for prayer and water-based hygiene, and room for extended family, even when they are not statutorily overcrowded. 46% needed adaptations, and 70% of those needed them immediately. Tenure in the sample: 50% social housing, 34% owner-occupiers, 11% specialist housing, 5% private rent. https://oro.open.ac.uk/109445/
- **Agent view of multigenerational buyers.** Savills (2025) reports continuing demand from buyers planning to live with relatives. They want ancillary accommodation: an annexe, a converted loft, a self-contained floor, or a second reception room. Savills does not break this down by ethnicity, but it matches what the community sources describe. https://www.savills.co.uk/blog/article/373829/residential-property/a-pick-of-the-best-homes-for-multi-generational-living.aspx
- **Leasehold distrust and the freehold/house preference.** On Nairaland's long UK property thread, buyers weigh a flat (about GBP 250k for a new-build 2/3-bed) against a semi-detached house (from about GBP 400k):
  - One poster explains how a first flat became a buy-to-let and was remortgaged to fund a second property.
  - Others warn that a friend's leasehold flat is "bleeding" from rising ground rent and service charges.
  - A Scottish poster points out that all property there is freehold.

  Freehold houses are the aspiration and leasehold flats a tolerated stepping stone (Nairaland, Aug 2023, page 12 of 80 read). https://www.nairaland.com/7534564/living-uk-propertymortgage-related/11

### 1.4 Finance: deposits, remittances, visa-holder lending, faith-compliant finance (primary gatekeeper)

- **Visa holders.** Nairaland posters on Skilled Worker visas look for lenders who accept 5% deposits from visa holders earning under GBP 100k. One used two employer-provided brokers and short-listed Barclays, TSB and Halifax at 10% deposit. Posters also corrected a member who planned to use first-time-buyer stamp-duty relief twice by buying in each spouse's name, explaining that married couples are treated as one for the relief. https://www.nairaland.com/7534564/living-uk-propertymortgage-related/11
- **Gifted deposits from abroad (India).** A buyer's deposit was partly gifted by his father in India. One solicitor refused the case because the money had not been in a UK account for six months. Forum regulars called that unusually risk-averse: other firms accept overseas gifts with source-of-funds evidence, sometimes with translation or third-party verification. Solicitors' appetite for such cases varies widely (MSE 2020). Remittance-funded deposits therefore add friction and time, and the choice of conveyancer matters. https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/6226525/gifted-deposit-from-abroad
- **Diaspora money flowing into London.** In 2015 India raised the Liberalised Remittance Scheme limit to USD 250k per person per year, so a family of four can send about USD 1m a year. In 2017 Black Brick said 30% of its new buyer enquiries came from resident Indians, typically looking around GBP 1m for a pied-a-terre or buy-to-let; cash buyers secure discounts (Property Industry Eye 2017). https://propertyindustryeye.com/london-property-gains-benefit-from-indias-overseas-investment-rules/
- **Indian nationals' share of buying (2024).** Indian nationals made up 40% of Asian buyers' London purchases in Q1 2024, and Asian buyers were 11% of all London purchases, up from 6% in 2016. International buyers were 27% overall. The prime focus is on areas like Mayfair. Transport, safety and education are named priorities for Asian buyers generally (Eastern Eye 2024, relaying The Times). https://www.easterneye.biz/london-property-market-indian-buyers/
- **Nigerian home-country cash buyers.** A Lagos-based saver asked whether NGN 50m (then about GBP 95k) buys a UK house. The thread is dominated by scam warnings and fixers offering "trusted" Lagos offices. The sensible advice was:
  - buy outside London, for example in the West Midlands or Manchester;
  - check that the agent is registered and the conveyancer is with the Law Society;
  - commission a RICS survey;
  - never send money to intermediaries.

  For home-country buyers, London is often unaffordable, and fraud risk is a first-order concern (Nairaland 2022). https://www.nairaland.com/7149040/buying-house-uk
- **Islamic (riba-free) home finance.** UK Islamic mortgage volumes are small, about 2,500-3,000 residential and buy-to-let deals a year, even though Al Rayan has offered them since 2004 and Lloyds and HSBC have withdrawn. A survey of 144 mortgage consultants finds that perceived *authenticity* (genuine Sharia compliance) is the strongest driver of the product's prospects, followed by accessibility; affordability is not significant (Ahmed et al. 2023, Sustainability). For some Pakistani and Bangladeshi households, the finance route is itself a search filter. https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/15/5/4188

## 2. Secondary and niche criteria (home-country habits)

- **Vastu (Indian buyers).** Black Brick (buying agent) told the Telegraph in 2019 that about half of Indian buyers in London, especially non-resident Indians, look for vastu-compliant homes. For those buyers it is a deal-breaker, and it is hard to satisfy in existing stock. Relevant features: plot shape, orientation of the main door (south and south-west entrances avoided), light, water, and room layout. This is a niche but binary filter that portals do not capture. https://black-brick.com/insights/in-the-press/indian-buyers-pile-in-to-londons-property-market-on-the-hunt-for-vastu-compliant-homes/
- **Cooking and ventilation.** A Central London landlord on LandlordZONE (2024) wanted a tenancy clause banning "pungent" foods such as curry after a high-earning Indian tenant's daily cooking left odours. Most respondents called it unenforceable and probably indirect racial discrimination. Some proposed a better extractor fan or deep cleaning instead. Others openly advised "being more selective with tenants", which is the discriminatory screening that Equality Act case law (the 2017 Fergus Wilson ruling, linked in the thread) prohibits. For buyers and renters, a separate or well-ventilated kitchen and an extractor are practical secondary criteria. https://forums.landlordzone.co.uk/c/residential-letting-questions/restriction-on-certain-type-of-foods-in-the-rental-agreement-ast
- **Faith and water-based hygiene in the home.** Bangladeshi elders need prayer space, wudu-friendly bathrooms (water-based hygiene) and privacy for gendered living, and generic adaptations often fail them (OU 2026). https://oro.open.ac.uk/109445/
- **Ethnic food shops and restaurants.** These are named alongside transport in Ilford and Gants Hill, and the Chesham thread mentions a mosque. Shops and worship places act as a quick test of whether a neighbourhood has a community (Mumsnet 2020; MyLondon 2022). https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/education/4091018-Are-there-any-Indian-Pakistani-community-in-Chesham
- **Buy-to-let as wealth-building.** Among Nigerian-diaspora professionals the flat-to-buy-to-let-to-remortgage ladder is presented as a model. Among Indian investors buy-to-let is a diversification play. https://www.nairaland.com/7534564/living-uk-propertymortgage-related/11 ; https://propertyindustryeye.com/london-property-gains-benefit-from-indias-overseas-investment-rules/

## 3. Discrimination in renting (a constraint that reshapes the search)

- **Right to Rent.** A 2024 survey of 2,000 landlords (cited by Shelter, 2025) found:
  - 24% believe they cannot rent to people without a UK passport;
  - 56% of those cite fear of a civil penalty;
  - 47% find the checks too difficult.

  The earlier JCWI research found that 42% of landlords were less likely to consider someone without a British passport, and 27% were reluctant to engage with people with "foreign accents or names". https://england.shelter.org.uk/what_we_do/updates_insights_and_impact/right_to_rent_entrenching_discrimination
- **Name and photo tests.**
  - White-sounding names got 25% more responses than traditionally South Asian names in a recent mystery-shopping exercise (cited by Shelter).
  - In Generation Rent's 2023 AI-profile test across more than 200 listings, white profiles were 17% more likely to get any reply and 36% more likely to get a positive reply than Black profiles.

  https://www.generationrent.org/2025/03/31/long-read-a-cycle-of-inequality-minority-ethnic-renters-experiences-of-discrimination/
- **Generation Rent 2024 survey (n=1,058).**
  - Minority-ethnic renters were refused a tenancy at 12.5%, against 6.3% for white British/Irish renters.
  - They failed reference checks at 10.2%, against 5.6%.
  - They were 27.4% more likely to be asked for several months' rent upfront.
  - 60% of Asian respondents reported struggling with rent, against 44% of white respondents.
  - Rent rises averaged GBP 131/month, against GBP 87.

  Census 2021 analysis: 57% of Black households with adult children living at home rent, against 27% of white households. https://www.generationrent.org/2025/03/31/long-read-a-cycle-of-inequality-minority-ethnic-renters-experiences-of-discrimination/
- **Practical consequences for the search:**
  - minority and migrant renters apply to more listings;
  - they prefer platforms with direct landlords (OpenRent was recommended on Reddit and on LandlordZONE);
  - they rely on co-ethnic landlords and agents;
  - they tolerate higher upfront payments.

  London-specific weight: the evidence is national, but London has the largest minority private-rented population.

## 4. Segment nuances

| Segment | Primary drivers | Secondary or niche | Typical London/outer geography (from sources) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Established Indian (Gujarati, Punjabi, East African Asian), 2nd gen | Grammar/11+, community and temple, freehold semi or detached, size for extended family | Vastu, annexe, parking | Harrow/Edgware moving to Chesham/Amersham/Bucks; Redbridge (Gants Hill, Barkingside) moving to Essex |
| New Indian professionals (Skilled Worker visa), renting | Commute, budget, schools (Ofsted), Indian groceries | OpenRent, Right to Rent friction | Ilford, Gants Hill, Uxbridge, West Drayton, Feltham, Bexley (Reddit advice, uncited) |
| NRI / India-resident investors | Capital preservation, buy-to-let yield, prime address | Vastu (about 50% per Black Brick) | Prime central London around GBP 1m+; Mayfair |
| Pakistani / Bangladeshi | Family proximity, mosque, halal shops, house size (overcrowding 14-18%) | Islamic finance, prayer and wudu space | East London (Tower Hamlets, Newham to Redbridge), Barnet, Hackney |
| Nigerian / West African | Affordability, own-community area, transport | Leasehold distrust, buy-to-let ladder | Peckham historically; now Woolwich, Plumstead, Abbey Wood, Thamesmead, Essex; outside London if priced out |

## 5. Disagreements and caveats

- **Segregation or welcome.** Mumsnet respondents disagree on whether rural or white areas are welcoming. Some report acceptance; others report micro-aggressions or having to anglicise names. Some white posters argue that concentration itself causes "us versus them" tensions, while minority posters treat co-ethnic areas as protection.
- **Woodford County High's composition.** One poster's 90% Asian figure is anecdotal and five years old. A second poster says the mix "is still the same".
- **Grammar demographics.** The Telegraph and Mumsnet attribute the skew to parental aspiration. The quoted Lee Elliot Major warns against like-for-like ethnic comparisons, because income and geography confound them.
- **Wealth gaps.** Runnymede (2020, via Generation Rent) found Indian households hold 90-95p of wealth for every GBP 1 held by White British households, Pakistani about 50p, and Black African and Bangladeshi about 10p. This underlies very different starting points: Indian households compete in grammar catchments, while Black African and Bangladeshi households are concentrated in renting and overcrowded homes.
- **The vastu figure is a single agent's estimate**, from 2019 and from the prime and NRI segment. It should not be generalised to all British Indians.
- **Gifted deposits from India.** Forum anecdote says requirements vary by firm. No regulator source was read.
- **Not covered by sources read:** Sri Lankan Tamil and Ghanaian-specific behaviour; the Southall and Wembley temple and gurdwara premium (no agent or academic source found); quantified price premia near places of worship.

## 6. London-specific versus other UK cities

- **London-specific:**
  - selective schools and super-selective grammar competition span borough boundaries: Barnet, Redbridge and Sutton, plus Bucks and Kent catchments reached from London;
  - prime and NRI investment and the vastu market;
  - the highest absolute numbers of minority private renters, so Right to Rent and name discrimination bite hardest;
  - regeneration displacing established African hubs (Peckham to the Thames Gateway).
- **Other cities:** The Nairaland advice steers home-country buyers to Birmingham and Manchester for affordability. The Mumsnet rural thread names northern mill towns with past race riots (posters guessed Oldham or Bradford) as places Asian Londoners fear returning to. Peer-reviewed tenure gaps (Pandya et al.) are national.
