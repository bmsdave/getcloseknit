# Verification: Surveyors, mortgage valuation and AVMs

- **1. UK down-valuation rates (25.2% e.surv Q1 2022; 19.2% at 1.54% SDL 2020-22; ~20% JLM 2025; linked to Jan 2025 Red Book)** — partially. All figures match the sources. But e.surv calls 25.2% "unremarkable" against long-term trends, so the rates are not plainly "high". The Red Book link is one broker's guess (JLM's Sebastian Murphy, who hedges it himself), not something brokers in general say.
- **2. AVMs ~25% of UK residential valuations; Hometrack 16/20 (press) vs 18/20 (own site); 80% within 10%** — supported. The 25% comes from LSL's own statement in The Negotiator. The other figures match Mortgage Strategy and Hometrack's site.
- **3. AVMs limited to low-risk cases (65% LTV, £750k cap, houses and single flats; BTL remortgage up to 75% LTV); prime London and high-LTV purchases still physically valued** — partially. The criteria match Castle Trust (BTL) and Landbay (BTL remortgage). Both are BTL lenders. Neither source says anything about prime London or high-LTV purchases, so that conclusion is the researcher's own inference.
- **4. US: ~30% of appraisals exactly at contract price, <10% below; AVMs predict default better** — supported. Philadelphia Fed WP 18-28 says this ("approximately 30%… precisely", "less than 10%… below"; 6% below in its main sample). It says appraisals are "somewhat less predictive of default than AVM estimates". The contrast with the UK is the researcher's own framing.
- **5. London: street-based local areas >30% of variance; images lift R² 72.5% → 84.7%; EPC premium small/insignificant in London vs +5% A/B, -7% G nationally** — partially. The UCL "constantly above 30%" figure and the Cambridge EPC figures (A/B +5%, G "nearly 7%" less; London shows lower or insignificant effects) are supported. The R² figure misleads. 72.50% is a linear model on attributes only, and 84.67% is the full nonlinear model. Adding images to the same model gives 72.5% → 76.9% (linear) or 81.7% → 84.1% (XGBoost).
