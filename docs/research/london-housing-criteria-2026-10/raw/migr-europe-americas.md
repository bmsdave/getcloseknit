# Migrants from Europe, the Americas, Ukraine and Russia: how they search for London housing

Stream: migr-europe-americas. Research date: October 2026.

## Method and limits (read first)

- **The WebSearch budget for this session was already spent** when this stream started. So discovery came from four routes instead: browsing forum index pages (expat.com in English, French and Italian), StackExchange API listings by tag, the OpenAlex and Crossref academic indexes, and The Negotiator's on-site search.
- **Reddit (www and old.reddit) and britishexpats.com were blocked** (fetch errors or 403). Mumsnet's living-overseas board had no threads about moving into London. The Polish forums (londynek.net, mojawyspa) only redirected to their home pages. So there is **no Reddit, Polish-forum or Russian-forum evidence here**. Polish, Romanian, Russian and Brazilian patterns rest on academic and encyclopedic sources.
- **Forum evidence is mostly thin.** The expat.com threads are short (2 to 18 replies). The StackExchange threads are older (2014-2018) but have higher-quality answers. Ages are given per thread.
- **Some topics in the brief had no source this stream could read:** US MLS/Zestimate expectations, price per m², Eurostar as a criterion, and rent caps. These are marked "not evidenced here" instead of being filled from memory.

## 1. Who the movers are and where they cluster (context for "community area" criteria)

| Group | London clustering (source) | Anchor institution |
|---|---|---|
| French | Census 2011: 66,654 French-born people (48.4% of the UK total) lived in Greater London. They concentrate in Kensington & Chelsea, Westminster, and Hammersmith & Fulham. The consulate claimed about 270,000 in the London region; ONS disputed this. [W-French] | Lycée Français Charles de Gaulle, 35 Cromwell Road, South Kensington: about 3,500 pupils aged 3-19. Its primary annexes are in Fulham (2008), Ealing (1995) and Clapham (1993). The French state subsidises fees for French nationals only. [W-Lycee] |
| US | Chelsea 6.53% and Kensington 5.81% US-born (2001). 231,853 US-born people live UK-wide (2021/22). [W-US] | American School in London, St John's Wood: about 1,350 pupils, about 80% with US passports, fees around £46k a year for grades 9-12. Wikipedia credits it with St John's Wood's appeal to American families. [W-ASL] |
| Polish | Census 2021: 718,251 Polish-born in England. London clusters are Ealing, Hammersmith, Haringey, Hounslow, Brent and Barnet. Historically the "Polish Corridor" ran through Earl's Court and later Balham and Lewisham, displaced by rising costs. [W-Poles] | POSK (Hammersmith), St Andrew Bobola church |
| Romanian | 175,991 Romanian-born people in Greater London (2021); Harrow alone has 21,082. Burnt Oak/Edgware is called "Little Romania". The Romanian-born population grew 576% between 2011 and 2021. [W-Romanians] | — |
| Brazilian | Brent has about 30,000. Bayswater (nicknamed "Brazilwater") and Stockwell ("Little Portugal") are other centres. Most work in cleaning, hospitality and couriering despite above-average education. [W-Brazil] | — |
| Russian-speaking | Kensington & Chelsea, Belgravia, Hampstead, Canary Wharf [W-Russians]. Third-wave (2000s) IT and finance professionals chose Chelsea, Hampstead and St John's Wood, later Notting Hill and Canada Water. They settled near work (the City, Canary Wharf) or in areas that already had Russian community infrastructure [Zemnukhova 2017]. | Russian Embassy School and Russian-language Saturday schools around Notting Hill Gate [W-Russians] |

**Takeaway:** for every group, **the community anchor is usually a school or church, not an ethnic "quarter"**. For the French it is the Lycée and its annexes, so the French cluster also covers Fulham, Ealing and Clapham. For Americans it is ASL in St John's Wood. For Poles it is POSK and the parish network. The richer the group, the more its clustering follows fee-paying schools and prime postcodes. The lower-wage EU groups (Romanian, Brazilian, Polish) cluster in outer and inner-suburban boroughs where rooms and flat-shares are cheap.

## 2. Primary criteria these movers bring or add

### 2.1 Budget versus London rent shock (PRIMARY, renters)
- **A French single mother paid £500 a month all-inclusive** for a furnished ensuite studio in Ilford and reported pests. Other French parents looked for a shared house ("colocation") to pool childcare. Budget and house-sharing came first; schools were barely discussed. [expat.com FR 837751, about 2019]
- **An Italian newcomer from Calabria asked for the cheapest single-room areas.** The answer listed Eltham at about £520 a month, other south-east areas at £535-560, and Plumstead, Woolwich, Norwood and Forest Gate as options. A second reply warned that Oyster/commute costs cancel out cheap rents further out. [expat.com IT 668281, about 2017]
- **An Indian tech worker relocating from Seattle with a £2,000 budget** wanted a furnished one-bed in Zone 2, under 30 minutes from EC1, ideally on the Elizabeth line. Replies:
  - A flat listed at £1,850 easily passes £2,100 once council tax and energy are added.
  - The tube adds £120+ a month.
  - A poor EPC rating raises heating bills.
  - Stick to Rightmove and Zoopla to avoid scams.
  - One reply pushed crime data (phone snatching), which another member disputed.

  [expat.com EN 1089469, 2025-2026]
- **A French/Swiss engineer estimated £70k a year** for a family of four. Replies put core monthly costs at about £3,400-4,000. [expat.com EN 1025556, about 2023]
- **A French family's €4,000-6,000 a month budget** was itself flagged as tight for a family of four in London. [expat.com FR 1060100, about 2024]
- **A French mother of three asked whether £4,000-5,000 a month** could rent a furnished four-bed in South Kensington or Paddington. Nobody answered directly, which itself says something. [expat.com FR 911698, about 2021]
- **Rents are rarely negotiable in London.** Expect to pay the asking rent; demand is high and the main lever is the landlord. Small private landlords negotiate more than professional ones. Days on market and price reductions on Rightmove are signals. Being able to move in fast or having a vacant unit are levers. [money.SE 89772, 2018]

**Nuance:** what newcomers underestimate is the **total monthly cost (rent + council tax + energy/EPC + transport)**, not the rent itself. A cost-to-commute view is a gap that newcomers explicitly ask for.

### 2.2 Paperwork barriers: credit history, references, guarantors, bank account, Right to Rent (PRIMARY, renters; very London-heavy)
- **Bank account catch-22:** you need proof of address for a bank account, and a bank account for proof of address. Workarounds that answerers reported:
  - "New to UK" accounts opened with a passport (Lloyds).
  - Accounts opened from the home country (HSBC via Travelex).
  - A work contract accepted as proof of address.

  Central London branches accept more than suburban ones. A US bank statement was rejected because it had no UK sort code. [expatriates.SE 85, 2014+]
- **No UK credit file:** a referencing agency could not score a tenant who had been in the UK under 3 weeks. Accepted answer: offer an HR letter, a 12-month instead of 6-month lease, and rent plus deposit paid upfront. A Canadian got through with an international credit check and a reference from their previous foreign landlord. [expatriates.SE 6189, 2015]
- **Credit scores do not transfer between countries.** A Dutch mover was surprised that UK credit scoring matters at all, since the Netherlands has no equivalent. US scores are tied to the SSN and do not carry over. [money.SE 37005, 2014]
- **Right to Rent:** landlords must check immigration status. Penalties run from £80 (lodgers, first breach) to £3,000 (repeat breaches). [expatriates.SE 4356, 2014]
- **Ukrainians:** ONS (April 2024) found 45% renting privately and 56% paying for their own housing. **50% hit obstacles renting privately: 49% cited no guarantor or references and 42% could not afford rent on their income.** [ONS Ukraine 2024]
- **Older process norms:** one month's rent plus six weeks' deposit plus referencing fees. Tenant fees were later banned by the Tenant Fees Act 2019, so treat that part as historical. The advice was to visit 2-3 weeks before move-in, because landlords will not hold a unit for long. [expat.com EN 15637, 2009+]
- **Post-Brexit:** EU movers now need a visa or job offer. French forum members repeatedly corrected newcomers who assumed free movement. [expat.com FR 1014803; FR 911698]

**Implication:** for newly arrived Europeans, Americans and Ukrainians, **"can I pass referencing?" is as primary as price**. Listings or tools that flag "accepts rent in advance / no UK credit history / guarantor service accepted / company let" would answer a real need.

### 2.3 Temporary first base, then search in person (PRIMARY, process)
- **Recommended sequence:** Airbnb or a short let for 1-2 weeks or 1-2 months, then search in person. SpareRoom and Rightmove's room section were suggested, and coliving was named as an option. One answerer, a French mover to Edinburgh, said it took a week from viewing to moving in. [expatriates.SE 6829, 2015]
- **Do not commit online before seeing a flat** unless it is through a reputable agent. [expat.com EN 15637]
- **Scam risk is mentioned in almost every newcomer thread.** Red flags are upfront payments and private listings off the major portals. [expat.com EN 1089469]

### 2.4 Schools as a location driver (PRIMARY for families)
- **French families:**
  - The Lycée system (South Kensington plus Fulham, Ealing and Clapham annexes) anchors the French cluster [W-Lycee].
  - A Béarn family thought bilingual independent schools at about €15k per child too expensive. They weighed English state school plus Saturday French classes. Their plan was to choose the school first, then the location, but they realised they needed an address before enrolling. One reply steered them to Bristol for lifestyle and a French community. [expat.com FR 1060100]
  - Longitudinal interviews with French professionals in London (2011-2018) show children in "very British schools" and bi-national families are **the strongest reason to stay put**. A Brexit-anxious mother said she felt "trapped" until her children finish school. [Mulholland & Ryan 2022]
- **Italian family:** the state-school process needs the child physically in England. You apply through the local council, state preferences (for example Catholic school, distance from home), and go on a waiting list. One poster got a place within a month of arriving in late August. [expat.com IT 748642, 2018]
- **US families:** the ASL in St John's Wood is a magnet [W-ASL]. North American buyers are increasingly owner-occupiers, not investors: a record 23% of international applicants are first-time buyers and only 12% are investors [Negotiator/Hamptons 2026].

**Pattern:** continental families face a **school-first vs address-first** puzzle. State-school places depend on home address (catchment and distance), but movers cannot fix an address before they arrive. Fee-paying national schools (Lycée, ASL) cut that link and so pull families into specific prime postcodes.

### 2.5 Community and "familiar" areas (SECONDARY for professionals, PRIMARY for lower-wage EU and Brazilian movers)
- Russian IT professionals used language-based networks for "renting or moving, sports or education". They gathered in pubs and at "Ziferblat". The newest "Be-Mobile" wave treats London as one stop among many and does not build neighbourhood ties. [Zemnukhova 2017]
- Polish migrants (41 interviews, 2015-16) judged neighbourhoods as safe or unsafe partly by racial stereotypes, and adjusted where they lived after being racialised by other residents. Perceived safety and ethnic mix are real but sensitive criteria. *(Abstract only; full text was blocked. Read status: false.)* [Shankley 2023]
- An Indian couple were pointed to Wembley and Harrow for community. The same logic applies to Romanians in Harrow and Burnt Oak, Brazilians in Brent, and Poles in Ealing and Hammersmith. [expat.com EN 1089469; W-Romanians; W-Brazil; W-Poles]

### 2.6 Transport and international access
- **Commute time to a named office** and a specific line (the Elizabeth line) were explicit hard filters [expat.com EN 1089469].
- **Air links home were a criterion:** a French family required a location within 2 hours of an airport with flights to CDG [expat.com FR 1060100].
- **Eurostar as a criterion was not evidenced here.** The JEMS paper quotes a migrant who simply "took the Eurostar" to London, but nobody named St Pancras access as a search criterion.

## 3. What buyers from abroad expect versus UK realities

### 3.1 Binding offers and gazumping (UK-specific pitfall)
- **In England and Wales an accepted offer is not legally binding until contracts are exchanged,** usually 10-12 weeks later. This is what makes gazumping possible. In Scotland, solicitors' written acceptance binds both sides, so gazumping is rare. In the US, a signed written purchase agreement typically binds. Gazumping was common in the late-1980s boom, notably in London. [W-Gazumping]
- **Not evidenced here:** forum confirmation of how US, French or German buyers react. Treat "buyers from notary or contract-at-offer systems are shocked by gazumping" as a plausible hypothesis, not a finding.

### 3.2 Financing and status
- **An American asked whether buying a home would give the right to live in England.** Replies: no visa follows from buying property, a mortgage is "very unlikely" without status, and Golden Visas (for example Malta) are an EU option, not a UK one. [expat.com EN 1037746, about 2023]
- **Stamp duty:** a prime London agent advises Americans staying under 5 years to rent rather than buy because of stamp duty [Negotiator/Rightmove 2025].
- **Tax:** UK non-dom reforms matter less to Americans because they pay worldwide US tax anyway [Negotiator/Glentree 2024].

### 3.3 Who is buying and where (professional-journal data)
- **Hamptons (Q1 2026):**
  - North Americans are 19% of international buyer registrations, up from 15% a year earlier and double the share a decade ago.
  - Europeans are still the biggest group at 54%.
  - International demand in London rose 8% year on year while falling 10% nationally.
  - Only 5% of North American applicants target prime central London, down from 13% in 2013. Americans are spreading beyond PCL.

  [Negotiator/Hamptons 2026]
- **Rightmove (May 2025):** US enquiries were up nearly 20% year on year, an 8-year high. Edinburgh was the top US target, then Westminster, Camden and Glasgow. 47% searched for 1-2 beds and 32% for 3-4 beds. [Negotiator/Rightmove 2025]
- **Knight Frank (2019):** Americans were 6.0% of super-prime buyers (second after Chinese at 9.4%), with an average purchase of £7.3m. Their favoured areas were Marylebone, Mayfair, Chelsea, Islington and Belgravia. [Negotiator/KF 2019]
- **Beauchamp (H1 2026):** US and Gulf buyers made 55% of PCL sales above £15m. There were 34 deals worth £1.24bn, with an average of £36.5m. [Negotiator/Beauchamp 2026]
- **Glentree (2024):** wealthy Californians want Hampstead and Highgate for green space (agent claim of "45% green"). Their image of London is shaped partly by films. [Negotiator/Glentree 2024]
- **London Square survey (July 2026):** of 1,000 people in New York, Singapore, Dubai, Hong Kong and Paris, 64% would live in London. Respondents valued culture (83%), education (79%), stability (70%) and safety (57%). The survey claims London two-beds are 23% cheaper than New York. This is developer-commissioned marketing research. [Negotiator/London Square 2026]

### 3.4 Price per m², floor area and automated valuations: not evidenced here
- **No source read in this stream** documents continental expectations about €/m² and m² in listings, or US expectations about MLS/Zestimate.
- **Hypothesis for other streams to confirm:**
  - French, Polish, German and Italian buyers think in price per m², while UK listings lead with bedroom count and show floor area mainly on floorplans or the EPC.
  - Americans expect one MLS with full sold history, while the UK uses Rightmove, Zoopla and OnTheMarket plus Land Registry sold prices.
- The Rightmove data above at least confirms that **bedrooms are how US searches are recorded and reported** (1-2 vs 3-4 beds).

### 3.5 Rental-stock expectations
- **Furnished vs unfurnished:** newcomers often ask specifically for furnished flats [expat.com FR 911698; EN 1089469]. One French thread on furnishing a flat existed but was not read.
- **Pets:** a Paris mover moving to north Kent could view only 3 of about 40 suitable properties because almost none accepted cats. He also found the stock dated (old carpets, single glazing). That thread had no answers, so it is excluded from the counts.
- **Lifestyle features:** an American looking for a working wood-burning fireplace learned these are rare in rentals. Landlords fear fire risk, and smoke and air-quality rules limit them. [expat.com EN 839586, 2019]

## 4. Structural context: tenure and price effects

- **Tenure (Migration Observatory, Dec 2024):**
  - Owner-occupation is 43% for the foreign-born vs 67% for the UK-born.
  - **People born in the post-2004 EU accession countries (Poland, Romania etc.) are 61% private renters, the highest share of any group.**
  - Ownership is 17% for those who arrived within 5 years vs 68% for those here 20+ years.
  - Overcrowding among foreign-born households in London is 14%.
  - A Migration Advisory Committee estimate: a 1 percentage-point rise in population from migration raised house prices about 1%.

  [MigObs 2024]
- **Local price effects (Sá, IZA DP 5893, 2011; Economic Journal 2015):** across 159 local authorities, an immigrant inflow equal to 1% of the local population lowers local house prices by about 1.6%. Mechanism: higher-wage natives move out. The effect is driven by areas where low-education migrants settle. Where migrants are highly educated, the income effect offsets it. [Sá 2011]
- **Implication for the app:** areas with high-skill migrant clusters (South Kensington French, St John's Wood American) and areas with low-wage migrant clusters (Harrow and Burnt Oak Romanian, Brent Brazilian) sit at opposite ends of this mechanism.

## 5. Disagreements and caveats

- **Crime as a criterion:** one expat.com veteran urged newcomers to put crime data first, while another called this alarmist [EN 1089469]. Newcomers do look at safety, but how much weight they give it varies by person.
- **Can you negotiate rent?** The top answer says rarely. A second answer (2018) argued London favoured tenants because yields were low. The disagreement depends on market conditions. [money.SE 89772]
- **London vs regions:** forum members and Rightmove data both show some movers redirected away from London (to Bristol for a French family, Edinburgh for US searchers) on cost and lifestyle grounds [FR 1060100; Negotiator/Rightmove 2025].
- **Survey vs registration data:** the London Square survey is developer-commissioned. Hamptons and Rightmove reflect registrations and searches, not completed purchases.

## 6. Summary: primary vs secondary criteria for migrants from these regions

| Criterion | Tier | Side | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total monthly cost (rent + council tax + energy/EPC + commute) | Primary | rent | Newcomers underestimate the extras |
| Ability to pass referencing (credit history, guarantor, Right to Rent, bank account) | Primary | rent | London-heavy; 49% of Ukrainians cite no guarantor or references |
| Commute time and line to a named workplace | Primary | rent/buy | Elizabeth line, City, Canary Wharf |
| School access (national school: Lycée/ASL; or state school by address) | Primary (families) | all | Drives French and US clustering |
| Furnished, short-let or temporary first base | Primary (first 1-3 months) | rent | Airbnb, then an in-person search |
| Community and language networks, community areas | Secondary for professionals, primary for lower-wage EU and Brazilian movers | rent | Harrow and Burnt Oak, Ealing, Brent, Hampstead |
| Scam avoidance (major portals, no upfront payment) | Primary process guard | rent | Raised in nearly every thread |
| Green and leafy, space (Hampstead, Highgate) | Secondary (prime US buyers) | buy | Agent claims |
| Air or rail link home (CDG flights; Eurostar not evidenced) | Secondary | all | — |
| Stamp duty and holding period (rent if staying under 5 years) | Primary for buy-vs-rent | buy | — |
| Pets, fireplaces, other lifestyle features | Niche | rent | Rarely available |
