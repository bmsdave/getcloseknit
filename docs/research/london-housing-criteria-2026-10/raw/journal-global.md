# Global professional journals: buyer criteria, CMA, appraisal and AVMs

Stream: Global professional journals (October 2026). Focus: international trade/professional publications (NAR research, HousingWire, RISMedia, Appraisal Institute, IVSC/TEGoVA, Knight Frank research notes) plus a small set of academic papers on valuation and seller pricing.

Method note: the shared WebSearch budget for the run was exhausted before this stream started, so discovery was done by browsing publisher index/search pages (HousingWire, RISMedia, NAR, Knight Frank, Appraisal Institute) and the arXiv API, then reading individual articles with WebFetch. Inman (403), Mansion Global (blocked), Zillow (403) and the Appraisal Institute journal archive (member login) could not be read. Bloomberg/Economist were not attempted for the same paywall reason. This skews the source base toward US trade press and Knight Frank's global notes.

## 1. How buyers search: channels and the role of the agent (US benchmark)

- **Agents remain the dominant intermediary in the US despite portals and AI.** NAR's 2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers (survey period July 2024 to June 2025, released 4 Nov 2025) reports 88% of buyers purchased through an agent (92% of previously-owned home buyers) and 91% of sellers used an agent (tied for the record); FSBO fell to an all-time low of 5%. Sellers' median tenure reached an all-time high of 11 years; median distance moved fell to 30 miles. First-time buyers were a record-low 21% share, median age 40; repeat buyers median age 62; 30% of repeat buyers paid all-cash. Only 24% of buyers had children under 18 at home (an all-time low), which matters because it lowers the weight of school catchment as a population-wide criterion in the US. Sources: https://www.nar.realtor/newsroom/first-time-home-buyer-share-falls-to-historic-low-of-21-median-age-rises-to-40 ; https://www.nar.realtor/research-and-statistics/research-reports/highlights-from-the-profile-of-home-buyers-and-sellers ; https://www.housingwire.com/articles/nar-91-sellers-agents/
- **AI assistants are reportedly displacing portals as the first research step (vendor data, treat with caution).** A HousingWire piece (5 May 2026) summarising FlyDragon's "2026 State of AI SEO in Real Estate" claims 67% of homebuyers now use AI as a primary research method (up from 17% eighteen months earlier), Zillow's share of agent-discovery traffic fell from 41.2% to 33.8%, and 71% of buyer queries were hyperlocal; buyers ask about 8.7 questions before shortlisting 2-3 agents. This is a marketing-vendor study and not independently verified; the direction (hyperlocal Q&A replacing filter-based search) is the useful signal. Source: https://www.housingwire.com/articles/agents-invisible-ai-search/
- **Luxury and international search behaviour.** Coldwell Banker's Global Luxury 2026 mid-year report (HousingWire, 16 Jul 2026): luxury buyer interest doubled in the first five months of 2026, international buyer searches up 100%, searches for unique properties up 146% and land up 97%; nearly 40% of luxury specialists said clients will compromise on condition for a desirable location; nearly two-thirds reported more all-cash deals. Source: https://www.housingwire.com/articles/luxury-buyer-interest-doubles-demand-shifts-to-larger-properties/

## 2. Buyer criteria: primary vs secondary

### Primary (consistently top-ranked)
1. **Price / affordability** - ranked first in John Burns Research and Consulting's buyer survey cited by HousingWire (10 Mar 2026). Source: https://www.housingwire.com/articles/builders-home-design-trends/
2. **Location** - second in the same survey; in luxury, location dominates condition (about 40% of specialists say clients trade condition for location). Sources: https://www.housingwire.com/articles/builders-home-design-trends/ ; https://www.housingwire.com/articles/luxury-buyer-interest-doubles-demand-shifts-to-larger-properties/
3. **Safety / low crime** - Redfin's May 2025 survey of 1,224 prospective buyers: 78% call personal safety a must-have, 74% low crime, 68% low natural-disaster risk, about 67% access to grocery stores. Yet 22% would compromise on safety to afford a home - showing that affordability can override even the "must-haves". Source: https://www.redfin.com/news/survey-homebuyers-compromise-safety/
4. **Size / bedrooms and outdoor space** - for families in the Redfin survey, number of bedrooms and indoor/outdoor space ranked above schools. Source: same Redfin URL.
5. **Construction quality and condition** - third in the John Burns ranking; maintenance needs and energy efficiency follow. Source: https://www.housingwire.com/articles/builders-home-design-trends/

### Secondary / tie-breakers
- **Schools** - 59% of buyer-families in the Redfin survey call highly-rated schools essential, but 41% would trade them off; NAR's record-low share of buyers with children reinforces that schools are a segment criterion, not universal. Sources: Redfin URL above; NAR newsroom URL above.
- **Commute time, restaurants, neighbourhood character** - in the Redfin survey these were prioritised below home features and safety. Source: Redfin URL above.
- **Design and style** - only sixth in John Burns' ranking; buyers favour functional kitchens (counter and storage), pocket offices, mudrooms, and inexpensive trim/lighting upgrades over aesthetic finishes. New US homes averaged 2,155 sq ft in 2025 vs 2,465 in 2015 (NAHB, via HousingWire). Source: https://www.housingwire.com/articles/builders-home-design-trends/
- **Wellness / aging-in-place (luxury)** - Sotheby's International Realty 2026 mid-year luxury outlook ($10M+): 38% of agents cite aging-in-place as increasingly important; lifestyle ranked top factor (62%), followed by taxes and economic stability. Source: https://www.housingwire.com/articles/longevity-emerges-as-key-driver-of-luxury-real-estate-demand/

## 3. How sellers and agents set the price (CMA practice)

- **CMA is the agent's tool for list price; appraisal is the lender's tool for collateral.** RISMedia's explainer distinguishes the CMA (agent, sets listing/offer price), BPO (agent, probable future selling price, used by servicers), AVM (statistical model, used by lenders/investors) and appraisal (licensed appraiser, required for most federally regulated loans above $250,000). They are complementary; practitioners triangulate. Source: https://www.rismedia.com/2019/01/09/making-sense-valuation-alphabet-soup-cma-bpo-avm-appraisal/
- **Core CMA logic** (NAR Center for REALTOR Development via RISMedia): comparables adjusted for current market conditions; absorption rate used to show whether it is a buyer's or seller's market; overpricing leads to longer time on market and real/opportunity costs. Typical seller objections and the prescribed responses: renovation cost is not buyer value; Zestimates miss condition and concessions; a neighbour's sale may not be a true comp. Source: https://www.rismedia.com/2018/12/05/pricing-strategy/
- **Sellers should test the agent's comp logic, not the presentation.** HousingWire (31 Jul 2026, Gary Lanham): ask why each comp was chosen, which is most similar, and what would make a buyer pay more or less; real skill lies in explaining what comps cannot capture - floor plan, natural light, road noise. Source: https://www.housingwire.com/articles/ai-real-estate-listing-pitch/
- **Net vs gross.** HousingWire (12 Aug 2026) frames the agent's value as strategic pricing against buyer behaviour, knowing which improvements return value, and handling multiple offers. Source: https://www.housingwire.com/articles/nar-91-sellers-agents/
- **AI-assisted CMAs are now mainstream tooling.** RPR's AI CMA (NAR-owned data platform) scores comps by similarity, lets agents edit comp criteria with automatic re-scoring, and outputs four pricing strategies; refreshes every 48 hours. Source: https://www.rismedia.com/2026/08/13/rpr-wins-2026-real-estate-analytics-platform-year-proptech-breakthrough/
- **Exposure affects price.** ARELLO study (HousingWire, 23 Sep 2026), 10M+ US transactions Jan 2024-Jun 2026 benchmarked against Zillow AVM expectations: private (off-MLS) listings sold 1.26% below expectation in 2024 and 0.87% below in 2025; coming-soon listings sold 1.16-1.38% above; the penalty was larger in urban markets (1.54%) than rural (0.81%) and largest in majority non-white neighbourhoods (2.21%; Hispanic 3.33%). Source: https://www.housingwire.com/articles/arello-private-listings-study/

### Behavioural evidence on seller pricing (academic)
- **Loss aversion anchors asking prices.** Genesove & Mayer (NBER w8143, 2001; Boston condos 1990s): sellers facing nominal losses set asking prices 25-35% of the gap between expected price and original purchase price higher, achieved 3-18% higher sale prices than comparable properties, but had markedly lower sale probability; owner-occupants showed twice the effect of investors. The purchase price paid is therefore a de facto (non-market) seller criterion. Source: https://www.nber.org/papers/w8143
- **Valuation precision limits intermediation.** Buchak, Matvos, Piskorski & Seru (NBER w28252, 2020, rev. 2025): iBuyers using algorithmic pricing can only intermediate liquid, easy-to-value homes; faster transactions mean less accurate valuations and adverse-selection risk. Implication: standardised stock (e.g. new-build flats) is AVM-friendly; idiosyncratic period houses are not. Source: https://www.nber.org/papers/w28252
- **AVM signals help sellers.** A Marketing Science study (reported by HousingWire, 15 Dec 2025) finds Zestimate availability raised buyer surplus about 6% and seller profit more than 4%, with larger gains (buyer surplus >9%) in lower-income areas, because sellers price more confidently and underprice less. Source: https://www.housingwire.com/articles/study-finds-zillow-home-estimates-improves-buyer-seller-outcomes/ (secondary report; original paper not read).

## 4. Appraisal standards and the comparable-sales method

- **Sales comparison is primary for residential.** NAR's consumer guide: appraisers mainly use recently sold comparables from MLS data and adjust for condition, recent renovations, amenities, location and size. Source: https://www.nar.realtor/the-facts/consumer-guide-the-appraisal-process
- **Comp selection rules (Fannie Mae Selling Guide B4-1.3-08, updated 4 Jun 2025):** at least three closed comps; sales within 12 months preferred but the most similar sale beats the most recent; comps from the subject's market area, distance reported as straight-line with direction; similar site, room count, finished area, style and condition; competing-area comps allowed if justified and location-adjusted; listings and pending sales are supplementary only. Source: https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b4-1.3-08/comparable-sales
- **Appraisals cluster at contract price.** Calem, Lambie-Hanson, Nakamura & Kenney (Philadelphia Fed, 2018): about 30% of purchase appraisals equal the contract price exactly and fewer than 10% fall below; AVMs were more informative about default risk than appraisals. This "confirmation bias" means the agreed price, not independent evidence, often sets the appraised value. Source: https://www.philadelphiafed.org/consumer-finance/mortgage-markets/appraising-home-purchase-appraisals
- **Appraisal Institute position (HousingWire op-ed by its president, 28 Aug 2026):** AVMs and AI lack standards for bias and transparency, and miss condition, design appeal, unusual features (tennis courts, solar), local cost factors (insurance premiums, zoning, utility costs) and the difference between renovation cost and value added. Source: https://www.housingwire.com/articles/ai-home-valuation-appraisals/
- **International standards.** IVS (IVSC) is organised as seven general standards (IVS 100-106: framework, scope, bases of value, approaches, data and inputs, models, documentation) plus asset standards; the explicit "data and inputs" and "valuation models" standards are relevant to AVM governance. Source: https://www.ivsc.org/standards/ . TEGoVA's EVS 2025 (10th edition, effective 1 Jan 2025) keeps Market Value as the basis, adds a "property value" concept for prudent mortgage lending, and in EVS 6 requires valuers to reflect recognisable market impacts of energy efficiency and mandatory EU renovation requirements; it retains a residential valuation report template. Source: https://tegova.org/european-valuation-standards-evs

## 5. AVMs: accuracy, inputs and regulation

- **Published accuracy.** Redfin Estimate (Sept 2025): median error 1.89% on-market and 7.38% off-market, using 500+ data points; explicitly not a substitute for an agent's CMA. Source: https://www.redfin.com/redfin-estimate . Zestimate off-market median error is about 7% nationally per a HousingWire op-ed (14 Jul 2026), which also reports KBB Homes' claim of within-3% accuracy by requiring owner photos, condition and renovation inputs. Sources: https://www.housingwire.com/articles/kelley-blue-book-homes-vs-zillow/ ; https://www.housingwire.com/articles/ai-home-value-estimates/
- The low on-market error is partly because the list price is itself an input - so on-market AVMs mostly echo the agent's CMA.
- **Owner-supplied condition data is the frontier.** Multiple 2026 trade pieces argue AVMs "don't know the home" (renovated kitchen, new roof not in public records) and that the next generation combines AI with owner/agent inputs; an anecdote reports a $50M deal nearly derailed by ChatGPT valuations given to both sides. Source: https://www.housingwire.com/articles/ai-home-value-estimates/
- **Regulation.** US interagency AVM quality-control rule (CFPB, OCC, FRB, FDIC, NCUA, FHFA; issued 24 Jun 2024, effective 1 Oct 2025) requires accuracy/confidence, data-manipulation safeguards, conflict-of-interest controls, random-sample testing and nondiscrimination compliance for AVMs used in primary-residence mortgage decisions. Source: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/final-rules/quality-control-standards-for-automated-valuation-models/
- **Academic evidence on what improves AVMs:**
  - Images: Poursaeed, Matera & Belongie (2017/2018) show CNN-scored interior/exterior "luxury level" plus size/bedrooms beat Zillow's estimates on their test data - visual condition and finish are price-relevant criteria that tabular AVMs miss. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.05489
  - Multimodal: Huang et al. (2025 survey) identify five modalities - attributes, market data, text, images/street view, GIS/POI - and report multimodal models outperform single-modality ones (e.g. adding listing text gave about 2.16% accuracy improvement in one cited study). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.22119
  - Submarkets: O'Donovan, Finley & Sweeney (2025, Ireland - a major London-migrant origin) segment the country into urban/suburban/rural submarkets; out-of-sample R2 0.83 in Dublin vs 0.70 rural; national median absolute percentage error about 14% (13% Dublin, 16% rural). Energy rating (BER) is strongly priced: A-rated homes carry roughly 15-16% premium and G-rated about 15% discount relative to C. Location dominates variable importance. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.12625
  - Spatial neighbours: Riveros et al. (2024, about 200,000 houses in Santiago, Chile) find graph neural networks capturing neighbouring-house similarity improve prediction - formalising the "nearby comps" logic. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.06553

## 6. Global prime cities and London's position (Knight Frank Wealth Report 2026)

- **Global prime prices +3.2% in 2025** (3.6% in 2024); 73 of 100 PIRI markets rose. Middle East led (Dubai 500+ sales above $10M), North America the only negative region. Six drivers: wealth creation, tax/political rhetoric triggering migration, multi-jurisdiction lifestyles, competing hubs attracting family-office outposts, scarcity of move-in-ready prime stock, branded-residence premiums. London: tax rules push budgets lower and encourage renting; Singapore volumes constrained by stamp duty; NYC low turnkey inventory. Source: https://www.knightfrank.com/research/article/2026/4/piri-100-ultimate-prime-residential-property-index
- **Europe:** Zurich and Frankfurt rising, Paris and Milan slightly up, London down; Spain, Portugal and Italy (flat-tax regime) the winners; tax stability and predictable governance cited as the top cross-border criterion; UK non-dom abolition triggered relocations. Source: https://www.knightfrank.com/research/article/2026/4/europe-prime-housing-markets
- **London specifically:** globally $10M+ sales are rising but London is "moving backwards"; the non-dom change pushed marginal residents to leave, though many keep their London homes; competitors (UAE, Italy, Switzerland) lack London's breadth of institutions and culture. Source: https://www.knightfrank.com/research/article/2026/4/london-learns-share-spotlight
- **Neighbourhood "place-making" as a value driver:** Cadogan Estate (Chelsea) reports food-and-drink venues up 70% since 2020 and a curated retail/artisan-food street strategy aimed at street vitality rather than rent; Chelsea ranked among Knight Frank's 10 "hottest" markets (US$0.875M-13.4M). Sources: https://www.knightfrank.com/research/article/2026/4/london-chelsea-curated-collaboration ; https://www.knightfrank.com/research/article/2026/4/hottest-housing-markets
- **What global lifestyle buyers seek elsewhere:** the top-10 list emphasises culture/restaurants/members' clubs (Upper East Side), lakeside understated living (Zurich), privacy and space (Queenstown), walkable waterfront (Geelong), beach and views (Pacific Palisades). Branded residences and wellness infrastructure are rising criteria (Knight Frank Residence Report, about 1,800 schemes in 90 countries). Sources: https://www.knightfrank.com/research/article/2026/4/hottest-housing-markets ; https://www.knightfrank.com/research/reports/global/the-residence-report

## 7. Regional and segment nuances

- **US mass market:** price > location > quality/condition > safety > size; schools and commute are segment-dependent. AVMs are widely used and published by portals; the MLS comp is the price anchor.
- **Luxury/global prime:** location and lifestyle beat condition; tax regime and political stability are city-choice criteria; scarcity of turnkey stock lifts premium for move-in-ready homes; all-cash buying common, reducing appraisal discipline.
- **Europe (EVS):** energy performance is now an explicit valuation consideration; the Irish evidence puts EPC-equivalent premiums around +/-15%.
- **Thin/rural markets:** AVM accuracy drops sharply (Ireland rural R2 0.70 vs Dublin 0.83; Redfin off-market 7.38%); human comps and competing-area adjustments matter more.

## 8. Disagreements and caveats

- **AVM value vs appraiser value:** the Appraisal Institute argues AVMs lack accountability and miss condition; the Philadelphia Fed paper finds appraisals themselves add little information beyond the contract price and that AVMs predict default better; the Marketing Science study finds AVMs improve outcomes even where less accurate. Both can be true: AVMs are good at market-level signals, poor at idiosyncratic condition.
- **Safety vs affordability:** Redfin shows 78% call safety a must-have yet 22% would compromise it - stated priorities are elastic under budget pressure.
- **AI search adoption figures** (67% using AI as primary research) come from a vendor and conflict with NAR's 88% agent usage; agents still close most deals even if discovery is shifting.
- **Exposure:** ARELLO's off-MLS penalty is measured against a Zillow AVM baseline, so it inherits AVM error.
- **US-centric:** most trade data is US; transferability to London (where there is no MLS and asking prices are agent-set from Land Registry and portal comps) is partial.
