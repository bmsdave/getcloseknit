# ldn-renters: fact-check verdicts

- **1. 41.6% ONS / 48% Canopy, highest region, every borough >40%** - partially. 41.6% (2024, ONS via Property Reporter) and 48% of take-home pay (Canopy, The Negotiator) are confirmed, and so is London being least affordable. No source says every borough exceeds 40%. ONS says only that the least affordable LAs are all in London, and Generation Rent says the five least affordable places are London boroughs (London average 42%).
- **2. RRA from 1 May 2026: bidding ban, 1-month rent-in-advance cap; Foxtons 10-15% / ~450k** - supported. london.gov.uk and NRLA confirm both rules from 1 May 2026 (NRLA: for new tenancies). Foxtons gives 10-15% of tenants and ~450k of 3m London renters; 450k is the top of the range, since 10-15% of 3m is 300-450k.
- **3. 36% more landlords raised asking rents; enquiry:application 1.61 -> 1.99; selection by salary/persona** - supported. Chestertons (The Negotiator) reports a 36% annual increase in landlords lifting initial prices, and 1.99x vs 1.61x averaged over the previous six months. London The Inside supports the shift to salary/persona, but the evidence is anecdotal. Which? supports none of these points.
- **4. GBP 1,620 / 2,206-2,294 / 2,791** - partially. 2,206 (Hometrack/Zoopla Q2 2026), 2,294 (Hamptons new lets) and 2,791 (Rightmove Q2 2026 asking) are confirmed. Trust for London's 1,620 is a median one-bed (geometric mean, 2024/25), not an all-stock average.
- **5. Accent ~13% less likely to get viewing; minority 39-44% vs 51% White British; job info doesn't close gap** - partially. IZA DP15460 confirms 8.6pp, which is 13% lower. Cambridge WP 2023-07 (London) Table 4 with no job info: British 51%, Indian 39%, Nigerian 42%, Polish 40%, Chinese 49%. So the minority range is 39-49%, and 44% is the all-groups average. Polish is not minority-ethnic. The abstract confirms that job info doesn't bridge the gap.
