# International buyers, relocation and prime London: research notes

Stream: international buyers, relocation agents and prime London. Research date: 5 October 2026.

Method note: the WebSearch budget for this session was used up before this stream began, and DuckDuckGo, Mojeek and Brave returned CAPTCHAs or rate limits, which I did not try to get around. ## 1. Context: the prime London market in 2025-2026 (numbers)

- **Prices are still well below the 2014-15 peak.** In September 2026, Knight Frank recorded the first quarterly rise in prime central London (PCL) prices in four years, +0.3%. The annual change was -2%, and prices are 22% below the August 2015 peak. Prime outer London (POL) was -0.6% for the year and has stayed within ±1% since May 2025. Exchanges were down 2.5% year on year. The £10m+ segment had 121 sales worth £2.4bn in the year to September, 14% more by value. The low point was 106 sales in November 2025, against 155 a year earlier. [KF Sep-2026; PIE Oct-2026]
- **PCL and POL are diverging.** In the three months to August 2026, PCL transactions were 8% below the five-year average and POL transactions 10% above it. PCL was -3.3% for the year and -23% over 11 years. POL was -0.4% and -7% over a decade. [KF Sep-2026 transactions; KF "Another billionaire says goodbye"]
- **Savills' figures are a little more bearish.** Savills puts PCL 26% below its 2014 peak and -2.5% so far in 2026. It expects growth to return in 2028, with +7.5% cumulative to 2030, and says performance will stay "highly selective". Sub-markets differ: Notting Hill (family market) is worse than -4% for the year, and fringe areas such as Earl's Court, Pimlico and Westminster are worse than -6%. Knightsbridge is 25-30% down, attributed to "missing the traditional Middle East interest". Chelsea is about 27% below 2014. [Savills "Value is in the eye of the buyer"; Savills "PCL sales market", both 10 Sep 2026]
- **Disagreement on the size of the fall from peak:** Knight Frank says -22% (from August 2015) and Savills says -26% (from 2014). The difference comes from each firm's own index and base date. Neither is a Land Registry figure.

## 2. Tax regime: the main external filter for international demand

### 2a. Non-resident SDLT surcharge (primary cost factor for non-resident buyers)
- A 2-percentage-point surcharge has applied to residential purchases in England and Northern Ireland since 1 April 2021. It stacks on top of all other rates, including the higher rates for additional dwellings. A buyer is non-resident if they were in the UK for fewer than 183 days in the 12 months before purchase. If any joint buyer is non-resident, all buyers are treated as non-resident. The surcharge can be refunded if the buyer then spends 183 days in the UK within a continuous 365-day period inside the two-year window, and the claim must be made within two years. [GOV.UK rates for non-UK residents]
- Policy history: the 2019 consultation proposed 1%. The final rate was 2%, announced in July 2020. The stated aim was that non-resident purchases were "pushing up house prices for UK residents". The consultation landing page gives no quantified estimate of foreign buyers' share or of the price effect. [GOV.UK consultation page]
- How forum users experience it: on r/HENRYUK, a family relocating from France with a £3-4m budget was told by the top-voted reply that the slice above £1.5m is taxed at 12%, with a further 5% if the home counts as an additional dwelling. Others estimated about £300k of tax and advised renting first. Some said a buyer must hold for around 15 years to recoup the tax. [Reddit 1ngxek6]
- A cross-border detail: overseas property counts towards the additional-dwelling surcharge. A London first-time buyer who owned two Spanish flats at about £25k each debated the £40k de minimis rule. Most replies, citing HMRC manual SDLTM09780, read it as applying per property interest. One dissenter argued it applies to the combined value. [Reddit 191sbyi]

### 2b. End of the non-dom regime (6 April 2025)
- What changed: domicile was replaced by a residence test. There is a 4-year foreign income and gains (FIG) regime for people arriving after 10 years of non-residence, inheritance tax is now residence-based, and there is a temporary repatriation facility. [GOV.UK policy paper, Oct 2024]
- **Size of the group (HMRC, final data to 2024-25):** 73,400 non-dom taxpayers (-0.5%) plus 8,500 deemed-domiciled, together 81,900 (-1%). They paid £13.6bn in total liabilities (+9%). **57% live in London and 73% of their income tax, CGT and NICs comes from London.** About 8,600 arrived and about 9,000 left in the latest year. [HMRC statistical commentary]
- **Where they live (CAGE Warwick, 2022):** non-doms make up more than one in ten adults in Kensington and in Cities of London & Westminster, and more than 20% in some local areas. There is a strong positive link between non-dom share and local house prices. They form national enclaves: Western Europeans in Westminster, Kensington and Chelsea; Americans in parts of north central London; other English-speaking nationals south of the river around Richmond; Indians in suburban Harrow, Hillingdon and Bromley. The share of non-doms rises steeply with income, to 41% of those earning £5m or more. About 93% were born abroad. The largest groups are from India, the US, Western Europe (especially France) and other English-speaking countries, and the Indian share rose from about 4% in 2001 to about 14% in 2018. [Advani, Burgherr & Savage, CAGE Briefing 36]
- **Effect on housing, as the consultancies see it:** Knight Frank's 2026 Wealth Report describes "a thinning of demand" rather than an exodus. Wealthy people on the margin left, but "they're not selling their houses". [KF "London's shifting spotlight"] Savills makes the same point: non-doms who left for tax reasons keep their London "foothold", which cuts new instructions. Billionaires who split time between several homes are less affected and still buy trophy homes. Millionaire "pied-à-terre" buyers of £5-15m large flats are the most exposed group, including buyers of new developments with "price premiums and hefty service charges". [Savills PCL sales market]
- **Moving from buying to renting:** Knight Frank's PIRI 2026 summary says London tax rules "push budgets lower and encourage renting". [KF PIRI 100] Super-prime lettings (over £5,000 a week) started 13% above the five-year average, and occupiers are "wait-and-see" on buying for 12-24 months. [KF super-prime lettings, Sep 2026] Beauchamp's survey found deals at £1,000+ a week up 154% year on year in H1 2025: 1,588 deals worth £82.8m, 37% of them in Mayfair/Marylebone. [PIE / Beauchamp]
- **Investor exit:** HMRC data obtained by Bowmore show non-resident individuals sold 16,520 UK homes in 2025-26, down 8.9%. Only 70 sales were over £5m. The article cites London gross yields of about 2% against 4.6% on five-year gilts, the Renters' Rights Act, and a planned 2-point rise in property income tax in 2027. [PIE non-residents selling less]
- **Forum split:** in a 186-comment r/unitedkingdom thread on "tax data allays fears of non-dom exodus", one side said the data proves fears were overblown. The other side gave anecdotes of mobile millionaires leaving for Dubai, Italy and Switzerland and argued the data lags by 18 months. Several commenters said property is a minor share of the super-rich's assets and can be held from abroad. [Reddit 1mpuj3x] A Knight Frank commentary on hedge-fund founder Chris Rokos moving to Greece shows how the industry frames these high-profile departures. [KF "Another billionaire says goodbye"]
- **Wealth-hub competition:** the Wealth Report says the UAE, Italy, Switzerland, Greece and Portugal now compete harder for mobile wealth, though none matches London's breadth. It quotes an EU Tax Observatory researcher: only a small share of the wealthy move purely for tax. [KF "Beyond the breaks"; "London's shifting spotlight"]

### 2c. A coming threshold effect: the 2028 High Value Council Tax Surcharge
- From April 2028, homes valued at £2m+ pay an extra annual charge in four bands: £2,500, £3,500, £5,000 and £7,500 (£5m+). The OBR estimates 156-165k homes are affected. It expects full pass-through into prices, about 3.8% fewer homes above £2m, a further 1.5% "clustering" just below band thresholds, and about 10% less high-value new-build. A buying agent reports this is already raised in £2m+ negotiations. [Pryor & Co, Apr 2026] This makes valuation-band thresholds a new pricing factor, much like the SDLT bands.

## 3. Who the international buyers are now (2025-2026)

- In central London, domestic buyers are 49% of Savills' sales, up from 44% ten years ago. North Americans are nearly 10%, helped by the exchange rate. [Savills "Value is in the eye of the buyer"]
- Gulf buyers are 25% of £15m+ sales, up from 20% in 2024, and are called the largest buyer group in that band. Beauchamp reported 15% more Gulf enquiries in two weeks, plus 10% more from UK expatriates in the Gulf. [PIE Mar-2026] Beauchamp also puts US plus Gulf buyers at 50% of £15m+ transactions. [PIE / Beauchamp lettings]
- Among tenants (a proxy for relocators), 35% of Savills' central London tenants are UK nationals, against about half in outer prime London. Western Europeans and North Americans are the largest international groups. Eastern Europeans are more common in Knightsbridge and Mayfair. Over-50s are concentrated in Knightsbridge, Belgravia, Kensington and Marylebone. [Savills "What makes PCL different"]
- In global terms, London was 3rd by $10m+ sales value in Q2 2026 ($1.43bn from 44 sales, about $32.6m average). Dubai was first (131 sales, $2.17bn) and Hong Kong second. [KF "Risers and fallers"] Savills World Cities: London capital values -1.9% in H1 2026, against Tokyo +7%, San Francisco +4.8%, New York +2.4%, Lisbon +3.3%. Across 30 cities, rents (+1.1%) outpaced capital values (+0.6%). Savills says London "remains the top destination for lifestyle factors". [Savills "Five things"]
- Academic background: Badarinza & Ramadorai (JFE 2018) find that foreign "risk" (political or economic shocks in buyers' home countries) has a strong and lasting effect on London house prices. The effect is concentrated in areas favoured by people from the shocked countries ("preferred habitat") and works through both safe-haven buying and immigration. I read only the abstract, from IDEAS. Bourne, Ingianni & McKenzie (arXiv 2022) count about 42,000 London homes owned through offshore companies, averaging £1.33m and about £56bn in total. These are more expensive and more geographically concentrated than ordinary stock, low-use homes or Airbnb listings.

## 4. Search criteria of prime and international buyers and relocators

### Primary (decisive) criteria
1. **Postcode and "village" quality.** Savills calls location "ever true", with buyers "future-proofing" by buying in the best postcodes. Belgravia is strong across all price ranges because the Grosvenor and Cadogan estates have invested in the high streets (Elizabeth Street, Motcomb Street, Sloane Street, Pavilion Road). [Savills PCL sales] Knight Frank calls London's "villages" a key differentiator from other world cities and names Marylebone, Islington, Kensington and Bayswater/Queensway. [KF "What to consider... PCL"] Neighbourhood segments: Kensington £3-8m family homes as the "sweet spot"; Notting Hill for the young tech-wealthy and for American second-home buyers; Hampstead now the next most active £5m+ market; St John's Wood and Regent's Park for family houses; Pimlico for value next to Belgravia (£1-4m houses). [Savills PCL sales]
2. **Move-in-ready condition.** Savills finds "immaculate" homes sell at a 30% premium to those needing upgrade, and lets at a 31% premium to "moderate" condition. Turnkey family homes are the resilient segment and can draw 2-3 bidders, while buyers avoid planning risk and the cost of extending. [Savills "Value is in the eye"; "What makes PCL different"; "PCL sales"] Knight Frank's PIRI cites a "scarcity of move-in-ready properties" as one of six global drivers. [KF PIRI 100]
3. **Schools (for families).** 55% of buyers with school-age or pre-school children put school proximity in their top two priorities. Areas in the top percentile for schools carry a 56% price premium over the regional average, 69% for detached houses. The analysis combines the share of pupils at Outstanding primaries, Key Stage 4 points and the independent-school share. London competition makes parents look across both state and independent schools. [Savills "Education, education, education", 2023] In the relocation threads, schools are often the first filter: French families are steered towards the Lycée Français (South Kensington) and French or bilingual schools in Hammersmith & Fulham, Clapham, Kentish Town and Hampton Court House. Hampstead and Dulwich are recommended for clusters of private schools. [Reddit 1ngxek6] Note: the CAGE enclave pattern (Section 2b) matches nationality-specific school choices.
4. **Tax-adjusted holding period (rent versus buy).** For relocators, SDLT plus the non-resident surcharge makes renting first the default advice. [Reddit 1ngxek6] Savills notes that high acquisition costs push buyers to "trade fewer times in their lifetime". [Savills PCL sales]
5. **Connectivity for internationally mobile households.** Heathrow access via Paddington, Heathrow Express or the Elizabeth line is a stated priority for a relocator commuting between countries. Commenters suggested Notting Hill, Bayswater, Maida Vale/Warwick Avenue, Queen's Park and Elizabeth-line areas such as Ealing. [Reddit 1h6ckdb]

### Secondary (tie-breaker) criteria
- **Aircraft noise.** A relocating family rejected Richmond because of Heathrow flights and "feeling far from the action". Commenters flagged Barnes and Ealing (during easterly operations). [Reddit 1ngxek6]
- **Walkable amenities:** parks, cafés, restaurants, a "lively community". [Reddit 1ngxek6; KF "What to consider"]
- **House type versus amenity trade-off.** Commenters note that detached or semi-detached houses mostly sit in low-density areas with fewer walkable amenities, and that accepting a terrace opens up most of London. [Reddit 1ngxek6]
- **Lateral space.** Westminster-type caps of 200 sq m on new lateral apartments and penthouses are shrinking the future supply of large flats. [KF "What to consider"]
- **New-development premium and service charges** count against pied-à-terre buyers, with Chelsea Barracks the exception. [Savills PCL sales]
- **Service and authenticity.** Knight Frank argues that generic amenities such as spas, gyms and cinemas no longer set homes apart. Location character, heritage and long-serving staff and operators do. [KF "Luxury's new scarcity"]
- **Security, staff quarters and parking:** none of the sources I could read had quantified evidence. Knight Frank's PCL guide explicitly does not cover them. Treat them as segment-specific (ultra-high-net-worth and Gulf) and unverified in this stream.

### Investor criteria (yield versus growth)
- PCL gross yields rose to 4.0% for flats (from 2.8% five years ago) and 3.3% for houses (from 2.6%). Nine in ten Savills PCL landlords bought for investment, and only half are private individuals; the rest are institutions, the Great Estates and long-term international investors. [Savills "What makes PCL different"] Bowmore puts London yields at about 2% against 4.6% gilts, a different measure and probably net or blended, so the two are not directly comparable. [PIE non-residents]
- Savills reports investment and development buying has "declined substantially" because of the additional-property SDLT surcharges. [Savills "Value is in the eye"]
- Crossrail/Elizabeth line: the Savills Portugal-hosted article on Elizabeth-line price effects returned 429 and was not read. Elizabeth-line evidence here is limited to forum mentions of Heathrow access and Ealing. [Reddit 1h6ckdb; 1ngxek6]

## 5. Buying agents: search briefs, off-market and valuation

- **Brief first.** Garrington's buying-agent checklist (Sep 2026): write a ranked brief before searching. Compromises on space hurt most because they are hardest to fix later. Check sold prices for comparable homes, not asking prices. Visit at different times. Check planning and flood risk. Verify school catchments with the authority. Set a walk-away figure before the second viewing. Treat an offer as a package, including timing and the buyer's position. [Garrington "Common mistakes"]
- **Off-market share differs by firm:** Pryor & Co says about 30% of its purchases are off-market. It handles 30-40 clients a year, buying from £500k flats to £50m homes in London and the South East. [Pryor "Sort of homes"] A buying agent posting on r/HENRYUK said more than 50% of 2025 acquisitions were off-market and that London is becoming "more like the US". Other posters doubted this. [Reddit 1raz3it] Garrington says a "significant number" without giving a figure. [Garrington off-market]
- **How agents value off-market homes:** Land Registry comparables, an independent RICS valuation, and their own knowledge of earlier private deals. Off-market does not mean cheaper. Price depends on the seller's circumstances, and sellers use private sales to test a price and withdraw "without consequence", with privacy the main motive. [Garrington off-market] Pryor's line is to ignore the asking price once a viewing is booked. It claims an average saving of £82k below asking over 17 years, a best case of £2m, and some clients advised to pay £300k over asking. One example is a West Oxfordshire home offered off-market at £4.5m that exchanged at £3.7m. [Pryor "What have we saved"] The firm also cites long relationships with selling agents (for example about 21 purchases from one Knight Frank office) as its source of early access and frank property assessments. [Pryor "Some estate agents sell"]
- **Fees:** Pryor's full buying service is £15k, a partial service £7.5k, and "pocket agent" advice £1k. Another commenter said their recommended agent has a £1m minimum purchase price. [Pryor; Reddit 1sse6tk]
- **What HENRY users think:** sceptical. Top comments call buying agents a waste of money below "many millions" or "£15m+", and useful mainly for anonymity, offshore buyers who will not inspect in person, or complex country houses. A minority says selling agents prefer buying-agent clients because they are better prepared and less likely to pull out, and that buying agents give early "grey market" access. One poster bought a Greenwich house before it was listed by building relationships with local agents directly. [Reddit 1sse6tk; 1raz3it; 1raz3it also includes a visibly AI-drafted promotional reply] On r/london, a returner from Canada asked whether North American-style buyer representation exists. Replies said buying agents or "property finders" exist but are "used mainly by the uber rich, partly for anonymity". [Reddit ouksv9]
- **Relocation agents:** Garrington's international page lists the problems overseas buyers face: lack of local knowledge, little time for viewings, out-of-date price information. It adds introductions to banks, accountants and tax advisers. [Garrington international] Forum advice for relocators: get tax advice before arrival [Reddit 1ngxek6], and expect to pay 6-12 months' rent upfront or provide a UK guarantor without UK rental history, with employers sometimes acting as guarantor. [Reddit 1h6ckdb]

## 6. London-specific versus other UK cities and world cities

- **London-specific:** 57% of UK non-doms and 73% of their tax are in London [HMRC]. The non-resident surcharge and the £2m+ surcharge bite mainly in London because of its price levels. The Great Estates (Grosvenor, Cadogan) shape whole streets and the value of postcodes [Savills]. National "enclave" geography matches school choices [CAGE]. Heathrow flight paths affect location choice in the west [Reddit]. London is described as unusually strong on lifestyle, with "villages" setting it apart from other world cities [Savills Five things; KF].
- **Other UK cities:** non-doms outside London cluster in Oxford and Cambridge (about 1.1-1.3% of adults) and Aberdeen [CAGE]. A PropertyTribes Expat Investors thread from an Egyptian buyer was looking at Liverpool or Manchester rather than London (opening post seen only in page metadata; replies unreadable), which fits the yield-seeking pattern of overseas investors outside London.
- **World cities:** Dubai leads $10m+ volumes, and London falls behind in that segment as $10m+ sales grow elsewhere [KF]. Tokyo, Cape Town and Seoul are supply-constrained growth leaders [Savills]. The UAE, Italy, Switzerland, Greece and Portugal compete for tax-mobile wealth [KF].
- **Countries of origin (for the migrant-origin part of the study):** non-doms come from India, the US, Western Europe (France), other English-speaking countries and China (rising) [CAGE]. Prime buyers are increasingly Gulf (UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar), US, China and mainland Europe [Savills PCL sales; PIE]. Also Gulf-based expatriates from India, Pakistan, Yemen and Lebanon, and Israelis [PIE Mar-2026]. Russian buyers have largely disappeared since 2022, as implied by Knightsbridge and Mayfair weakness [Savills].

## 7. Disagreements and caveats
- Knight Frank and Savills give different peak-to-now falls (-22% vs -26%) and differ in tone. Savills expects small falls in 2026, while Knight Frank reports the first quarterly rise.
- Non-dom effect: the industry says "thinning, not exodus, and owners keep houses". HMRC 2024-25 data show only a 0.5% fall in numbers, but that is before the April 2025 change. Forum posters argue the true outflow will show up in later data.
- Off-market share: about 30% (Pryor), more than 50% (an anonymous agent on Reddit) and "significant" (Garrington). All are self-reported marketing claims.
- Yields: 4.0% PCL flats gross (Savills) against about 2% London (Bowmore via PIE). The bases differ (prime gross vs general and possibly net).
- Sources do not quantify security, staff accommodation or "golden postcode" premiums per square foot. PrimeResi's £/sq ft and garden-square premium pieces are paywalled.
