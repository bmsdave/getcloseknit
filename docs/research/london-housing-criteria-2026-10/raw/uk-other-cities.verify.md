# Verification: Other UK cities and Scotland

1. **Affordability ratios and rent share** — supported. ONS 2025: England 7.6x, London 10.6x, North East 5.0x. ONS private rental affordability (FYE 2024): London 41.6%, North East 19.8% of private-renter household income (England 36.3%).
2. **Aberdeen bid premium** — supported. White et al. (NTU): premium below 5% in 1995-2001; almost 35% by 2006 (annual) and peak around 35% in Q1 2007 (quarterly); asking prices lagged sale prices by two to three quarters, and the bid premium reflects expectations faster than asking or sale prices.
3. **Zoopla speed and price change** — supported. Three-quarters of Scottish homes listed April-June sold within three months vs 30% in London; London -1.0% (weakest region); NW +3.1%, Scotland +2.6%, NE +2.3%. Index for September 2026.
4. **School premium and oversubscription** — supported. Gibbons, Machin and Silva (England primary schools): about 3% per one SD of value-added (or prior achievement). Hamptons: 71% of primaries offered places to all first-choice applicants in 2025/26 (down from 72% the year before), with the easing put down to the falling birth rate.
5. **Parking, garage, garden outside London** — partially supported. The Negotiator (one West Yorkshire agent) lists gardens, garages and parking at the top of the wish list but says nothing about stalled sales. In the MSE thread, posters say parking that is not shown clearly in the listing photos puts off viewers (one listing, not a general finding). The LSE paper (sercdp0074) supports about a 1% price rise per 1 percentage point of ward land in gardens, green space or water. "Primary filters" and "agents cite stalled sales" are overstated.
