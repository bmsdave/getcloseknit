# London home buyers: search criteria (stream `ldn-buyers`)

Research date: October 2026. Scope: what London buyers filter on and prioritise; primary vs secondary criteria; segment differences (first-time buyers, families, downsizers, investors); post-COVID shifts. Sources: portal data (Rightmove, Zoopla), consultancy and agency research (Knight Frank, Savills, Hamptons, Foxtons), lender and housebuilder surveys (Santander, Halifax, Peabody/Dataloft, Bellway, Barratt, L&Q), three academic papers, and seven forum threads (Mumsnet, MoneySavingExpert). Reddit could not be reached (both the JSON and old.reddit endpoints were blocked), so Mumsnet and MSE stand in for it.

## 1. How London buyers actually search (portal mechanics)

- **The budget filter comes first and is "soft".** Datta, Kulka & Wehbe (Warwick/CAGE WP 1628, Aug 2026) analysed 16 billion searches on a major UK portal (2019-2024). About 60% of searches set a maximum price filter. A session averages about 5 searches, and users change the price cap with 25% probability at each step. The average property viewed is about 12% below the user's maximum price. The authors read this as preferences under a *soft* budget constraint. Spatial filters (city, neighbourhood, train station, postal district, user-drawn radius) are the other core control, so in practice **price cap + location (often a station or radius) + bedrooms** is the first-pass filter set. https://warwick.ac.uk/fac/soc/economics/research/workingpapers/2026/twerp_1628-datta-and-kulka.pdf
- **Floorplans before photos.** Zoopla's 2024 behaviour data shows 80% of buyers examined the floorplan before the photos, which points to layout and size being screened early. Zoopla added 16 AI-assisted filters in 2024 (e.g. bathrooms, bedrooms). https://propertyindustryeye.com/?p=144822
- **Keyword filters show what the structured filters miss.**
  - *UK-wide:* Rightmove's top buyer keywords have stayed stable for years: garage, annexe and garden were the top 3 in both 2024 and 2025 (https://www.mpamag.com/uk/news/general/homebuyers-most-searched-for-locations-in-2025-revealed/559773 ; https://propertyindustryeye.com/uks-top-property-hotspots-of-2025-revealed/). The 2018-19 Rightmove list was garage, annexe, acre, garden, detached, cottage, character, bungalow, freehold, rural (https://www.rightmove.co.uk/press-centre/?p=469). Zoopla's top 2024 keywords were "freehold" (no. 1 for the second year running), then garage and annexe. Rising terms were "in need of modernisation" (+134 places) and "study" (+22). https://propertyindustryeye.com/?p=144822
  - *London-specific (from a search snippet, page not readable):* a Homes & Property / Rightmove analysis ranked London buyer keywords as garden first, then **freehold** (because so many London houses are split into leasehold flats and buyers want to avoid service charges), then "Help to Buy" (an affordability signal), with **balcony** fifth. Houseboats also feature in London. https://www.homesandproperty.co.uk/property-news/what-buyers-want-the-top-features-that-will-make-your-home-appeal-to-london-househunters-a129416.html (read_fully=false)
  - The national list leans rural or suburban (acre, annexe, bungalow, rural). The London list swaps these for **tenure (freehold), private outdoor space at flat scale (balcony/terrace) and affordability schemes**. This is the clearest London-specific signal in the portal data.
- **Where London buyers search.** In 2025 London was the UK's most-searched place. Within London the top buyer searches were Wimbledon, Fulham and Chiswick: family-house, green, well-schooled SW/W suburbs. Renters' top three were Canary Wharf, Clapham and Fulham. https://www.mpamag.com/uk/news/general/homebuyers-most-searched-for-locations-in-2025-revealed/559773
- **Excess demand concentrates in inner SW London.** Datta et al. build a "housing gap" (searches relative to listings) for 235,243 output areas. Parts of boroughs such as Wandsworth rank among the highest-gap areas in Great Britain. (Same Warwick paper.)

## 2. Primary criteria (the filters that make or break a London search)

| Rank | Criterion | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Price / borrowing capacity** (incl. running costs: service charge, energy) | 60% of searches carry a price cap (Datta et al. 2026). A third of buyers lost spending power to rate rises, 17% by more than 10% (Knight Frank 2023). Service charges now cut how much buyers can borrow (Hamptons 2026). |
| 2 | **Location / neighbourhood** (named area, often anchored on a station or commute) | 79% of London first-time buyers (FTBs) said liking the neighbourhood was "extremely important" (Bellway/Censuswide 2021, n=500). 76% of young London renters rated location important, and the same share expected to compromise on it (L&Q 2017). Forum consensus: "location first". |
| 3 | **Commute / transport access** (tube, Overground, Elizabeth line, rail; walk to station) | Transport links rose from 14th to 6th in the MFS buyer ranking, 2021 to 2024 (74% important). Knight Frank 2023: 86% essential or important. Hedonic evidence: about 1.5-5.8% higher price per km closer to a station within 2 km (Gibbons & Machin). |
| 4 | **Bedrooms / size / layout** | Number of bedrooms was the most-cited deciding factor for London FTBs (48%) (Bellway 2021). Two-bed homes are the most-sought FTB product (Peabody/Dataloft 2021). Floorplans are checked before photos (Zoopla). |
| 5 | **Property type & tenure** (house vs flat; freehold vs leasehold) | Freehold is a top-2 keyword in London and nationally (Zoopla, Rightmove). 73% of FTBs (England & Wales) now buy houses, up from 65% in 2015 (Hamptons via The Negotiator 2026). |
| 6 | **Outside space** (garden for houses; balcony/terrace for flats) | Garden or outdoor space: 74% essential (Knight Frank 2023), 76% important (MFS 2024). In London, 50% said a garden had become more important (Savills 2021). Balcony is a top-5 London keyword. |

### Notes on the primary set
- **Price is a hard ceiling, but a soft line.** Buyers revise caps mid-session. Forum threads repeatedly show the budget deciding the zone or area band before anything else. A £500-550k budget gets a 2-bed in many inner-south and east areas (Mumsnet 2024). £300-350k means outer zones or emerging areas (Mumsnet 2015). £150k is unrealistic for SW London (MSE). https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/am_i_being_unreasonable/5136061-can-i-still-buy-in-london ; https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/property/2272047-Where-would-you-spend-a-third-of-a-million-in-London ; https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/4691569/where-in-london-would-you-buy
- **Running costs have moved into the primary set (2024-2026).** This is new and London-heavy:
  - London service charges average about £2,801/yr (+6.4% YoY, +41% over 5 years, +64.5% over 10 years).
  - Nationally, 37% of flats have service charges above 1% of value, which is the level at which many lenders refuse or cut lending.
  - Flats with charges at or below 1% of value were 50% more likely to sell than flats at 2% or more (Hamptons, Mar 2026). https://www.landlordtoday.co.uk/breaking-news/2026/03/soaring-service-charges-on-leasehold-flats-hit-capital-values/
  - Cladding and EWS1 certificates, and short leases, add lending barriers. 61% of England's high-rise buildings are in London. https://www.mpamag.com/uk/news/general/why-londons-small-flat-market-crisis-runs-deeper-than-the-data-shows/578291
  - Energy efficiency: 72% of buyers rated it more important than before (Knight Frank 2023).
- **Transport is primary but measured as door-to-door time, not "tube or nothing".** Mumsnet and MSE posters repeatedly push buyers to drop a tube-only rule and consider Overground, Thameslink or Southeastern rail areas (Forest Hill, Sydenham, Catford, Walthamstow, Sutton). They argue journey time and frequency matter more than the line type. One 2024 thread had 94 replies, many saying that dropping the tube filter widens the options a lot. https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/property/3741542-400K-budget-where-to-buy-in-London ; https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/am_i_being_unreasonable/5136061-can-i-still-buy-in-london
- **New lines reorder location rankings.** In Rightmove's Elizabeth line study (2012 to 2022), buyer demand rose up to +245% (Twyford) and asking prices near Maryland station rose +108%, against +55% for London. Competition rose +869% at Abbey Wood. https://www.rightmove.co.uk/press-centre/2149-2

## 3. Secondary criteria (tie-breakers and "nice to haves")

- **Green space nearby (parks, commons).** This is the London substitute for a private garden:
  - Savills 2021 (n>1,100): parks or open space were a top-two priority for 55% of all respondents, up from 42%. In London, 57% wanted to be near a park, up from 31% in the first lockdown. https://en.savills.pt/blog/article/312245/residential-property/home-buyers-put-fresh-air-and-family-ahead-of-proximity-to-the-office.aspx
  - Peabody/Dataloft (n=3,744 FTBs, Dec 2020): in London, proximity to local open green space was the factor most likely to have *risen* since COVID. Outside London it was the private garden. https://www.peabodygroup.org.uk/media/czqdmyjq/peabody-first-time-buyer-outlook-2021.pdf
- **Broadband and connectivity.** This was the no. 1 "important" factor in the MFS 2024 survey at 82%, and 90% in Knight Frank 2023. It is usually a hygiene check rather than a search filter. https://www.propertyinvestortoday.co.uk/breaking-news/2024/08/what-buyers-want-most-and-least-when-purchasing-a-home/
- **Home office / study.** 73% essential or important (Knight Frank 2023). 48% of London Savills respondents (2021) called a separate workspace essential. "Study" climbed 22 places in Zoopla keywords in 2024. Its weight is easing as hybrid work settles: 57% hybrid, 27% fully remote, 16% full-time in the office (Knight Frank 2023). https://www.knightfrank.co.uk/research/article/2023/8/what-are-buyers-prioritising-as-mortgage-rates-rise
- **Local amenities (walkable high street, cafés, gyms).** Knight Frank 2023: 87% essential or important, against 16% in May 2020. Over half of London FTBs want essential shops within walking distance, and gyms, restaurants and coffee shops lead their wish-list (Bellway 2021). https://www.propertyreporter.co.uk/property/london-first-time-buyers-focusing-on-neighbourhood.html
- **Storage.** London-specific: storage ranks higher in London than elsewhere, where parking takes its place (Peabody/Dataloft 2021).
- **Parking / garage.** This is the no. 1 Rightmove buyer keyword nationally, and 74% in the MFS survey. In London it is secondary for younger buyers but matters to downsizers: 37% of over-50 Londoners prioritised off-street parking (Barratt London 2021). https://www.whathouse.com/news/barratt-london-over-50s-survey-findings/
- **Safety / "feel" of the area.** This comes up in almost every forum thread (walking home at night, avoiding "grotty" areas), but portals offer no filter for it. https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/5771544/north-london-house-buying-areas-advice/p3
- **Condition / ready-to-move.** Knight Frank 2025 reports that London new-home buyers strongly prefer build-complete over off-plan stock. On the resale side, "in need of modernisation" is rising as a value-seeking keyword (Zoopla). https://www.knightfrank.co.uk/research/article/2025/2/london-new-homes-update-february-2025
- **Extension or conversion potential.** This fell from 8th to 14th in the MFS ranking (2021 to 2024), as the pandemic-era "race for space" faded.

## 4. Segment differences

### First-time buyers (London's largest buyer group)
- **Scale.** A record 48% of London homes sold in H1 2024 went to FTBs, at an average £443,550 (Hamptons). https://www.mortgagesolutions.co.uk/news/2024/08/27/lower-mortgage-rates-keep-first-time-buyers-in-london-hamptons/ . Halifax's FTB review puts the UK FTB at about 33 years old with a ~£61k deposit (2024). London deposits are far higher, roughly 3x the UK average in earlier reviews (search snippet only).
- **Priorities, in order:**
  1. Neighbourhood they like (79%)
  2. Bedrooms (48%)
  3. Transport proximity (43%)
  4. Outdoor space (43%)
  5. Parks nearby (30%)
  6. Walkable shops (>50%)

  Over 60% of the Bellway sample preferred new-build. Apartment and semi-detached were roughly equal at 31% and 30% (Bellway 2021, London FTBs).
- **Compromise behaviour.** 76% expect to compromise on location, 50% expect to leave London, and 64% prefer to buy where they already rent (L&Q 2017, n=1,000 London renters aged 24-35). https://www.curvemag.com/blog/what-tops-the-wish-list-for-londons-first-time-buyers/
- **Scheme-driven.** Shared Ownership and Help to Buy drove new-build consideration. In London, two-thirds of respondents expected to work from home at least one day a week, against half elsewhere (Peabody 2021).
- **Shift from flats to houses.** 73% of FTBs now buy houses, against 65% in 2015. 19.9% of flats sold at a loss in 2025, and 38.1% of studios (Hamptons, Apr 2026). https://thenegotiator.co.uk/?p=198376 . Zoopla (Aug 2026) says London is the exception where flat demand persists, "with extra uncertainty". https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/uk-housing-market-news/flats-offer-growing-value-amid-falling-demand-says-zoopla/

### Families / upsizers
- **Schools become primary.**
  - Santander (Aug 2024, n=2,000): 63% of parents have paid or would pay extra for a catchment, against 26% in 2019. The catchment premium is about **25% in London** and 5% outside London. School proximity now outranks transport and closeness to family for parents. https://santander.co.uk/about-santander/media-centre/press-releases/parents-face-5-premium-on-properties-within-catchment
  - Savills (Oct 2023): only 82.6% of London secondary applicants got their first choice, and London and the South East dominate the top school-area lists. https://www.savills.co.uk/research_articles/229130/352571-0
  - Academic benchmark: a 1 standard deviation rise in school value-added or prior attainment raises prices about 3% (Gibbons, Machin & Silva, JUE 2013). https://cep.lse.ac.uk/pubs/download/sercdp0018.pdf
- **House over flat, garden, 3+ beds.** Family-buyer threads weigh garden, schools and commute together. In one 2020 Mumsnet thread with 78 replies, a ground-floor flat with a big garden in an outstanding-school area (Alexandra Park) clearly beat a small-garden house in SE London with weak schools and a long commute. That runs against the "house over flat" reflex. https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/property/3948709-flat-v-house
- **Lateral space over transit (2021).** Stacks Property Search reported buyers trading "vertical for lateral" space in suburbs such as Dulwich, West Norwood, Wimbledon and Crouch End, with the tube-proximity premium shrinking. https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/in-search-of-space-london-suburbs-red-hot-says-property-research-firm/
- **Upsizers who leave London.** Hamptons data (search snippets only) says Londoners buying outside the capital fell to 5.3% of purchases in Jan-Jul 2025, a 12-year low. Home Counties purchases by Londoners rose to 18.2%, the highest since 2017. Moves are clustering within 50 miles (54% in 2025). https://www.inkl.com/news/home-counties-hotspots-where-london-buyers-are-snapping-up-properties-revealed (read_fully=false)
- **Forum view.** Moving outward from a flat in an up-and-coming area to a house in Loughton, Buckhurst Hill or Shenfield was seen as sensible. Posters weighed station and frequency (Shenfield preferred) and Travelcard cost. https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/4966281/leave-an-up-and-coming-area-for-a-house

### Downsizers
- **Stay in London, prioritise access and low maintenance.** Barratt London (2021, over-50s):
  - Private outdoor space was rated most important by 51%.
  - Off-street parking 37%; proximity to family and friends 36%; access to green space 63%.
  - Only 32% think downsizing means leaving London, and just 21% of those in Zones 1-2 would leave.
- **What they buy and why.** Foxtons (Oct 2023) singled out downsizers as leading the London market, drawn to new-build: lateral layouts, lifts, lower bills, estate management and central locations. About 75% of over-65s own outright, which makes them cash buyers. https://www.foxtons.co.uk/discover/2023/10/downsizers-driving-london-demand-for-new-homes
- **Prime downsizers face shrinking lateral space.** Knight Frank notes Westminster caps new lateral apartments at 200 m², so the pipeline of large flats is depleting. Prime central London (PCL) buyers prioritise community, green space, the high street and schools. https://www.knightfrank.co.uk/theview/locations/what-to-consider-when-buying-in-prime-central-london

### Investors / landlords
- **A shrinking segment.** Landlords made just 10.9% of purchases in 2025, the first full year of the 5% stamp duty surcharge (Hamptons, search snippet). The Renters' Rights Act (May 2026) prompted sell-offs.
- **Criteria differ from owner-occupiers.** Investors look at yield, tenant demand, transport and EPC rather than garden or schools. London gross yields are about 5.7% against a 7.1% national average (BLA, search snippet). Knight Frank sees continued overseas-investor activity in London new homes. Investor criteria were less well covered in the sources read for this stream; see the rent and valuation streams.

## 5. Post-COVID arc: what changed, what reverted

- **2020 (race for space).**
  - 40% of buyers changed priorities, and 39% prioritised a garden or a bigger home.
  - All top-5 most-sought property types became houses, where two of the top five had been flats before.
  - One in five buyers wanted permanent work-from-home space (Rightmove via Housing Today, Jun 2020). https://www.housingtoday.co.uk/news/homebuyers-abandon-flats-in-favour-of-houses/5106370.article
  - Savills 2021, London: tube proximity as a top-two priority fell from 63% to 39%, and workplace proximity from 32% to 14%.
- **2021-2023 (consolidation).** Outdoor space stayed at the top (Knight Frank 2023: 74% essential). Amenities rebounded from 16% to 87%.
- **2024-2026 (partial reversal).**
  - In the MFS ranking (2021 to 2024), transport climbed 14th to 6th, square footage fell 2nd to 7th, and extension potential fell 8th to 14th.
  - Londoners leaving the capital hit a 12-year low in 2025 (Hamptons snippet).
  - Datta et al. (2026) find the extra search demand for private greenspace (+0.8% searches per extra 100 m² at peak) had **returned to zero by end-2023**. Transaction prices still showed a persistent premium, so prices overstate how lasting the garden preference is.
- **Disagreement.** Surveys (Knight Frank, MFS) still put garden or outside space at or near the top. The Warwick search data says the *incremental* COVID-era pull of greenspace has gone. Both can hold: gardens remain a top filter, but the extra premium has faded.

## 6. London vs the rest of the UK (within this stream's evidence)

| Dimension | London | Elsewhere in UK |
|---|---|---|
| Outdoor space | Balcony or terrace; proximity to parks and commons | Private garden; acreage and annexe (Rightmove national keywords) |
| Tenure | "Freehold" is a top London keyword; leasehold, service charges, cladding and EWS1 are primary risks | Freehold is common; leasehold issues are mostly in new-build estates and city flats |
| Transport | Tube, Overground, Elizabeth line and rail station proximity; door-to-door journey time | Car access: garage and parking are the no. 1 national keywords |
| Storage | Higher priority (Peabody) | Parking ranks in its place |
| Schools premium | About 25% catchment premium (Santander) | About 5% |
| Product | Flats still viable in London (Zoopla 2026), but FTBs are shifting to houses | FTBs mostly buy houses |
| Budget | London FTB ~£444k (Hamptons H1 2024) | UK FTB ~£311k (Halifax 2024) |

## 7. Implications for a search or area tool (synthesis)

- **Primary filters to expose:** price, including a monthly-cost view (mortgage plus service charge plus council tax); bedrooms; property type and tenure; door-to-door commute time to a user-chosen destination (not just "near a tube"); outside space as garden vs balcony/terrace; and park proximity.
- **Secondary layers:** school catchment and quality; walkable amenities; safety or crime; broadband; storage and study.
- **Leasehold risk flags are a London differentiator:** service charge as a share of value, lease length, building height and cladding.
