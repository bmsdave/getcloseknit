# Dubai and Middle East: how homes are searched for and priced (research notes, October 2026)

Stream: Dubai and Middle East (with short sections on Abu Dhabi, Riyadh and Tel Aviv).
Purpose: a comparison point for London. It shows which criteria matter more in a market that is state-registered, mostly expat, investor-heavy and off-plan-led.

## 1. The Dubai information system: why search and pricing differ from London

Dubai's market runs on registers and IDs that portals, agents and valuers all plug into. London has nothing directly comparable.

- **Every sale and lease is registered with the state, and the attributes are captured at registration.** The DLD/Property Finder House Price Index (2020 methodology) is built from data the Dubai Land Department (DLD) collects when a sale is registered. That data includes transaction date, master community/area, freehold flag, bedrooms, number of floors, parking flag, balcony flag, age, property type, registration/procedure/offering type, size and price. The index uses hedonic imputation with separate regressions per period, and runs separate models for all residential, apartments and villas. It excludes outliers: apartments under 31 m² or over 250 m², or priced under AED 250k or over AED 6.4m; villas under 112 m² or over 900 m², or priced under AED 630k or over AED 10.3m. The official model has **no view, floor-level or metro-distance variable**, even though the market prices these heavily (see section 4). https://dubailand.gov.ae/media/qnslugbi/the-dubai-house-price-index-methodology.pdf
- **Rents are registered too.** Ejari lease registration feeds the RERA/DLD rental index. Since January 2025 the "Smart Rental Index" has replaced the old RERA calculator. Its inputs are area, property type, bedrooms, current Ejari rent and renewal date. A 2025 upgrade added a **building classification on a 1-5 scale** (construction quality, finishes, maintenance, location, amenities), so two buildings in the same area can now have different benchmarks. https://www.bhomes.com/en/blog/betterinformed/how-rent-increases-are-calculated-under-rera-index-2025-edition-a-tenants-guide
- **Free public data on actual transactions.** DXBinteract (free, building-level DLD transactions, 1-2 week lag) is the comparables tool for consumers and agents. Dubai REST answers the ownership and encumbrance question. Property Monitor (Cavendish Maxwell, about AED 5-15k a year) layers Ejari rents and an AVM that separates floor, view and configuration effects; mortgage brokers, valuers and banks use it. Reidin (enterprise pricing) is used by institutions. According to this source, practitioners combine 2-3 tools: brokers pair Property Monitor for client valuations with DXBinteract for comps. The AVM is said to land within 5-10% of the eventual price for standard units, and individuals adjust median AED/sqft manually for floor and view (typically 5-15%). This is a promotional blog, so treat the accuracy claim as indicative. https://realestateclubdubai.com/blog/investment/dxbinteract-property-monitor-reidin-dubai-data-platforms-compared-2026
- **Portal valuations are tied to official IDs.** Bayut/dubizzle TruEstimate (launched summer 2024 with DLD data integration) looks up a unit by title-deed number, Oqood (off-plan contract) number, unit number, DEWA utility number or rental contract number. It returns an estimated sale and rental value, yield, transaction history, comparables and live competing listings. It is pitched to brokers as a way to align asking prices with demand and cut overpricing and time on market. https://www.dubizzle.com/blog/property/truestimate-dubizzle-brokers/
- **Listings must carry a permit.** Every advert needs a DLD Trakheesi permit (valid 60 days). Bayut's "TruCheck" badge goes further: the agent's permit must match the listing details, and the agent must take a live photo on site. Portals have removed listings from unlicensed agents. (Search-result summaries only; not read in full.) https://www.bayut.com/agentportal/heres-your-guide-to-the-trucheck-badge/

**London contrast:** in London, sold prices (Land Registry) and EPC data are public, but lease registration, unit-level IDs that portals can query, and rent caps tied to a public index do not exist. A Dubai agent's comparables step is close to automatic. A London agent still assembles comparables by hand from sold-price data plus local knowledge.

## 2. Primary criteria: renters

From the forums and portal data, roughly in order of importance:

1. **Budget versus the annual lump sum, and the number of cheques.** Rent is quoted per year and paid with post-dated cheques. The standard is 4. Some landlords want 1, and new or low-occupancy buildings and institutional landlords such as Wasl may allow 6-12 or monthly payments. Cushman & Wakefield (quoted in Gulf News, Aug 2024) says the number of cheques is negotiable and depends on occupancy, not on a fixed rent premium. Mumsnet posters say monthly payment is rare and quarterly is "as good as it gets". Cheques cannot be written without a residency visa and a bank account, so newcomers face a sequencing problem: visa, then bank, then lease. https://gulfnews.com/business/property/in-dubai-tenants-find-landlords-at-newer-buildings-flexible-on-rents---and-on-multiple-cheques-1.1723690802310 ; https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/living_overseas/5121446-dubai-offer ; https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/living_overseas/4826010-tips-to-relocate-to-dubai
2. **Community, chosen by lifestyle and family stage.** Families on Mumsnet name Arabian Ranches, Mira, Dubai Hills, Town Square, Damac Hills, The Springs, The Meadows, The Greens, JVC, Sports City and Motor City. Marina and JBR are seen as tourist-heavy and less family-oriented. Villa communities are preferred so that "kids can play out". https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/living_overseas/5031305-dubai-family-of-4-35000-aedmonth-will-it-work ; https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/living_overseas/5121446-dubai-offer
3. **Distance to school beats distance to work.** In the July 2024 thread, the family put school proximity ahead of the husband's commute (he drives). Posters point to KHDA-linked school finders. School fees of AED 45-65k per child per year are quoted, which strongly shapes how much is left for housing. https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/living_overseas/5121446-dubai-offer
4. **Traffic and car dependence.** JVC is described as having "horrendous" traffic (in search-result summaries of the threads). Car hire of AED 1,500+ a month is budgeted as standard. https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/living_overseas/5031305-dubai-family-of-4-35000-aedmonth-will-it-work
5. **Metro proximity (apartments, single renters).** Property Finder's June 2026 rent map says living more than 1 km from a major station cuts 1-bed rents by about 5-15%. Red Line stations rank above Green Line and Route 2020. 1-bed ranges include Downtown AED 120-190k, Marina 100-170k, Business Bay 90-160k, JVC 65-100k+ (no metro yet; Blue Line planned) and International City 42-55k. Gulf News (Mar 2024, citing Reidin-GCP) found the premium very uneven: up to 30% in JLT and Al Furjan, but only about 3% in Marina and Business Bay and about 6% in Downtown, where the whole district is already accessible. https://www.propertyfinder.ae/blog/the-dubai-metro-rent-map-the-cost-of-renting-a-1br-by-metro-station/ ; https://gulfnews.com/business/property/in-dubais-popular-residential-areas-metro-effect-showing-up-big-on-rents-1.1710388919279
6. **Affordability, location, space and lifestyle.** Bayut data for Jun-Aug 2026 shows rental searches up 44% year on year. The most-viewed apartment areas were JVC (median AED 70k), Business Bay (AED 99.9k) and Marina (AED 120k). The most-viewed villa areas were Damac Hills 2 (AED 110k), Mirdif (AED 150k) and Dubai Hills (AED 385k). https://www.khaleejtimes.com/property/dubai-residents-search-for-new-homes-rental-neighbourhoods-rents

**Secondary criteria for renters:** new building versus old (new landlords negotiate more), the building's index class (1-5), maid's room usable as an extra bedroom, bills (about AED 3k a month for a 4-bed, including DEWA cooling), and whether a property manager or the landlord collects the cheques.

**Regulatory overlay (Dubai-specific):** under Decree 43/2013, a landlord can raise rent at renewal only if the current rent is more than 10% below the index average. The allowed increase is 5% for a gap of 11-20%, 10% for 21-30%, 15% for 31-40% and 20% for over 40%. At least 90 days' written notice is required, and if it is missed the rent stays the same. This makes the index level a primary input for sitting tenants and landlords, and gives new leases a premium over renewals. Gulf News reports new tenants sometimes paying more for a 1-2 bed than sitting tenants pay for a 2-3 bed in the same building. https://www.bhomes.com/en/blog/betterinformed/how-rent-increases-are-calculated-under-rera-index-2025-edition-a-tenants-guide ; https://gulfnews.com/business/property/in-dubais-popular-residential-areas-metro-effect-showing-up-big-on-rents-1.1710388919279

## 3. Primary criteria: buyers and investors

1. **Off-plan versus ready, and developer reputation.** Off-plan made up 72% of Dubai transactions in Q1 2026 (32,607 off-plan against 12,551 ready), the same share as in 2025. Knight Frank notes that bond investors "are no longer treating all developers the same": they now focus on balance-sheet strength, and the gap between stronger and weaker developers is widening. Only about 60% of promised completions materialised in 2021-2025. 34% of units due in 2026 were still under 20% complete, so Knight Frank expects about 95.6k on-time completions against 144.9k forecast. Emaar (55k units), DAMAC (49k) and Azizi (40.8k) dominate the pipeline, and JVC alone has 35.8k units coming. https://www.knightfrank.ae/site-assets/pdf/2026/dubai-residential-market-review-q1-2026.pdf
   - Khaleej Times (Feb 2026) reports off-plan resales ("flips") at only 9% of off-plan deals in 2025. It also reports emerging double-digit secondary discounts in Sobha Hartland, Damac Lagoons and JLT, and buyers becoming "more selective". https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/is-dubai-off-plan-boom-facing-reality-check-on-flipping-risk
   - Forum view: a Mumsnet poster bluntly advises against off-plan, saying it may never be delivered and won't arrive on time. Others recommend renting for a year before buying (search-result summary of threads; the threads I read in full focused on renting). Posters also say the visa comes from the employer, not from owning property, unless the owner meets the golden-visa threshold. https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/living_overseas/5089086-uk-citizens-looking-to-live-in-dubai
2. **Price per sq ft against DLD comparables.** The citywide average was AED 1,933 psf in Q1 2026 (+10.5% year on year): apartments AED 1,872, villas AED 2,369. Selected areas: Palm Jumeirah AED 4,525, Emirates Hills AED 5,288, DIFC AED 3,290 (+47%), Downtown AED 3,010, Business Bay AED 2,613. The prime average across 10 neighbourhoods was AED 4,317. https://www.knightfrank.ae/site-assets/pdf/2026/dubai-residential-market-review-q1-2026.pdf
3. **Service charges, an ownership cost that drives yield.** Charges are quoted per sq ft and published in the DLD Service Charge Index (via Mollak or the Dubai REST app). They range from about AED 3 to AED 30+ psf: roughly AED 20-30+ in prime towers (Downtown, Palm), AED 12-18 in mid-range buildings, and low single digits for villas. JVC's low charges (about AED 12-16, with split AC units instead of district cooling) are a large part of why its net yields are high (blog summary, not read). BritishExpats posters reported a JBR service-charge rise of 229% to AED 21.5 psf, backdated, which comes to about AED 50k a year on a 3-bed (search snippet only). Buyers are told to check the index history, because new buildings often start with low charges. https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/housing/what-are-service-charges-in-dubai-everything-new-property-buyers-need-to-know-1.500439859
4. **Golden-visa threshold, a hard price cliff.** A property worth AED 2m or more qualifies for the 10-year visa. The test uses the DLD valuation, not the deed price. Since 2025, mortgaged and off-plan units qualify; off-plan must be at least 50% complete and from an approved developer, and the earlier AED 1m minimum down-payment rule was dropped. (Search-result summaries; pages returned 403.) Anarock (via Khaleej Times, Jul 2026) reports 21% of the 129.6k new investors in 2025 bought specifically for the golden visa, and 80% paid cash. The threshold should create bunching of asking prices at or just above AED 2m. https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/property/dubai-property-market-deals-indians-britons-top-buyers
5. **Yield.** UBS 2026 puts Dubai's price-to-rent ratio at about 16 years, one of the lowest in its 23-city sample, which is why investors underwrite by gross and net yield first. https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/property/2026/09/24/buy-dubai-property-bubble-strong-rents-demand-ubs-report/

**Secondary criteria for buyers:** view and floor (big money but unit-specific; see section 4), metro, building age and quality class, community master-developer brand (Emaar's Dubai Hills and Arabian Ranches carry a brand premium), handover date, payment-plan structure (for example 60% on handover), mortgage LTV and fees (DLD fee of 4%, plus other costs of up to about 8% per a BritishExpats snippet), and resale liquidity.

## 4. Price premiums: view, floor, metro

| Attribute | Evidence | Source |
|---|---|---|
| High floor with marina view (Dubai Marina towers) | 20-30% premium in both sales and rents | Cushman & Wakefield Core, Jul 2016 https://www.cushwake.ae/en/news/tenants-pay-premium-to-rent-top-floor-units-in-dubai |
| Sea-facing (JBR, Palm) | 10-20% over non-sea-facing units; Palm Shoreline sea-view premium grew 63% over five years | Global Capital Partners via Khaleej Times, Oct 2017 https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/what-is-a-room-with-a-view-worth |
| Burj Khalifa view (Downtown) | about 15% | same |
| Practitioner manual adjustment | 5-15% for floor and view on top of the median psf | realestateclubdubai 2026 (blog) |
| Metro, sale value (academic) | Residential: +7.8% within 1 km (diff-in-diff), peak +13% at 701-900 m, but -9% to -17.7% within 0.5 km (noise). Commercial: +41% to +76% | Mohammad, Graham & Melo, JTLU 2017 https://jtlu.org/index.php/jtlu/article/view/750 |
| Metro, rents (portal) | -5% to -15% once over 1 km from a major station; up to 30% in JLT/Al Furjan, 3-6% in Marina, Business Bay and Downtown | Property Finder 2026; Gulf News/Reidin 2024 |

Points of disagreement:
- The academic paper finds a **negative** effect for homes right next to a station (within 500 m). Portal and agent pieces show the largest rent premium closest to the station. The two can be reconciled: renters in studios and 1-beds value walkability, while sale values of larger homes are hit by noise. The paper also uses 2006-2013 data, from just after the metro opened.
- The view premium is quoted anywhere from 8% to 30% depending on the source and year. All of these are agent or consultancy estimates. The official DLD index does not model view.

## 5. Academic evidence (Dubai-specific)

- **Mohammad, Graham & Melo (JTLU 10(1), 2017; online 2015).** Uses DLD sale transactions with diff-in-diff and hedonic models on repeated cross-sections and pseudo panels. Results: the metro raised dwelling values within 1 km (+7.8% DID; +1.2% in the preferred hedonic model within 1.5 km); the peak is at 701-900 m (+13% residential, +76% commercial); and there is a negative effect within 500 m. https://jtlu.org/index.php/jtlu/article/view/750
- **Worku (IJHMA 10(4), 2017).** Uses 140,039 transactions over 10 years. Linear, nonlinear and double-log quantile regressions show that the effects of size, location, house type and regulation are nonlinear and differ across price quantiles: budget and luxury segments respond differently to the same attributes. This argues against a single hedonic weight set for all of Dubai, and by extension London. (Abstract read via RePEc.) https://ideas.repec.org/a/eme/ijhmap/ijhma-06-2016-0048.html
- **Hepşen & Vatansever (IJSPM 2012).** Uses monthly data 2003-2010 and cointegration. The Dubai residential price index moves with gold prices and foreign trade volume (positively) and with completed units (negatively). Supply completions are therefore a first-order macro driver, which matches Knight Frank's 2026 focus on delivery rates. https://journals.vilniustech.lt/index.php/IJSPM/article/view/4834

## 6. Market context, October 2026

- The Q1 2026 regional conflict (the "Iran war") ended a 5.5-year bull run. Knight Frank's anecdotal estimate is that mainstream prices fell up to 10%, while prime prices fell only 1-2%. Q1 had 45,158 deals (AED 137.3bn); 2025 was a record at 205,431 deals and AED 544.2bn. Knight Frank describes a "two-speed market" and forecasts prime +3% and mainstream +1% for 2026. https://www.knightfrank.ae/site-assets/pdf/2026/dubai-residential-market-review-q1-2026.pdf
- UBS Bubble Index 2026 puts Dubai at 1.16 (4th of 23 cities; "elevated"). Real prices rose 0.4% and real rents fell 4% (Q2 2026). A skilled service worker needs about 5 years of income for a 60 m² central flat, against 11 years in London. https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/property/2026/09/24/buy-dubai-property-bubble-strong-rents-demand-ubs-report/
- Anarock (via Khaleej Times): H1 2026 residential deals totalled AED 225.7bn and average prices were about AED 1,900 psf. Buyer nationalities in 2025 were India 22%, UK 17% and China 14%. Anarock calls the correction "sentiment-driven", with prices down 4-7%. Note: DLD publishes regional groupings, not exact per-nationality data, so broker nationality shares are extrapolations. https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/property/dubai-property-market-deals-indians-britons-top-buyers
- **Link to London:** Britons are the second-largest foreign buyer group in Dubai, and many Mumsnet posters are London or UK families comparing packages. Indians, Britons, Chinese and Russians are also large migrant or investor groups in London, so Dubai is both a competitor destination for London leavers and a reference market these buyers bring with them. They expect off-plan payment plans, portal valuations tied to official IDs, and per-sqft pricing.

## 7. Abu Dhabi (brief)

- A Mumsnet thread (Dec 2024, 11 participants) gives a clear area hierarchy. Saadiyat (beach, premium) and Yas/Al Zeina are expensive ("300k will not get you a villa"). Khalifa City gives space for money (a 500 m²+ villa for about AED 200k, 15 minutes to school, "max 30 mins to anywhere"). Reem Island is apartments only. Al Rawdah costs about half Saadiyat/Reem rents. Renting needs 12 months upfront with some negotiability. Posters distinguish standalone villas (mainly Emirati-occupied) from community compounds. https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/living_overseas/5234445-abu-dhabi-advice-from-expats
- Bayut H1 2026: on 3 June 2026 ADREC set the annual rent-increase cap to **0%** (previously 5%) as a temporary measure. Tenant demand is for larger homes, privacy and community living. Sample rents: 2-bed mid-tier AED 86-128k; 4-bed luxury villa AED 274-351k. Abu Dhabi also has an official ADREC rental index (Tawtheeq leases). https://www.bayut.com/mybayut/abu-dhabi-rental-market-report-h1-2026/

## 8. Riyadh (brief)

- Rents in Riyadh's urban area have been **frozen for 5 years from 25 Sep 2025**, for residential and commercial property and for existing and new contracts. A vacant unit's rent is locked at its last Ejar contract value. Leases renew automatically unless notice is given 60 days ahead, and landlords may refuse renewal only for non-payment, structural defects, own use or other cases REGA allows. Fines run up to 12 months' rent. Ejar registration is compulsory, with a 60-day window for objections. King & Spalding notes that valuations must now build in the freeze, so rent at the next letting becomes the key valuation input in Riyadh. https://www.kslaw.com/news-and-insights/saudi-arabia-introduces-rent-controls-and-automatic-lease-renewal

## 9. Tel Aviv (brief)

- The safety feature that matters most is the **mamad (protected room)**. Bank of Israel research covering about 99k deals since 2023 found a purchase premium of 8.2% before October 2023, falling to 6.4% by end-2024, then about 11% after the June 2025 Iran operation (preliminary). Rents are 3.7-4.1% higher (May-Dec 2024). In central Tel Aviv, new or protected flats sell for about NIS 71k/m² against about NIS 54k/m² for unprotected ones. Demand spikes after attacks and fades between them. https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-1001518377
- **Lesson for London:** London has no direct equivalent, but the pattern of a safety or resilience feature valued in step with perceived risk resembles how flood risk, cladding/EWS1 and fire safety get priced in London.

## 10. Synthesis: primary versus secondary across the region

**Primary in Dubai (both sides):** community or master-development name; price per sqft against DLD comps; off-plan versus ready; developer track record; service charge per sqft; annual rent plus number of cheques (renters); RERA/Smart Index gap (renewals); golden-visa AED 2m line (buyers); school proximity (families); metro (single renters in apartments).

**Secondary in Dubai:** view and floor (large in money terms but unit-level), building age or index class, payment plan, parking or balcony (present in the DLD model), maid's room, district cooling versus split AC, and handover date.

**Different from London:** no council tax or stamp-duty bands (instead a flat 4% DLD fee and the AED 2m visa line); service charges matter much more than in London leasehold (though London's are rising); annual upfront rent in cheques instead of monthly rent plus a 5-week deposit cap; a statutory rent index with 90-day notice; and a market that is about 70% off-plan, so developer credit risk is a primary criterion. In London, new-build is a minority segment and comes with a "new-build premium" discount problem.

**Similar to London:** school catchment dominates family choices, rail-station walkability carries a rent premium that is nonlinear with distance, noise near stations, view and floor premiums in towers, and segment-specific hedonic weights (Worku), which echo London's prime versus mainstream split.
